## What is at stake

In Israel, left and right are defined mostly by positions on the Palestinians, the occupied territories and a Palestinian state, not by economics ([CHES-Israel expert survey, 2021–22](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13540688231218917)). Yet in the 2026 campaign almost no one argues for a Palestinian state. Of the lists running on October 27, only Ra'am and the Joint List say they support a Palestinian state ([Jerusalem Post, Sept 1, 2026](https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-907154)). The Jewish-majority lists oppose one, say it is "not on the agenda", decline to answer, or, in the case of The Democrats, speak of "separation" ([Ynet party questionnaire, Sept 30, 2026](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/h1dpvu9cgl)).

The Knesset put this on record before the campaign began. On July 17, 2024 it passed, 68–9, a resolution stating that "the Knesset of Israel firmly opposes the establishment of a Palestinian state west of Jordan." Unlike a February 2024 resolution, which rejected only unilateral recognition, it rejected a state even as part of a negotiated settlement. Coalition parties were joined by members of Benny Gantz's National Unity; the nine votes against came from Ra'am and Hadash–Ta'al, and Yesh Atid and Labor left the chamber ([Times of Israel, July 2024](https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-votes-overwhelmingly-against-palestinian-statehood-days-before-pms-us-trip/)). The Palestinians of the West Bank and Gaza, whose state is the question, are not Israeli citizens and have no vote in this election (see [who votes, and who doesn't](/how-it-works/who-votes)).

Since then, Knesset votes have been about annexation rather than partition. On July 23, 2025 a non-binding declaration in favor of annexing the West Bank and the Jordan Valley passed 71–13 ([Ynet, July 2025](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/bkxfw9algl)). On October 22, 2025 a bill by Noam's Avi Maoz to annex the settlement areas of the West Bank passed a preliminary reading 25–24 ([Times of Israel, Oct 22, 2025](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/rebelling-against-pm-mks-pass-preliminary-reading-of-west-bank-annexation-bill/)). The [West Bank page](/issues/west-bank) covers settlement and annexation in detail.

Abroad, the question moved the other way. By late September 2025, 157 of the 193 UN member states had recognized the State of Palestine, among them the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and France in September 2025 ([Al Jazeera, Sept 23, 2025](https://aljazeera.com/news/2025/9/23/which-are-the-150-countries-that-have-recognised-palestine-as-of-2025)). In Israel the recognitions united the main Jewish-majority parties rather than dividing them: Yair Lapid called it "a harmful step, and a reward for terror", Yair Golan called it "destructive", and Itamar Ben-Gvir called it "a prize for the murderous terrorists" ([Times of Israel, Sept 22, 2025](https://www.timesofisrael.com/israel-unites-against-hollow-palestine-recognition-but-pm-could-go-too-far/)).

When the Israel Democracy Institute asked voters to name their most important considerations, Jewish respondents chose foreign affairs and security (39%), the economy and cost of living (38%) and conscription (27%). The Palestinian issue was not offered as a separate answer ([IDI, Aug 31–Sept 3, 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177)). In the campaign, Netanyahu has put the question this way:

<Quote who="Benjamin Netanyahu" role="Prime Minister, Likud" source="Jerusalem Post" url="https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-910068" date="Sept 18, 2026">Do you want a Palestinian state? (or) do you want a Jewish state?</Quote>

## What Israelis think

In 2026, 17% of Israelis and 16% of Jewish Israelis think Israel and a Palestinian state can coexist peacefully, down from 50% of all Israelis in 2013. For the first time in Pew's polling, more than half (56%) say coexistence is not possible ([Pew, Sept 15, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/09/15/many-israelis-and-palestinians-say-they-cannot-coexist-peacefully-and-explain-why/)). Pew says the share has not changed significantly from 21% in 2025. Pew's question asks about feasibility, not preference.

In Pew's 2025 survey, 54% of Israelis on the left, 29% in the center and 7% on the right said a way can be found for Israel and a Palestinian state to coexist. Among Jewish Israelis, 25% of Hilonim (secular), 11% of Masortim (traditional) and 9% of Haredim (ultra-Orthodox) and Datiim (religious), counted together, said peaceful coexistence is possible ([Pew, Feb–March 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/06/pg_2025.06.03_israel_report.pdf)).

Tel Aviv University's Peace Index asks a different question: how far people support or oppose each of several outcomes. Among Jews, support for "the creation of an independent Palestinian state alongside the State of Israel" fell from about 38% a month before October 7, 2023 (read from the Peace Index chart) to 23% in March 2026. Support for "annexation of the occupied territories and the creation of one state under Israeli rule with limited rights for Palestinians" rose from 30% to 38%, though in November 2025 half of Jews said they opposed it ([Peace Index, Nov 2025](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/2025-11.pdf)). Whatever they prefer, 48% of Jews expect the current situation to continue ([Peace Index, March 2026](https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/2026-03.pdf)).

The answers depend on the menu. When the Institute for National Security Studies offered a single choice that included "separation from the Palestinians", separation led among Jews at 32%, level with "full annexation without granting civil rights" at 31% ([INSS, June 16–22, 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-june-2026/)).

Palestinian citizens of Israel are far more favorable. In the March 2026 Peace Index, 71% of Arab respondents supported a Palestinian state alongside Israel, from a small subsample of 103; in the INSS survey, 57% of Arab respondents chose two states. Their own first voting concern is crime in Arab society (53.5%) ([IDI, Sept 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177)). The [Palestinian citizens page](/communities/palestinian-citizens) has more.

**What Palestinians think.** Pew asked the same question of Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, its first survey there in several years; it could not survey in Gaza. 21% said a way can be found for Israel and an independent Palestinian state to coexist peacefully and 68% said it cannot, against 17% and 56% of Israelis ([Pew, fieldwork March 30–April 28, 2026, 1,038 adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/09/15/many-israelis-and-palestinians-say-they-cannot-coexist-peacefully-and-explain-why/)). Asked why, they diverged. In Pew's summary, "While Israelis explain that an independent Palestinian state would pose a threat to Israel, many Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem say coexistence is not an option while they live under Israeli control." One Palestinian man told Pew: "They are an occupying force who took our land and separated us from each other with checkpoints."

The Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research (PCPSR) in Ramallah, which also interviews in Gaza, found in August 2026 that 44% supported and 50% opposed "the two-state solution" when it was offered without details, with almost the same support in the West Bank and Gaza. Offered three options, 56% chose "the two-state solution based on the 1967 borders" (52% in the West Bank, 61% in Gaza), 14% a confederation and 10% "one state in which Jews and Palestinians live with equal rights"; 58% said the two-state solution is no longer practical because of settlement expansion. Asked for the most effective way to end the occupation and establish a state, 44% chose negotiations, the highest share in four years, and 27% armed struggle, the lowest. Asked how Israeli policy toward Gaza and the West Bank would change if the Israeli opposition won and Netanyahu lost, 34% said it would not change, 33% said it would get worse and 28% better ([PCPSR, Aug 5–8, 2026, 1,270 adults: 830 in the West Bank, 440 in Gaza](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014)).

The last survey found that put the same questions to both publics, the Palestinian-Israeli Pulse (fieldwork July 17–29, 2024), found 40% of Palestinians and 21% of Jewish Israelis supporting a two-state solution; 6% of Palestinians and 10% of Jewish Israelis agreed that it is possible to trust the other side ([PCPSR and Tel Aviv University, July 2024](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/989)). No joint survey since then was found.

## Misreadings

**The election is not a referendum on two states.** No Jewish-majority list running on October 27 campaigns for a Palestinian state. Two of Netanyahu's leading opponents, Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar! and Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad, answered Ynet that a state is "not on the agenda" and "We oppose a Palestinian state", respectively.

**Left and right do not mean what they mean in the United States.** As the CHES survey above found, Israel's left-right axis is the Palestinian question. Bennett and Eisenkot oppose Netanyahu but are not on the left on this question, and the Knesset's 68 votes against a state in 2024 included centrists from Gantz's party.

**Young Israelis are not the most dovish.** In the Palestinian-Israeli Pulse of December 2022, the latest age breakdown found, 20% of Jewish Israelis aged 18–34 backed the two-state solution in general, against 47% of those over 55 ([Pulse, TAU and PSR, Dec 2022](https://resolution.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/resolution/Summary%20Report_%20English_Joint%20Poll%2024%20Jan%202023.pdf)).

**American Jews answer differently.** Asked Pew's same question before the war, 63% of American Jews thought a way could be found for Israel and an independent Palestinian state to coexist peacefully ([Pew, fieldwork Nov 2019–June 2020](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/05/11/u-s-jews-connections-with-and-attitudes-toward-israel/)). The Israeli Jewish figure in 2026, from a different survey taken after the war began, is 16%; no Pew figure for American Jews on this question since 2020 was found.

<Note title="American Jews after October 7">No post-war survey of American Jews found for this page repeats the coexistence question. In February 2024 Pew asked a different one: 46% of American Jews said a two-state solution would be the best possible outcome ([Pew, Feb 13–25, 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2024/03/21/majority-in-u-s-say-israel-has-valid-reasons-for-fighting-fewer-say-the-same-about-hamas/)).</Note>

**Opposing a state is not the same as backing annexation.** Among Jews, opposition to a Palestinian state is far larger than support for any single alternative. The status quo and separation draw large shares, and the outcome Jews most often expect, the current situation, is not the one they most often prefer.
