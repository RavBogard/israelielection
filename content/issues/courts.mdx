## What is at stake

Israel has no single written constitution. In 1950 the Knesset chose to write separate "Basic Laws" instead ([Knesset](https://main.knesset.gov.il/en/activity/pages/basiclaws.aspx)), and many can be passed or changed by a simple majority. The Knesset is the only legislature, with no second chamber ([Knesset](https://main.knesset.gov.il/EN/About/Pages/BranchesOfGovernment.aspx)). "In Israel, the Supreme Court is the only restraint on the power of the political majority," the Israel Democracy Institute wrote in 2022 ([IDI](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/46387)). In 1995 the court held that the Basic Laws have constitutional status and that it may void ordinary laws that infringe them; Supreme Court President Aharon Barak wrote that "the constitutional revolution occurred in the Knesset in March 1992" ([ruling, Nov 9, 1995](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/united-mizrahi-bank-v-migdal-cooperative-village)).

In January 2023 Justice Minister Yariv Levin announced a judicial overhaul: bar courts from voiding decisions as "unreasonable"; give the government a majority on the committee that picks judges; curb judicial review; let 61 Knesset members re-enact a struck-down law; and stop legal advisers' positions from binding ministries ([Times of Israel, Jan 4, 2023](https://www.timesofisrael.com/justice-minister-unveils-plan-to-shackle-the-high-court-overhaul-israels-judiciary/)).

On March 23, 2023 a bare Knesset majority amended a Basic Law so that the court cannot order a prime minister to take a leave of absence; removal requires physical or mental incapacity and 75% of ministers or Knesset members ([JTA, Mar 23, 2023](https://www.jta.org/2023/03/23/israel/netanyahu-vows-more-active-role-in-israels-judiciary-fight-following-a-day-of-tense-protests)). On July 24, 2023 the Knesset abolished the reasonableness standard ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-knesset-enacts-law-to-abolish-reasonableness-standard-1001453127)). On January 1, 2024 a full 15-justice panel struck that law down 8–7, and 12 of the 15 held that the court may review Basic Laws in exceptional cases ([ruling](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/movement-quality-government-israel-v-knesset); [NPR](https://text.npr.org/1222400537)). On January 3 it ruled 6–5 that the incapacity law was "clearly a personal amendment" and delayed it to the next Knesset ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/high-court-delays-contentious-law-shielding-netanyahu-from-recusal/)).

### Protests and the security services

In 2023 hundreds of thousands protested weekly, and more than 10,000 veterans pledged to boycott reserve duty ([JTA, July 24, 2023](https://www.jta.org/2023/07/24/israel/israel-passes-first-law-weakening-supreme-court-following-months-of-civil-strife)). After Netanyahu fired Defense Minister Yoav Gallant, who had urged a pause, a general strike backed by the Histadrut labor federation shut universities on March 27, and Netanyahu paused the bills that evening ([JTA, Mar 27, 2023](https://www.jta.org/2023/03/27/israel/there-must-not-be-a-civil-war-netanyahu-gives-in-to-calls-to-postpone-judiciary-reform-in-significant-victory-for-protesters); [JTA](https://www.jta.org/2023/03/27/israel/a-historic-day-in-israel-ends-with-a-political-compromise-and-big-questions-about-the-future)). On April 27 an estimated 200,000 supporters rallied in Jerusalem ([JTA](https://www.jta.org/2023/04/27/israel/we-will-not-give-up-on-judicial-changes-right-wing-protesters-at-israels-largest-pro-reform-rally-are-told)).

Otzma Yehudit MK Almog Cohen said a refusal leader's views "seriously harmed the cohesion and security of the country, and were a catalyst for the events of October 7" ([Times of Israel, Dec 2, 2024](https://www.timesofisrael.com/katz-nixes-reserve-duty-of-protest-leader-who-urged-refusal-to-serve-over-judicial-overhaul/)). On October 7, the Times of Israel reported, the army had "an unprecedented 120% turnout rate for reservists" ([Times of Israel, July 19, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/katz-orders-democrats-party-candidate-ousted-from-idf-reserves-over-call-to-refuse-service/)).

On March 21, 2025 the cabinet fired Shin Bet chief Ronen Bar, who then announced his resignation. On May 21 the court ruled 2–1 that the firing was improper and made while Netanyahu had a conflict of interest, but issued no orders since the case was moot. Netanyahu called the ruling "disgraceful" ([Times of Israel, May 21, 2025](https://www.timesofisrael.com/high-court-rules-shin-bet-heads-dismissal-unlawful-pm-had-conflict-of-interest/)).

### The next bench

In March 2025 the Knesset passed, 67 votes in favor, a law that removes the Israel Bar Association's two seats from the selection committee and lets justices be appointed without the sitting justices' consent, from the next Knesset on ([Times of Israel, Mar 27, 2025](https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-passes-law-greatly-boosting-political-control-over-judicial-appointments/)). The Supreme Court, sitting as the High Court of Justice, heard petitions against it on June 21, 2026; the Times of Israel counted nine of the 11 justices as critical of parts of it ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/regime-change-high-court-tears-into-coalition-bid-to-control-judicial-appointments/)). No ruling had been found as of October 5. Four of 15 seats are vacant and six more justices turn 70, the retirement age, within three years ([JTA, Sept 18, 2026](https://www.jta.org/2026/09/18/israel/judicial-reform-2-0-how-netanyahus-plans-to-reshape-the-courts-could-affect-israels-democracy)), so the next government could fill about two-thirds of the bench.

Supporters argue that unelected judges overrule voters. Levin promised that judges would no longer "choose themselves in back rooms with no protocol" (Times of Israel, Jan 4, 2023). Religious Zionism wants narrower rules on who may petition the court ([Ynet, Sept 22, 2026](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/hkcjtk1cme)). Conservative critics, IDI scholars write, hold that value judgments "should be decided by democratically elected representatives and not unaccountable judges" ([IDI, Feb 2023](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/47916)). The Kohelet Policy Forum, "a non-partisan research and action institute" by its own description, argues that "governmental legal counsel must be subordinate to appointed Ministers" ([Kohelet, July 16, 2026](https://www.kohelet.org.il/en/article/legal-advisors-must-answer-to-elected-ministers/)).

The government voted on August 4, 2025 to fire Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/high-court-freezes-dismissal-of-attorney-general-after-government-votes-to-fire-her/)); on December 14 seven justices, conservatives included, unanimously annulled the firing ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/high-court-annuls-firing-of-attorney-general-cabinet-ministers-urge-defiance-of-ruling/)). A law passed July 15, 2026, 65–51, lets the government reject the attorney general's opinions and control the post's appointment and dismissal; prosecution, including whether to investigate senior elected officials, stays with the attorney general. It takes effect in January 2027 ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/coalition-passes-law-gutting-attorney-generals-powers-removing-key-check-on-government/)). A dropped June draft would have let a new prosecutor general "reconsider the existing indictments against Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu" ([Jerusalem Post, June 2, 2026](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-898054)).

### Netanyahu's trial

Netanyahu has been on trial since May 2020: for fraud and breach of trust in Case 1000 (gifts from benefactors) and Case 2000 (alleged talks for favorable newspaper coverage), and also bribery in Case 4000 (regulatory decisions alleged to have benefited Bezeq shareholder Shaul Elovitch in exchange for favorable Walla coverage). He denies wrongdoing. On June 29, 2026 the judges again urged prosecutors to drop the bribery charge ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/judges-again-call-on-prosecution-to-drop-bribery-charge-from-netanyahu-trial/)). Ending his testimony on June 24, he called the case "a political assassination"; defense witnesses remain ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/netanyahu-wraps-up-trial-testimony-after-98-hearings-across-18-months/)). Supporters echo "his contention that the charges amount to a political witch hunt" ([Times of Israel, June 30](https://www.timesofisrael.com/ministers-coalition-mks-push-pardon-for-pm-after-judges-urge-dropping-of-key-charge/)). Watchdog groups argued the overhaul could affect his trial ([Times of Israel, Jan 3, 2024](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/high-court-delays-contentious-law-shielding-netanyahu-from-recusal/)).

In November 2025 he asked President Isaac Herzog for a pardon, without admitting guilt. On April 26, 2026 Herzog set it aside and proposed plea talks ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/herzog-pushes-for-new-plea-deal-talks-says-he-wont-consider-netanyahu-pardon-request-yet/)); as of June 30 his lawyers had not responded (Times of Israel, June 30).

### The court and this election

On September 23, 2026 the Central Elections Committee voted 18–5 to disqualify the Joint List and 18–5 to disqualify Ra'am, on petitions from Likud, Otzma Yehudit and others citing Basic Law: The Knesset's bar on supporting armed struggle against Israel. The attorney general saw grounds only against Balad leader Sami Abu Shehadeh, over an October 8, 2023 article calling the Hamas assault "an important historic event"; the Joint List accused backers of the bans of "deepening racism" ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/election-panel-votes-to-bar-balad-chief-abu-shehadeh-over-alleged-support-for-terror/)). The law also bars incitement to racism and denying Israel's existence as a Jewish and democratic state; bans need Supreme Court approval, and the committee is almost all party lawmakers. On October 2 nine justices unanimously reinstated both lists, and Abu Shehadeh withdrew after the court's president told him a majority would bar him ([Times of Israel, Oct 2, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/supreme-court-unanimously-overturns-election-panels-ban-on-arab-parties/)). ## What Israelis think

In each of three IDI surveys since 2023, more Israelis opposed the overhaul or its timing than backed it. In March 2026, 52% said it was not correct to advance it now and 32% said it was ([IDI, March 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30641/israeli-voice-index-march-2026-eng-data.pdf)). It ranks low as a vote factor: 18% of Jewish respondents named the judicial system as a first or second factor, behind security (39%), the economy (38%), the draft (27%), religion and state (26%) and the party leader (23%) ([IDI, Sept 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177)).

Jewish trust in the Supreme Court fell mostly before the overhaul: from 69% in 2003 to 41% in 2022, and 42% in May 2025 ([IDI Democracy Index 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). The 2026 index is not out.

In May 2025, 83% of left-wing Jews trusted the court against 21.5% on the right, and 66% of secular Jews against 3% of Haredim (ultra-Orthodox) (same source). IDI's reading is that "political identity outweighs religiosity."

In January 2025, 79% of Arab respondents opposed the overhaul, against 50.5% of Jews ([IDI, Jan 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/57945)). Yet in May 2025, 55.5% of them said the court "intervenes too much," against 50% of Jews (Democracy Index 2025). IDI's "Arabs" include Druze.

## What the parties say

Coalition parties that answered Ynet's September 2026 survey want to curb the court; Likud declined to answer. Zehut, Moshe Feiglin's party, says the court "has accumulated powers that were never explicitly granted by legislation" ([Ynet, Sept 22, 2026](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/hkcjtk1cme)). Yashar!, B'Yachad and the Reservists want a Basic Law setting rules for legislation or judicial review; Yisrael Beiteinu wants a written constitution put to a referendum; Blue and White would limit the court's intervention in government decisions.

## Misreadings

**It is not a fight over a culture-war docket.** Israel's fight is over the court's structure and power. Conservative justices joined the attorney-general ruling, and the conservative justice Alex Stein criticized the selection law at the June 2026 hearing ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/regime-change-high-court-tears-into-coalition-bid-to-control-judicial-appointments/)).

**"Judicial review" means something different.** The U.S. court reviews laws against a written constitution that is hard to amend. Israel's reviews them against Basic Laws, many passable by a simple majority, and since 2024 it says it may review those too.

**Appointments work differently.** Justices are not nominated and confirmed; they retire at 70. Under the old rules a Supreme Court appointment needed 7 of the committee's 9 votes, with three justices on it ([IDI, Dec 2022](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/46674)).

**The court also rules on who may run.** In 2026 it overturned the election committee's bans on the Joint List and Ra'am.
