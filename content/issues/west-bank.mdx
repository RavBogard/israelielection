## What is at stake

The West Bank is the land between Israel and Jordan that Israel has held since 1967. Two words compete to describe it. "Occupied" is the term used by the UN, whose humanitarian office reports on the "occupied Palestinian territory," by the European Union, whose annual report is titled [2025 Report on Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf), and by the International Court of Justice, whose non-binding 2024 advisory opinion refers to "the Palestinian territory occupied since 1967" ([Times of Israel, July 19, 2024](https://www.timesofisrael.com/world-court-israeli-presence-in-east-jerusalem-west-bank-is-illegal-and-must-end/)). "Disputed" is the word used by those who reject that label. Israel's government has long argued that its hold on the West Bank cannot be considered an occupation, "both because the territory historically belonged to ancient Israel and because it captured the territory from Jordan, which had no legal claim to it" ([Jerusalem Post, July 2023](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-752919)). Netanyahu answered the court: "The Jewish people are not occupiers in their own land" (same Times of Israel report). A July 2025 Knesset motion called the West Bank an "inseparable part of the Land of Israel" ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-votes-71-13-for-non-binding-motion-calling-to-annex-west-bank/)). This page uses "occupied" from here on.

The 1995 Oslo II agreement, meant to last five years, split the territory into three zones that are still in force: Area A, the Palestinian cities (about 18%), under the Palestinian Authority; Area B (about 22%), under Palestinian civil and Israeli security control; and Area C (about 60%), under full Israeli control ([B'Tselem](https://www.btselem.org/area_c/what_is_area_c)). More than 3 million people live in the West Bank, 85% of them Palestinian (end of 2023, [Peace Now](https://peacenow.org.il/en/settlements-watch/settlements-data/population)).

How many settlers there are depends on East Jerusalem: the EU counts its Israeli neighborhoods as settlements, while Israel does not. Without East Jerusalem, 541,085 Jews lived in the West Bank on Jan 1, 2026 ([JNS](https://www.jns.org/exclusive-judea-samaria-jewish-population-grew-at-twice-israels-overall-rate-in-2025/)). With it, the EU counts over 750,000 Israeli settlers, 15% of the West Bank's population, East Jerusalem included ([EU, 2025 report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf)). The two figures come from different sources, count slightly different groups (Jews; Israeli settlers) and are not for the same date, so the gap between them is not an exact count of East Jerusalem's settlers. The settlements include Haredi cities in the West Bank, such as Modi'in Illit and Beitar Illit, whose residents are Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) and are counted in these totals; they are covered on the [Haredim page](/communities/haredim) and the [settlers page](/communities/settlers).

"Annexation" means applying Israeli law and administration to this land. Israel's right calls it "applying sovereignty." The Knesset has not passed it into law. The July 2025 motion passed 71–13 and has no legal force. On Oct 22, 2025, two bills passed a preliminary reading, one applying Israeli law to "all areas of settlement" ([Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-871299)) 25–24 and one for Ma'ale Adumim 32–9 ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/israel-west-bank-annexation-bills-trump-objection-vance-visit/)); the Jerusalem Post called the 25–24 vote a first reading. Netanyahu told Likud MKs to abstain. No later vote on either bill was found before the Knesset dissolved in July 2026. Trump had already ruled annexation out in September 2025:

<Quote who="Donald Trump" role="U.S. president" source="CBS News" url="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/israel-west-bank-annexation-bills-trump-objection-vance-visit/" date="September 2025">I will not allow Israel to annex the West Bank. It's not going to happen.</Quote>

The larger change has come through administration. In 2023 Bezalel Smotrich set up a Settlement Administration inside the Defense Ministry. On May 29, 2024, the army's Central Command delegated Civil Administration powers over land, planning and registration to a civilian deputy ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/drawing-annexation-claims-idf-hands-many-west-bank-powers-to-civilian-ally-of-smotrich/)). In February 2026 the cabinet eased Israeli purchase of private land, extended oversight into Areas A and B, and opened land registration in Area C. The Foreign Ministry called this "an administrative measure"; Smotrich called it part of "the normalization and de facto sovereignty revolution that we are leading" ([Times of Israel, Feb 19, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/expansion-of-west-bank-rule-appears-to-shift-creeping-annexation-into-higher-gear/)).

Settlement planning reached records. In 2025, 54 new settlements were approved and 86 outposts set up, about 60 of them "agricultural farms" (Peace Now, [2025 summary](https://peacenow.org.il/en/summary-of-2025-in-settlements)).

One project runs into election week. E1, the land between Ma'ale Adumim and Jerusalem, received final approval for 3,401 units in August 2025. A tender for 1,234 of them closes on Oct 19, 2026, eight days before the vote ([Peace Now, Aug 2026](https://peacenow.org.il/en/e1-tender-published-and-open-for-bidding)). On Sept 28 the attorney general's office dropped its own one-month postponement of the deadline ([Arutz Sheva](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/433778)).

Settler violence has also risen. UN OCHA counted 1,189 settler attacks causing Palestinian casualties or property damage in 2023, 1,420 in 2024 and 1,828 in 2025, the highest since its count began in 2006 (OCHA via the [EU report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf)). Of the police investigation files on ideologically motivated offenses by Israelis against Palestinians that Yesh Din has monitored since 2005, 93.6% ended without an indictment ([Yesh Din](https://www.yesh-din.org/en/data-sheet-law-enforcement-on-israeli-civilians-in-the-west-bank-settler-violence-2005-2025/), Jan 2026).

<Note title="Two counts, two directions">The IDF counts "nationalistic" Jewish incidents differently: 1,045 in 2023, 682 in 2024 and 867 in 2025 ([Jerusalem Post, Jan 19, 2026](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-883857)). OCHA's count rises every year; the IDF's falls in 2024 before rising again. INSS, using defense-establishment data, counts 660 "nationalist crime" incidents in the first half of 2026, up 63% on the previous half-year ([INSS, Sept 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/jewish-terror-alert/)).</Note>

## What Israelis think

Israelis do not split two ways on this. In June 2026, INSS found three camps of about a quarter each: separation from the Palestinians, a two-state solution, and full annexation without civil rights for Palestinians ([INSS, June 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-june-2026/)). The divide runs between the blocs: half of coalition voters chose annexation, while opposition voters split between separation (40%) and two states (36%). Among Arab respondents, 57% chose two states.

<Chart id="west-bank.inss-approach" />

Other surveys word annexation differently and get different numbers. In August 2026, 37% of all Israelis supported full annexation in the Jewish People Policy Institute's index, and fewer than 1% of Jewish respondents said Palestinians should get full Israeli citizenship upon annexation ([Jerusalem Post, Aug 12, 2026](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-905340)).

The share of Israelis who think Israel and a Palestinian state can coexist peacefully has fallen by two-thirds since 2013, from 50% to 17% in 2026. In Pew's 2025 survey, 54% of the left thought Israel and a Palestinian state can coexist peacefully, against 29% of the center and 7% of the right; by religiosity, 25% of Hilonim (secular), 11% of Masortim (traditional) and 9% of Haredim and Datiim (religious) combined ([Pew, June 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2025/06/03/views-of-the-potential-for-lasting-peace/)).

<Chart id="west-bank.pew-coexist" />

On settler violence, 55% told INSS in January 2026 that the defense establishment was "acting too leniently" on nationalist crime by Jews, and 25% called enforcement appropriate ([INSS, Jan 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-january-2026/)). No 2026 survey found breaks annexation views down by religiosity, and none measures how much voters weigh this issue.

## What the parties say

Of the lists running, only Ra'am and the Joint List name a Palestinian state as a goal. No Zionist list that answered Ynet names one as a goal. They differ on how much land to annex, from all of it (Religious Zionism–Zehut, Otzma Yehudit) to Area C ([B'Yachad](/parties/byachad), [Yisrael Beiteinu](/parties/yb)) to "sovereignty through actions, not words" (Reservists). The Democrats' own word is "separation."

## Misreadings

**"Annexation" is not mostly a Knesset vote.** The bills are symbolic or stalled. The change has come through planning approvals, the 2024 delegation order and the 2026 land decisions, which Smotrich himself describes as de facto sovereignty.

**"Separation" is not "two states."** Without the map of Areas A, B and C, "sovereignty over Area C" sounds like annexing everything, and the Democrats' "separation" sounds like a Palestinian state. They are different plans.

**The blocs do not line up with the positions.** Two lists running against Netanyahu, B'Yachad and Yisrael Beiteinu, back annexing Area C, and Yisrael Beiteinu backs building in E1. The Haredi parties, Netanyahu's allies, do not make the issue central to their campaigns, Ynet reports.

**"Settlements" are not one thing.** They range from Haredi cities and suburbs near the 1967 line to hilltop communities and outposts, which are set up without Israeli government approval. The EU counts East Jerusalem neighborhoods as settlements; Israel does not. That is the main reason the settler count appears as 541,085 or as over 750,000.

**The terms are positions.** The biblical name used in most of the party statements above or "West Bank," "sovereignty" or "annexation," "farms" or "outposts," "nationalist crime," "Jewish terror" or "settler violence": each word signals where its speaker stands.
