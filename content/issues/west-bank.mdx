## What is at stake

The West Bank is the land between Israel and Jordan that Israel captured in 1967 and has held under military occupation since. "Occupied" is the term used by the UN, whose humanitarian office reports on the "occupied Palestinian territory," by the European Union, whose annual report is titled [2025 Report on Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf), and by the International Court of Justice, whose non-binding 2024 advisory opinion refers to "the Palestinian territory occupied since 1967" ([Times of Israel, July 19, 2024](https://www.timesofisrael.com/world-court-israeli-presence-in-east-jerusalem-west-bank-is-illegal-and-must-end/)); this site uses it too. Israel's government rejects the label and calls the territory "disputed," arguing that its hold on the West Bank cannot be considered an occupation "both because the territory historically belonged to ancient Israel and because it captured the territory from Jordan, which had no legal claim to it" ([Jerusalem Post, July 2023](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-752919)). Netanyahu answered the court: "The Jewish people are not occupiers in their own land" (same Times of Israel report).

The 1995 Oslo II agreement, meant to last five years, split the territory into three zones that are still in force: Area A, the Palestinian cities (about 18%), under the Palestinian Authority; Area B (about 22%), under Palestinian civil and Israeli security control; and Area C (about 60%), under full Israeli control ([B'Tselem](https://www.btselem.org/area_c/what_is_area_c)). More than 3 million people live in the West Bank, 85% of them Palestinian (end of 2023, [Peace Now](https://peacenow.org.il/en/settlements-watch/settlements-data/population)). The Palestinians among them live under Israeli military rule, are not Israeli citizens and have no vote on October 27; the Israeli settlers living beside them do (see [who votes, and who doesn't](/how-it-works/who-votes)). The two groups also live under different law: the International Court of Justice found that "Palestinians in the West Bank are thus subject to military law and military courts, whereas settlers benefit from the criminal law and criminal justice system applicable to civilians in Israel" ([ICJ, July 19, 2024, para. 136](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=A/78/968&l=en&t=pdf)).

How many settlers there are depends on East Jerusalem: the EU counts its Israeli neighborhoods as settlements, while Israel does not. Without East Jerusalem, 541,085 Jews lived in the West Bank on Jan 1, 2026 ([JNS](https://www.jns.org/exclusive-judea-samaria-jewish-population-grew-at-twice-israels-overall-rate-in-2025/)). With it, the EU counts over 750,000 Israeli settlers, 15% of the West Bank's population, East Jerusalem included ([EU, 2025 report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf)). The two figures differ in source, group counted and date, so their gap is not an exact count of East Jerusalem's settlers. The settlements include Haredi cities in the West Bank, such as Modi'in Illit and Beitar Illit, whose residents are Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) and are counted in these totals; they are covered on the [Haredim page](/communities/haredim) and the [settlers page](/communities/settlers).

"Annexation" means applying Israeli law and administration to this land. Israel's right calls it "applying sovereignty." The Knesset has not passed it into law. A July 2025 Knesset motion calling the West Bank an "inseparable part of the Land of Israel" passed 71–13 and has no legal force ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-votes-71-13-for-non-binding-motion-calling-to-annex-west-bank/)). On Oct 22, 2025, two bills passed a preliminary reading, one applying Israeli law to "all areas of settlement" ([Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-871299)) 25–24 and one for Ma'ale Adumim 32–9 ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/israel-west-bank-annexation-bills-trump-objection-vance-visit/)). Netanyahu told Likud MKs to abstain. No later vote on either bill was found before the Knesset dissolved in July 2026. Trump had already ruled annexation out in September 2025:

<Quote who="Donald Trump" role="U.S. president" source="CBS News" url="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/israel-west-bank-annexation-bills-trump-objection-vance-visit/" date="September 2025">I will not allow Israel to annex the West Bank. It's not going to happen.</Quote>

The larger change has come through administration. In 2023 Bezalel Smotrich set up a Settlement Administration inside the Defense Ministry. On May 29, 2024, the army's Central Command delegated Civil Administration powers over land, planning and registration to a civilian deputy ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/drawing-annexation-claims-idf-hands-many-west-bank-powers-to-civilian-ally-of-smotrich/)). In February 2026 the cabinet eased Israeli purchase of private land, extended oversight into Areas A and B, and opened land registration in Area C. The Foreign Ministry called this "an administrative measure"; Smotrich called it part of "the normalization and de facto sovereignty revolution that we are leading" ([Times of Israel, Feb 19, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/expansion-of-west-bank-rule-appears-to-shift-creeping-annexation-into-higher-gear/)).

Settlement planning reached records. In 2025, 54 new settlements were approved and 86 outposts set up, 58 of them "agricultural farms" (Peace Now, [2025 summary](https://peacenow.org.il/en/summary-of-2025-in-settlements)).

One project runs into election week. E1, the land between Ma'ale Adumim and Jerusalem, received final approval for 3,401 units in August 2025. A tender for 1,234 of them closes on Oct 19, 2026, eight days before the vote ([Peace Now, Aug 2026](https://peacenow.org.il/en/e1-tender-published-and-open-for-bidding)).

Settler violence has also risen. UN OCHA counted 1,189 settler attacks causing Palestinian casualties or property damage in 2023, 1,420 in 2024 and 1,828 in 2025, the highest since its count began in 2006 (OCHA via the [EU report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf)). Of the police investigation files on ideologically motivated offenses by Israelis against Palestinians that Yesh Din has monitored since 2005, 93.6% ended without an indictment ([Yesh Din](https://www.yesh-din.org/en/data-sheet-law-enforcement-on-israeli-civilians-in-the-west-bank-settler-violence-2005-2025/), Jan 2026).

OCHA counts deaths in both directions: Palestinians and Israelis killed or injured "in the context of the occupation and conflict," each incident "validated by at least two independent and reliable sources." It counts as a civilian "whoever is neither a member of security forces (including police) nor fulfills a combat function within an armed group" ([OCHA](https://www.ochaopt.org/data/casualties)). In 2025, by its count, Israeli forces killed 225 Palestinians in the West Bank and settlers killed nine; for six more it is unknown whether forces or settlers killed them. Of the 240, 55 were children. Palestinians killed 17 Israelis in the West Bank, one of them a child and six of them members of Israeli forces, and attacks by Palestinians from the West Bank killed three Israelis inside Israel ([OCHA, Jan 7, 2026](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/humanitarian-situation-update-352-west-bank)).

<Note title="Two counts, two directions">The IDF counts "nationalistic" Jewish incidents differently: 1,045 in 2023, 682 in 2024 and 867 in 2025 ([Jerusalem Post, Jan 19, 2026](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-883857)). OCHA's count rises every year; the IDF's falls in 2024 before rising again. INSS, using defense-establishment data, counts 660 "nationalist crime" incidents in the first half of 2026, up 63% on the previous half-year ([INSS, Sept 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/jewish-terror-alert/)).</Note>

## What Israelis think

In June 2026, INSS found three camps of about a quarter each: separation from the Palestinians, a two-state solution, and full annexation without civil rights for Palestinians ([INSS, June 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-june-2026/)). The divide runs between the blocs: half of coalition voters chose annexation, while opposition voters split between separation (40%) and two states (36%). Among Arab respondents, 57% chose two states.

<Chart id="west-bank.inss-approach" />

Other surveys word annexation differently and get different numbers. In August 2026, 37% of all Israelis supported full annexation in the Jewish People Policy Institute's index ([Jerusalem Post, Aug 12, 2026](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-905340)).

The share of Israelis who think Israel and a Palestinian state can coexist peacefully has fallen by two-thirds since 2013, from 50% to 17% in 2026. In Pew's 2025 survey, 54% of the left thought Israel and a Palestinian state can coexist peacefully, against 29% of the center and 7% of the right ([Pew, June 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2025/06/03/views-of-the-potential-for-lasting-peace/)). Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, asked the same question in spring 2026, were about as doubtful: 21% said a way can be found ([Pew, Sept 15, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/09/15/many-israelis-and-palestinians-say-they-cannot-coexist-peacefully-and-explain-why/)).

<Chart id="west-bank.pew-coexist" />

On settler violence, 55% told INSS in January 2026 that the defense establishment was "acting too leniently" on nationalist crime by Jews, and 25% called enforcement appropriate ([INSS, Jan 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-january-2026/)). No 2026 survey found breaks annexation views down by religiosity, and none measures how much voters weigh this issue. When IDI asked voters in September 2026 which considerations would decide their vote, the Palestinian issue was not offered as a separate answer ([IDI, Aug 31–Sept 3, 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177)).

**Palestinians in the West Bank.** In August 2026, 87% of West Bank residents told the Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research (PCPSR) that they were worried settlement expansion and new outposts would cost them their land or homes ([PCPSR, Aug 5–8, 2026](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014)). In Pew's survey, 92% of Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem called Israeli checkpoints a major problem for them and their families, the top concern of nine asked about ([Pew, March 30–April 28, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/15/about-9-in-10-palestinians-in-the-west-bank-and-east-jerusalem-see-israeli-checkpoints-as-a-major-problem/)). OCHA counted 925 movement obstacles in March 2026, 43% above the average of the previous two decades ([Times of Israel, July 27, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/restrictions-on-palestinian-movement-in-west-bank-allegedly-at-levels-not-seen-in-decades/)). Morshed Awad of Beit Ummar, who said the army closed both of the village's main entrances with gates, told the Times of Israel:

<Quote who="Morshed Awad" role="Resident of Beit Ummar, southern West Bank" source="Times of Israel" url="https://www.timesofisrael.com/restrictions-on-palestinian-movement-in-west-bank-allegedly-at-levels-not-seen-in-decades/" date="July 27, 2026">Residents use alternative roads. The drive from Beit Ummar to Hebron used to take five minutes. Today it takes 40 minutes. It creates real hardship.</Quote>

The IDF said checkpoint decisions are made "solely in accordance with operational assessments and security considerations" (same report).

## What the parties say

Of the lists running, only Ra'am and the Joint List name a Palestinian state as a goal. No Jewish-majority list that answered Ynet names one as a goal. They differ on how much land to annex, from all of it (Religious Zionism–Zehut, Otzma Yehudit) to Area C ([B'Yachad](/parties/byachad), [Yisrael Beiteinu](/parties/yb)) to "sovereignty through actions, not words" (Reservists). The Democrats' own word is "separation."

## Misreadings

**"Annexation" is not mostly a Knesset vote.** The bills are symbolic or stalled. The change has come through planning approvals, the 2024 delegation order and the 2026 land decisions, which Smotrich himself describes as de facto sovereignty.

**"Separation" is not "two states."** Without the map of Areas A, B and C, "sovereignty over Area C" sounds like annexing everything, and the Democrats' "separation" sounds like a Palestinian state. They are different plans.

**The blocs do not line up with the positions.** Two lists running against Netanyahu, B'Yachad and Yisrael Beiteinu, back annexing Area C, and Yisrael Beiteinu backs building in E1. The Haredi parties, Netanyahu's allies, do not make the issue central to their campaigns, Ynet reports.

**The terms are positions.** The biblical name used in most of the party statements above or "West Bank," "sovereignty" or "annexation," "farms" or "outposts," "nationalist crime," "Jewish terror" or "settler violence": each word signals where its speaker stands.
