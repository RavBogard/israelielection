## Size, growth and geography

Haredim (singular Haredi), often called ultra-Orthodox in English, are Israel's most strictly religious Jewish community, with their own school systems and their own parties. In the Central Bureau of Statistics' 2026 survey, 11.5% of Jewish adults described themselves as Haredi ([CBS via Ynet, Sept 2026](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/sjc9lpcufl)). Because the community is very young, its share of the whole population is larger than its share of adults.

<Note title="Other counts">
The Israel Democracy Institute's 2025 yearbook counts 1,452,350 Haredim, 14.3% of Israel's population, up from about 750,000 in 2009 ([IDI, Dec 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf)). The Haredi Institute for Public Affairs, a Haredi-run research body, counts about 1.26 million, 12.5% of the population and 16% of Jews, at the end of 2024 ([Haredi Institute, 2025](https://machon.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/english-digital-1-1-1.pdf)). The counts define membership differently; both show the share rising.
</Note>

The community grows by about 4.2% a year, and 57% of Haredim are under 20, against 31% of the general population. Fertility has fallen from 7.5 births per woman in 2003–05 to 6.5 in 2021–23, still about three times the rate among other Jewish women (2.2) ([IDI, Dec 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf)).

Projections differ. A CBS projection cited by IDI has Haredim at about 20% of the population by 2040 and 24.4% by 2050; IDI's own estimate for 2050 is about 22% ([IDI, Feb 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/63385)). An older CBS projection, from 2017, put the share at 32% by 2065 ([Israel National News, May 2017](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/229972)). The Haredi Institute's 2065 scenarios range from 17.7%, if fertility falls substantially, to 22.6%, if it stays at its 2022 level ([Haredi Institute, 2025](https://machon.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/english-digital-1-1-1.pdf)).

The community is not one bloc. By the Haredi Institute's count, about a third are Sephardi and the rest belong to Ashkenazi streams: the Lithuanian (Litvish) stream, meaning non-Hasidic Ashkenazi Haredim, and the Hasidic courts, with Chabad counted separately.

<Chart id="haredim.streams" />

The streams map loosely onto parties. United Torah Judaism (UTJ) joins Agudat Yisrael, the Hasidic party, and Degel HaTorah, the Lithuanian one ([Davar, Sept 2026](https://www.davar1.co.il/696518/); [UTJ](/parties/utj)). Shas, the Sephardi Haredi party, is a Haredi-led party with a traditional base: many of its voters are not Haredi ([Shas](/parties/shas); see [Masorti Israel](/communities/masorti)).

Most Haredim live in a handful of cities. In 2023 Jerusalem held 22.6% of all Haredim, Bnei Brak 15.6%, and the four "satellite" cities (Beit Shemesh, Modi'in Illit, Beitar Illit and Elad) 25% ([IDI, Dec 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf)).

<Chart id="haredim.cities" />

Two of the largest, Modi'in Illit and Beitar Illit, are settlements, Haredi cities in the West Bank. Their residents are counted in settler population totals (see [Settlers](/communities/settlers)). Over the past decade, about 75,000 Haredim left Jerusalem and 42,000 left Bnei Brak ([Haredi Institute via Ynet, Dec 2024](https://www.ynetnews.com/jewish-world/article/h1cch618jg)).

Work divides the community by sex. Haredi women's employment has risen to about the level of other Jewish women; Haredi men's has been flat for a decade.

<Chart id="haredim.employment" />

Haredi men earn on average 49% of what other Jewish men earn ([IDI via The Media Line, Dec 2025](https://themedialine.org/headlines/israel-democracy-institute-report-finds-stalled-integration-of-ultra-orthodox-men-as-community-growth-accelerates/)).

Poverty and home ownership have to be read together, and the sources disagree on both. IDI counts 33% of Haredi families below the poverty line in 2023, against 14% of other Jewish families, and IDI's report, as summarized by The Media Line, says 75% of Haredim own their apartments ([IDI, Dec 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf); [The Media Line, Dec 2025](https://themedialine.org/headlines/israel-democracy-institute-report-finds-stalled-integration-of-ultra-orthodox-men-as-community-growth-accelerates/)). The Haredi Institute counts 40% of Haredi households as poor in 2022, against 11% of other Jewish households, and puts Haredi home ownership at about 65%, down from 69%, against 61% among other Jews ([Haredi Institute, 2025](https://machon.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/english-digital-1-1-1.pdf)).

## How their towns have voted, 2019–2022

It shows how these towns voted, computed from the Central Elections Committee's locality files; it is not a count of how every Haredi voted.

<Chart id="haredim.towns" />

About nine in ten votes in these towns go to UTJ or Shas in every election. Likud took between 2.6% and 5.1%. The only outside list that grew was the far-right one: after Itamar Ben-Gvir's Otzma Yehudit joined the Religious Zionism list in 2021, it took 5.7% and then 5.4%, with 11.2% in Elad in 2022. Turnout in the five towns ran from 76.3% to 80.0%, against 67.4% to 71.5% nationally.

Surveys show the same picture. In IDI's pooled post-election surveys, self-identified Haredim voted 58% UTJ, 28% Shas, 7% Religious Zionism–Otzma and 4% Likud in 2022; 86% voted for a Haredi list, down from 89% in 2021 ([IDI, June 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/64807)). Nationally, Shas won 8.25% and 11 seats in 2022 and UTJ 5.88% and 7 seats ([CEC, Nov 2022](https://media25.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv)).

Shas's vote reaches well beyond Haredim. A veteran Shas activist told the Times of Israel in August 2026: "Shas has a base of four seats. It will hold on to that no matter what. But everything beyond that is up for grabs." The paper describes the rest as moderately traditional, working-class voters on Shas's periphery ([Times of Israel, Aug 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/eisenkot-threatens-to-siphon-traditional-voters-away-from-a-panicking-shas/)).

For 2026, UTJ filed one list, but with three factions, for the first time apart from a few days in 1992: Degel HaTorah, Agudat Yisrael and a new faction, Homat Torat Yisrael, led by Meir Porush and Elyakim Stark, whose members may split from UTJ once elected ([Davar, Sept 2026](https://www.davar1.co.il/696518/)). Asked in July 2026 to commit to Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc after the election, UTJ's Moshe Gafni cited his rabbi's ruling that "as of now, there is no bloc," and Agudat Yisrael answered, "First you pass the laws, then we'll talk," according to a Channel 12 report cited by the Times of Israel ([Times of Israel, July 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/netanyahu-said-seeking-haredi-loyalty-after-election-but-party-leaders-noncommittal/)). Only 36% of Haredim say they will vote for the same party as always, against 7% of the public ([IDI, Aug 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65364)). No 2026 survey of Haredi vote intention with a full Haredi sample was found.

## What they think on the six issues

**The draft.** Draft orders and arrests of yeshiva students who did not report (called "draft evaders" in Israeli debate) are covered on the [Haredi draft](/issues/haredi-draft) page. In early 2024, IDI found that Haredim were "the only group in which the majority of respondents think that the new circumstances created by the war should not affect the issue of conscription of Haredim in the future" ([IDI, March 2024](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/53305)). In July 2026, IDI found "very high levels of support among Haredim" for the law to halt arrests of what it called "deserters," against 25.3% support among Jews overall ([IDI Voice Index, July 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65483); [IDI data, July 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/media/31733/july-2026-index-data.pdf)). In 2025, 0.3% of Haredi men aged 21 to 44 served in the reserves in an average month, against 1.7% of secular men ([IDI, Aug 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65427)).

**Courts.** Haredi trust in the Supreme Court fell from 11% in 2023 to 7% in 2024 and 3% in 2025, and 93.5% agreed that the court "intervenes too much in government decisions" ([IDI Democracy Index, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)).

<Chart id="haredim.trust" />

**War and hostages.** Haredi trust in the IDF was 61% in 2025, below every other Jewish group. Yet the share agreeing that "Israel ensures the security of its citizens" rose from 23% in 2022 to 68% in 2025, while among secular Jews it fell from 52% to 30% ([IDI, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). In August 2025, a majority of Shas and UTJ voters supported "the release of all the hostages in exchange for the cessation of hostilities and the withdrawal of the IDF from all of the Gaza Strip," while support for Jewish settlement in Gaza was highest among Haredim ([IDI, Sept 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61601)). In September 2026, about a quarter of Haredim said October 7 was central to their vote, against about three-quarters or more of secular Jews ([IDI, Sept 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177)).

**West Bank.** No 2026 survey publishes a Haredi figure on the West Bank. Pew's 2025 report combines Haredim with Datiim: 78% of that group said continued settlement building helps Israel's security (secular Jews 27%), and 9% think Israel and a Palestinian state can coexist peacefully (secular 25%) ([Pew, June 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/06/pg_2025.06.03_israel_report.pdf)).

**Religion and state.** 41% of Haredim name parties' positions on religion and state as the most influential factor in their choice, against 17% of non-Haredim; 5% name security ([IDI, Aug 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65364)). In 2025, 73% said the democratic component of the state is too dominant relative to the Jewish one ([IDI, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)).

**Economy.** 3% of Haredim name the economy as their most influential factor, against 21% of non-Haredim ([IDI, Aug 2026](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65364)). Haredim are the Jewish group most satisfied with their own lives: 85% call their personal situation good or very good, against 43% of secular Jews ([IDI, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)).

## Who they are

The draft fight has moved onto the streets. On June 1, 2026, after six yeshiva students were arrested (by the group's own count), the Jerusalem Faction, a movement of about 60,000, used its "national alert system" to block Route 4 at the Ganot Interchange, the entrance to Jerusalem, and roads in Bnei Brak, Safed and Netivot.

Another part of the community takes the opposite view, and in 2026 it has its own list. The Haredi Public party launched its campaign in August 2026 with billboards reading "Those who do not learn should serve" and "Haredi children too need math and English."

Its chairman, Moti Leitner, said: "To fight poverty, we will work to ensure our children acquire basic life skills and study math and English at a high level" ([Times of Israel, Aug 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/launching-campaign-new-haredi-party-slams-detachment-stupidity-of-existing-factions/)).
