The Druze are an Arabic-speaking religious community, most of whom live in villages on Mount Carmel and in the Galilee. Statistics usually place them among Israel's Arab citizens. The Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) counts them inside its "Arab" population group, where they made up 7.4% at the end of 2022 ([CBS, April 2023](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Society_&_Culture/druze-stats2023.pdf)). In a 2014–15 survey, 71% of Druze said they are ethnically Arab, against 99% of Muslims and 96% of Christians ([Pew Research Center, March 2016](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/israels-religiously-divided-society/)). Asked which part of their identity matters most, most name something else. In May 2026, 64.8% of Druze respondents named Israeli citizenship as the most important component of their identity ([Konrad Adenauer Program, Tel Aviv University, May 2026](https://dayan.org/file-download/download/public/18837)). In an older IDI survey (May 2016), in which Druze were about 12% of 362 Arab respondents, 54% named their religious identity, 37% Israeli, 5% Arab, and none Palestinian ([IDI, May 2016](https://www.idi.org.il/media/8292/)). Unlike Muslim and Christian Arab citizens, Druze men are drafted. This page treats the Druze as a group of their own. The [Palestinian citizens of Israel](/communities/palestinian-citizens) page covers Muslims and Christians.

## Size, growth and geography

CBS counted about 150,000 Druze at the end of 2022, 1.6% of Israel's population ([CBS, April 2023](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Society_&_Culture/druze-stats2023.pdf)). That count includes the Druze of the Golan Heights, and the release does not give a figure without them. They are covered in the box at the end of this page and left out of the figures that follow wherever the source allows. In 2021, about 81% of Druze lived in the Northern District and 19% in the Haifa District. The largest communities were Daliyat al-Karmel (17,500), Yarka (17,300) and Maghar (13,500). The median age was 29.6 at the end of 2021. Fertility has fallen from 7.92 children per woman in 1964 to 2.00 in 2021, and the growth rate was 1.2% that year (same source).

The Carmel villages are Daliyat al-Karmel and Isfiya. The Galilee villages include Yarka, Julis, the seat of the spiritual leadership, Beit Jann, Hurfeish, Kisra-Sumei, Yanuh-Jat, Sajur, Ein al-Asad and Peki'in. Druze also live in mixed towns such as Maghar, Abu Snan, Rameh and Shefa-Amr. In 2022 the employment rate was 50.8%, against 42.6% among Muslims and 68.7% among Christians, and 14.8% of adults held an academic degree ([CBS](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Society_&_Culture/druze-stats2023.pdf)).

Druze men have been conscripted since 1956 ([Zeidan Atashi, former Druze MK, via Jewish Virtual Library](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/druze-in-israel-and-the-question-of-compulsory-military-service)). No primary IDF enlistment rate was found. In Pew's 2014–15 survey, about six in ten Druze men had served (45%) or were serving (15%) ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2016/03/21/5-facts-about-israeli-druze-a-unique-religious-and-ethnic-group/)).

## How their villages have voted, 2019–2022

The figures below add up the ten localities that a Moshe Dayan Center study treats as at least 95% Druze ([Shanan and Ilat, 2021](https://dayan.org/he/content/5714)): Daliyat al-Karmel, Yarka, Beit Jann, Hurfeish, Kisra-Sumei, Yanuh-Jat, Peki'in, Julis, Sajur and Ein al-Asad. They leave out Isfiya and the mixed towns, whose results cannot be read as Druze votes, and the Golan villages.

<Chart id="druze.vote" />

The vote goes overwhelmingly to the parties IDI classes as Zionist, about 90% in 2022 ([IDI, December 2022](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/47986)), but it is not a vote for the right. In 2022, Likud, Religious Zionism, Shas and United Torah Judaism together took about 13% in the ten villages. Most of the rest went to opposition parties ([CEC, computed](https://media25.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv)).

The vote follows the home villages of Druze candidates. In 2022, Beit Jann gave Meretz 61.6%; Ali Salalha, Meretz's no. 4, is from Beit Jann. Daliyat al-Karmel gave National Unity 54.9%; Akram Hasson, its no. 16, lives there ([CEC, computed](https://media25.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv)). The Dayan Center study, covering 1996 to 2020, found the same pattern: "nearly every party that placed a Druze candidate in a realistic spot won the most support in his village" ([Shanan and Ilat, 2021, translated from Hebrew](https://dayan.org/he/content/5714)).

Turnout has fallen from 76.8% in 1996 ([Dayan Center](https://dayan.org/he/content/5714)) to 50.8% in 2022, well below the national rate.

<Chart id="druze.turnout" />

Only one Druze member of Knesset was elected in 2022, Hamad Amar of Yisrael Beiteinu. IDI called it "an all-time low" ([IDI](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/47986)). For 2026, Ali Salalha finished 18th in the Democrats' primary ([Ynetnews, July 20, 2026](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/synmdasnzx)). A Druze party, Brit Achim, was launched in May 2026 by Wajdi Sarhan, a reserve officer ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/former-idf-commander-wajdi-sarhan-launches-first-druze-political-party-in-decades/)). In September Sarhan announced he would not run, saying that with the small parties "the conditions for a joint and equal run agreement were not created" ([Ynetnews, September 8, 2026](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/bfi2ue5qp)).

## What they think on the six issues

No large Druze-only survey exists. In IDI's Democracy Index 2025 (fieldwork May 4–28, 2025), Druze were about 8.4% of 364 Arab respondents, roughly 31 people. IDI itself limited its use of the religion breakdown for that reason ([IDI, 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). The figures below are a rough guide only. No 2026 survey breaks out Druze views on any of the six issues.

<Chart id="druze.idi-2025" />

**[The Haredi draft](/issues/haredi-draft).** IDI 2025 gives no Druze breakdown. The community's own draft is described above.

**[Courts](/issues/courts).** Druze trust the Supreme Court more than Muslims or Christians do (61%). They are also the most likely of the three groups to say it intervenes too much (71%). 83.5% support a constitution ([IDI, 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)).

**[War and hostages](/issues/war-hostages).** Trust in the IDF rose from 64% in 2024 to 84% in 2025, about the Jewish level (83.5%). Two-thirds (67%) say the state provides security for its citizens, and 81% are optimistic about Israel's future (same source). No sourced total of Druze soldiers killed since October 7, 2023 was found.

**[West Bank](/issues/west-bank).** The only Druze figure found is old: in 2014–15, 66% said settlements hurt Israel's security ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/israels-religiously-divided-society/)).

**[Religion and state](/issues/religion-state).** No Druze poll on the 2018 Nation-State Law was found. The evidence is how the community acted (below).

**[Economy](/issues/economy).** 58% say the state manages to ensure citizens' welfare, against 31–32% of Muslims and Christians. But only 29% trust the government and 19% trust the Knesset ([IDI, 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)).

## Who they are

The Druze spiritual leader since 1993 has been Sheikh Mowafaq Tarif ([Moment, February 2018](https://momentmag.com/interview-sheik-mowafaq-tafik-spiritual-leader-israeli-druze/)), born in Julis, who succeeded his grandfather, Sheikh Amin Tarif ([University of Haifa](https://rlgslsb.haifa.ac.il/en/2025/02/sheikh-mowafaq-tarif/)). Jewish-Israeli leaders have called the bond a "blood covenant," *brit damim*. Sephardi Chief Rabbi David Yosef, visiting Julis in August 2025, said: "We have a blood covenant with the Druze community, and the state must do everything to protect the Druze wherever they are" ([Times of Israel, August 28, 2025](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/chief-rabbi-stresses-blood-covenant-with-druze-brethren-in-meeting-of-religious-leaders/)). The Druze speakers quoted here use other words: loyalty, sacrifice, equality.

The 2018 Nation-State Law tested that bond. On July 25, 2018, about 100 Druze reserve officers protested it ([Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/articles/0,7340,L-5316576,00.html)).

<Quote who="Brig. Gen. (res.) Amal As'ad" source="Ynetnews" url="https://www.ynetnews.com/articles/0,7340,L-5316576,00.html" date="July 25, 2018">After we built this home together with the Jews, the Nationality Law excludes the Druze community from that home and put it outside the fence.</Quote>

On August 4, 2018, some 90,000 Druze, with other opponents of the law, filled Rabin Square, by Ynet's estimate ([Ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-5322446,00.html)). Sheikh Tarif told the rally:

<Quote who="Sheikh Mowafaq Tarif" role="Druze spiritual leader" source="Ynet (translated from Hebrew)" url="https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-5322446,00.html" date="August 4, 2018">Despite our unreserved loyalty to the state, the state does not see us as equals.</Quote>

From July 13, 2025, clashes in Suwayda in southern Syria pitted "Druze fighters against Sunni Bedouin tribes and the army and its allies" ([France 24, July 17, 2025](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20250717-death-toll-from-syria-clashes-rises-to-594-after-days-of-sectarian-violence)). The UN Commission of Inquiry on Syria reported in March 2026 that more than 1,700 people were killed and nearly 200,000 displaced, in violations "mainly against the Druze and Bedouin communities" by actors including government forces and Druze armed groups. It found that after government forces withdrew, "Druze armed groups then attacked Bedouin civilians" ([UN News, March 27, 2026](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/03/1167210)). Israel struck Syrian government forces in Suwayda, and dozens of Israeli civilians crossed the border near Majdal Shams ([Ynetnews, July 15, 2025](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/by11g11e4ull)). Hamad Amar said: "The prime minister and defense minister have repeatedly promised to protect our brethren across the border" (same source).

<Note title="The Golan Druze">
About 26,000–29,000 Druze live in four villages in the Israeli-annexed Golan Heights: Majdal Shams, Mas'ade, Buq'ata and Ein Qiniyye ([Times of Israel, January 2025](https://www.timesofisrael.com/taboo-no-more-one-in-five-golan-druze-now-holds-israeli-citizenship/); [March 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/after-years-of-hesitation-more-druze-of-the-golan-heights-seek-israeli-citizenship/)). Israel annexed the Golan under Israeli law in 1981. Most residents hold permanent residency, not citizenship, and only citizens vote for the Knesset. They are not counted in this page's vote figures.

At the end of 2024, about 6,000 held citizenship, roughly one in five ([Times of Israel, January 6, 2025](https://www.timesofisrael.com/taboo-no-more-one-in-five-golan-druze-now-holds-israeli-citizenship/)). Applications numbered 438 in 2022 and 406 in 2023 (same source), then surged to 3,750 in 2025. By March 2026 about 38% of Majdal Shams residents and 37–43% in the other villages held citizenship ([Times of Israel, March 15, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/after-years-of-hesitation-more-druze-of-the-golan-heights-seek-israeli-citizenship/)).

In 2022 the four villages had 2,755 registered voters, and 23.2% voted: Yesh Atid 24.3%, National Unity 19.4%, Likud 15.5%, Yisrael Beiteinu 15.5% ([CEC, computed](https://media25.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv)). The 2026 roll has not been published and will be larger.

On July 27, 2024, a rocket, which Israel said Hezbollah fired and Hezbollah denied, struck a soccer field in Majdal Shams and killed 12 Golan Druze children. Council head Dolan Abu Saleh, four of whose relatives were among the dead, said: "All of the Majdal Shams family lean towards peace. We do not want war or retribution" ([The National, July 30, 2024](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2024/07/31/majdal-shams-residents-golan-heights/)).
</Note>
