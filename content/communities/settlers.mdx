"Settlers" is a cross-cutting group: it is defined by where people live, not by religion or origin. It takes in Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) families in large towns a few kilometers over the Green Line, commuters in suburban towns near Jerusalem and Tel Aviv, and national-religious communities deep in the occupied West Bank. The usual shorthand, 36% Haredi, 36% religious Zionist and 28% secular, is the settler leadership's own estimate, from the Yesha Council in May 2023. Newer counts confirm only the Haredi third.

## Size, growth and geography

On January 1, 2026, 541,085 Jews lived in West Bank settlements, not counting East Jerusalem ([Interior Ministry population registry, compiled by former National Union MK Yaakov Katz for Bet El Institutions, which he founded, January 2026](https://jr.co.il/ys/West_Bank_Jewish_Population_Stats_Jan_2026.pdf)). That is 5.32% of Israel's population ([JNS, February 2026](https://www.jns.org/exclusive-judea-samaria-jewish-population-grew-at-twice-israels-overall-rate-in-2025/)). Counting the Jewish neighborhoods of East Jerusalem as well, the total is "over 750,000" in 147 settlements ([EU, 2025 settlements report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf)). Israel does not treat those neighborhoods as settlements; the EU, the UN and Peace Now do.

<Chart id="settlers.count" />

The settler population grew 2.2% in 2025, double Israel's 1.1% ([registry compilation, January 2026](https://jr.co.il/ys/West_Bank_Jewish_Population_Stats_Jan_2026.pdf)). The growth is not where an American reader might expect. Ma'ale Adumim, the best-known suburban settlement, has stopped growing: 40,943 residents in February 2021, 40,800 in January 2026. Over the same five years the Modi'in Illit bloc grew 13.8%, Beitar Illit 15.7%, Samaria 21.5%, the Jordan Valley 25.8% and Eastern Etzion 35.0% (same source). The growth is in the Haredi cities and in small communities far from the Green Line.

**Who they are.** Three estimates agree that about a third of settlers are Haredi. They disagree on the rest, partly because they measure different things.

<Chart id="settlers.composition" />

**Where they live.** The two largest settlements, Modi'in Illit and Beitar Illit, are Haredi cities in the West Bank. They are counted in settler totals here, but they are covered mainly on [the Haredim page](/communities/haredim). Beitar Illit's mayor said in 2017, "We came here due to the housing distress" ([Times of Israel, 2017](https://www.timesofisrael.com/black-is-the-new-orange-30-of-settlers-are-now-haredim/)). Next come suburban settlements (in Israeli usage, "quality-of-life" settlements) such as Ma'ale Adumim, Givat Ze'ev and Ariel. By this site's classification, 26.7% of settlers live in them ([registry compilation, January 2026](https://jr.co.il/ys/West_Bank_Jewish_Population_Stats_Jan_2026.pdf)). The remainder live mostly in about 80 other communities, most of them small and national-religious, plus the older farming villages of the Jordan Valley.

<Chart id="settlers.towns" />

**Outposts.** Peace Now, an Israeli group that opposes settlement, counted 86 new outposts in 2025 ([Peace Now, February 2026](https://peacenow.org.il/en/summary-of-2025-in-settlements)), against a previous record of 26 in 2023 ([EU, 2025 report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf)). By the same EU report's count, using Peace Now and Kerem Navot figures, farm outposts "effectively control more than 1,070,000 dunams — approximately 18% of the West Bank." The government approved 104 new or retroactively legalized settlements over its four-year term ([Times of Israel, September 28, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/two-new-west-bank-settlements-inaugurated-with-cabinet-ministers-on-hand/)).

## How these towns voted, 2019–2022

These results come from the Central Elections Committee's official locality files, grouped by type of settlement. They show how these towns voted, not how settlers voted. Votes cast by double envelope, mostly by soldiers, are counted nationally, and East Jerusalem cannot be separated from the rest of Jerusalem.

<Note title="Our classification">
The grouping below is this site's own judgment, published so readers can check it. Settlements are the localities with CBS codes 3400–3899 in the Central Elections Committee files (124 in 2022).

**Haredi cities (8):** Modi'in Illit, Beitar Illit, Emmanuel, Kochav Yaakov (merged in the files with Tel Tzion), Matityahu, Ma'ale Amos, Asfar/Metzad, Nachliel.

**Suburban settlements (11 in 2022):** Ma'ale Adumim, Ariel, Givat Ze'ev, Oranit, Alfei Menashe, Har Adar, Karnei Shomron, Shaar Shomron (Sha'arei Tikva and Etz Efraim before 2022; Ma'ale Shomron before its merger), Beit Aryeh, Ganei Modi'in, Barkan.

**Jordan Valley:** the 24–25 small villages of the valley.

**Other settlements (about 80):** everything else, mostly national-religious communities such as Beit El, Ofra, Shiloh, Kedumim, Efrat, Kiryat Arba and Yitzhar.

The suburban list is the least certain: Karnei Shomron gave 54.4% of its 2022 vote to Religious Zionism–Otzma Yehudit, and Givat Ze'ev gave about 45% to the Haredi parties ([CEC, 2022](https://media25.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv)).
</Note>

<Chart id="settlers.vote-2022" />

In 2022 the settlements voted as three blocs. The Haredi cities gave 90.3% of their vote to Shas and United Torah Judaism. The other, mostly national-religious, settlements gave 57.3% to Religious Zionism–Otzma Yehudit. The suburban settlements' largest party was Likud, at 34.1% ([CEC, 2022](https://media25.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv)). Turnout in all settlements was 75.3%, against 70.6% nationally.

<Chart id="settlers.vote-trend" />

Religious Zionism–Otzma Yehudit, the joint list of two parties the Israel Democracy Institute places [on the far right](https://en.idi.org.il/israeli-elections-and-parties/parties/religious-zionist-party/), is settler-heavy but not settler-made. In 2022 settlement localities held 3.9% of Israel's eligible voters and supplied 12.2% of the list's 516,470 votes. In April 2019, the URWP list drew 21.1% of its votes from settlements, and the 2021 Religious Zionism list drew 17.9% ([CEC locality files](https://media25.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv)). As the list grew nationally, the settler share of its vote fell: about 92% of the list's growth from 2021 to 2022 came from outside the settlement localities. That story, a shift within the right, is told on [the masorti page](/communities/masorti).

No 2026 poll of settlers' vote intention was found.

## What they think on the issues

No settler-specific opinion survey from 2024–26 was found, from any pollster. What exists are breakdowns by religiosity or by vote, which overlap with settlers but are not the same group. The town results above suggest why a single "settler opinion" is hard to state: the Haredi third and the national-religious third vote for different parties, with different priorities.

- **The Haredi draft.** The Haredi cities vote overwhelmingly for Shas and United Torah Judaism, the two Haredi parties. The national-religious settlements vote for the Religious Zionist lists, whose community's views are set out on [the Religious Zionists page](/communities/religious-zionists). No survey measures either group's view as settlers. See [the Haredi draft](/issues/haredi-draft).
- **The West Bank.** No settler breakdown exists. Among coalition voters, 50% chose "full annexation without granting civil rights" as their preferred approach, against 25% of all Israelis ([INSS, June 16–22, 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-june-2026/)). See [the West Bank and annexation](/issues/west-bank).
- **Courts, war and hostages, religion and state, the economy.** No settler breakdown found. See [courts](/issues/courts), [the war and the hostages](/issues/war-hostages), [religion and state](/issues/religion-state) and [the economy](/issues/economy).

## Who they are

Settler leaders describe the settlements' civil status as unfinished. In 2023, when the population passed half a million, the head of the Gush Etzion Regional Council put it this way:

<Quote who="Shlomo Ne'eman" role="Head, Gush Etzion Regional Council" source="Times of Israel" url="https://www.timesofisrael.com/yesha-settler-umbrella-group-says-over-half-a-million-israelis-live-in-west-bank/" date="May 12, 2023">Unfortunately, even after we've crossed half a million residents, we are still prisoners of a military administration that does not correspond to the reality on the ground.</Quote>

The same military administration rules the West Bank's Palestinian majority, who, unlike the settlers, have no vote for the Knesset (see [who votes, and who doesn't](/how-it-works/who-votes)). Since 2023 it has changed for the settlers. Under the coalition's agreements, powers over planning, land, infrastructure and enforcement were, in the EU's words, "transferred from the Civil Administration and the military chain of command to Finance Minister Smotrich and the Settlement Administration" ([EU, 2025 report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf)).

**Settler violence.** UN OCHA counted 1,828 settler attacks causing casualties or damage in 2025, up from 1,189 in 2023 (via the [EU, 2025 report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf)). Israeli security officials put those involved at about 300 people, about 70 of them "hardcore." The Israeli rights group Yesh Din found that 93.6% of investigation files from 2005 to 2025 were closed without an indictment ([Times of Israel, September 9, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/as-settler-violence-surges-some-ask-whether-israel-cant-crack-down-or-simply-wont/)). In 2025, by OCHA's count, settlers killed nine Palestinians in the West Bank, and for six more it is unknown whether settlers or Israeli forces killed them; Palestinians killed 17 Israelis in the West Bank, six of them members of Israeli forces ([OCHA, Jan 7, 2026](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/humanitarian-situation-update-352-west-bank)).

Some settlers themselves draw the line between the few hundred and the half million. Israel's ambassador to the United States lives in the settlement of Eli ([Jewish Insider, November 2024](https://jewishinsider.com/2024/11/yechiel-leiter-israel-ambassador-washington/)):

<Quote who="Yechiel Leiter" role="Israeli ambassador to the United States, resident of Eli" source="Ynet, via Times of Israel" url="https://www.timesofisrael.com/israels-us-envoy-says-settler-violence-a-stain-costing-the-nation-friends/" date="March 25, 2026">It's a handful of a few hundred people who are staining an entire enterprise — and everyone is silent.</Quote>

Across the West Bank, 87% of Palestinian residents told the Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research in August 2026 that they were worried settlement expansion and new outposts would cost them their land or homes ([PCPSR, Aug 5–8, 2026](https://www.pcpsr.org/en/node/1014)). A Palestinian on the receiving end of these attacks sees the state's role differently. "The army comes to protect the settlers," said Luai Beiruti of the village of Qusra ([Times of Israel, September 9, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/as-settler-violence-surges-some-ask-whether-israel-cant-crack-down-or-simply-wont/)).
