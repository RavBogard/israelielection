Palestinian citizens of Israel are the Arab Muslims, among them the Negev Bedouin, and Arab Christians who hold Israeli citizenship. Israeli officials, the Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) and Israeli pollsters such as IDI call them "Arabs," "Arab citizens" or "Arab Israelis." The name itself is contested, and the contest shows up in the surveys: asked in 2019 to pick a label, most picked a Palestinian one; asked to pick the single most important part of who they are, most pick "Arab" or "Israeli citizenship". This page uses "Palestinian citizens of Israel" and gives each source's own term where it quotes one.

<Note title="The name, and who is inside the numbers">
CBS counts Druze inside its "Arab" population group, where they were 7.4% at the end of 2022 ([CBS, April 2023](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Society_&_Culture/druze-stats2023.pdf)). IDI and the Konrad Adenauer Program at Tel Aviv University write "Arab citizens" or "Arab society." The Abraham Initiatives uses "Arab citizens" in its crime report and "Palestinian citizens" on [its homepage](https://abrahaminitiatives.org/). Druze have [their own page](/communities/druze). Where a source breaks out Muslims and Christians, this page uses those figures. Where it does not, the figure includes Druze, and the page says so.
</Note>

## Size, growth and geography

How many there are depends on who is counted. CBS put Israel's Arab population at 2,173,000 in September 2026, about 21% of 10,305,000 residents ([CBS via Times of Israel, September 9, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/ahead-of-rosh-hashanah-israel-population-is-at-10305000-up-1-2-over-last-year/)). IDI notes that CBS's Arab count includes Arab residents of East Jerusalem, most of whom hold permanent residency rather than citizenship, and it includes Druze. IDI's split is for the end of 2020: 1,957,270 Arabs including East Jerusalem (21.1%), and 1,595,300 citizens without it (17.2%). The difference was "almost 362,000" East Jerusalem residents ([IDI, March 2022](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/38540)). East Jerusalem Palestinians are outside this page's group and appear here only in that population note.

Among citizens outside East Jerusalem at the end of 2020, 82.9% were Muslim, 9.2% Druze and 7.9% Christian. Just over half (51.6%) lived in the north and Galilee, 19.7% in the Triangle, 17.5% in the Negev and 8.3% in mixed cities ([IDI, 2020 data](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/38540)). CBS counted 1.849 million Muslims in Israel in May 2026 ([CBS via TPS, May 26, 2026](https://tps.co.il/articles/israels-muslim-population-reaches-1-85-million-ahead-of-eid-al-adha/)).

The Negev Bedouin numbered about 325,000 at the end of 2023, about a fifth of the Negev's population. They grew sixfold between 1995 and 2023, and 48.6% are under 17. Between 70,000 and 90,000 live in what the State Comptroller calls "dispersed communities" and are commonly called unrecognized villages ([State Comptroller via Jerusalem Post, April 28, 2026](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-894474)). In the unrecognized villages, IDI found "only 10 elementary schools, and not a single secondary school" ([IDI, 2021](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/38540)).

The newest income figures found are from 2018: 45.3% of Arab families were below the poverty line, against 13.4% of Jewish families, and Arab men earned 77% of what Jewish men earned ([IDI, 2018 data](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/38540)).

## How their towns have voted, 2019–2022

Turnout was highest, 64.8%, in March 2020, when four parties ran as one Joint List, and fell to what IDI called "an all-time low" of 44.6% a year later, after Ra'am left ([IDI, April 2021](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/34420)).

The table shows how five large towns voted. Each list appears under the name it ran under that year. Rahat in the Negev is Ra'am's base. In the Galilee and the Triangle, the 2022 vote split three ways.

In 2022, Balad's 138,617 votes (2.91%) fell below the 3.25% threshold and won no seats, while Ra'am and Hadash–Ta'al won five each ([CEC results, computed](https://data.gov.il/dataset/votes-knesset)). In Arab and Druze localities that year, Ra'am took 35.2%, Hadash–Ta'al 28.8%, Balad 21.7% and Zionist parties 14.3% ([IDI, December 2022](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/47986)).

For 2026 two lists are running: Ra'am under Mansour Abbas, and a re-formed Joint List of Hadash, Ta'al and Balad. The Central Elections Committee disqualified both in September. On October 2 the Supreme Court unanimously reinstated them, and Balad's leader Sami Abu Shehadeh withdrew his own candidacy ([Al Jazeera, October 2, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/2/israels-supreme-court-overturns-election-panel-ban-on-arab-parties)). In three TV polls after the lists were filed, the Joint List polled at 7–8 seats and Ra'am at 4–6 ([Times of Israel, September 10, 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/election-polls-show-little-change-continued-deadlock-after-party-slates-finalized/)). In May, KAP projected Arab turnout of 53% if the parties ran as three separate lists, as in 2022 ([KAP, May 2026](https://dayan.org/file-download/download/public/18837)). Pollster Yousef Makladeh estimated that a Hadash–Ta'al–Balad slate could lift it "from about 53% to 59%" ([Ynet, August 2, 2026](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/s1xafktsfg)).

## What they think on the six issues

The best cross-tabulation is IDI's Democracy Index 2025: 364 Arab respondents interviewed by phone in Arabic in May 2025, including Druze ([IDI, 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). The newest Arab-sector survey used here is KAP's, from April 26 to May 3, 2026. Its sample of 500 also includes Druze. Neither survey was taken after the October 2 ruling.

**What decides the vote.** Asked which issue would most affect their choice of party, 49.1% of Arab respondents named the cost of living, against 16.8% of Jews ([IDI, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). Crime is the top item on the community's agenda: 71.3% named it the most important issue in May 2026 ([KAP](https://dayan.org/file-download/download/public/18837)). The Abraham Initiatives' count of those killed more than doubled from 2022 to 2023 and has stayed near that level ([Abraham Initiatives, December 2025](https://abrahaminitiatives.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2025-Crime-and-Violence-Report.pdf)).

<Chart id="palestinian-citizens.killed" />

**[The Haredi draft](/issues/haredi-draft).** Only 1.7% named Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) conscription as their deciding issue, against 16.6% of Jews ([IDI, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). Palestinian citizens are not drafted: it has been "the declared policy of the Israeli army" not to draft Arabs, IDI's Mordechai Kremnitzer and Talya Steiner wrote ([IDI, June 2013](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/6807)). The question asked of them is civic service. In May 2026, 75.8% supported voluntary non-security civic service for Arab high-school graduates ([KAP](https://dayan.org/file-download/download/public/18837)).

**[Courts](/issues/courts).** The Supreme Court is the institution Arab respondents trust most (40%), yet 55.5% also say it "intervenes too much in decisions made by the government." The judicial overhaul was the deciding issue for 11.2% ([IDI, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)).

**[War and hostages](/issues/war-hostages).** In late 2023, 56% agreed with Mansour Abbas's statement that the October 7 attack did "not reflect Arab society, the Palestinian people, and the Islamic nation," and 33% disagreed ([IDI, December 2023](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/52016)). Trust in the IDF in 2025 was 28% among Muslims and 34% among Christians ([IDI](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). In November 2025, 74.6% said the war had damaged Arab-Jewish relations ([KAP](https://dayan.org/file-download/download/public/18732)).

**[West Bank](/issues/west-bank).** Asked for the most realistic solution, 46.4% chose two states on the 1967 lines, 21.4% said there is no solution and 18.7% chose one state (May 2026). In May 2023 only 17.2% chose two states, and 55.6% said there was no solution ([KAP](https://dayan.org/file-download/download/public/18837)). The question asks what is realistic, not what is preferred. See also [what is not on the ballot](/issues/not-on-ballot).

**[Religion and state](/issues/religion-state).** 80% of Arab respondents say the Jewish component of Israel's identity is too dominant, against 44% of Jews ([IDI, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). The 2018 Nation-State Law, passed 62–55 ([Reuters/AP via ABC News, July 19, 2018](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2018-07-19/israel-passes-controversial-jewish-nation-state-law/10014970)), declares: "The realization of the right to national self-determination in the State of Israel is exclusive to the Jewish People." It gives Arabic "a special status," where Arabic had been an official language before ([Knesset English text](https://main.knesset.gov.il/EN/activity/Documents/BasicLawsPDF/BasicLawNationState.pdf)).

**[Economy](/issues/economy).** Beyond the cost of living, 73.4% called their own economic situation fairly good in November 2025, the highest share KAP had recorded in the past year ([KAP](https://dayan.org/file-download/download/public/18732)).

**Joining a government.** 77.2% support an Arab party joining the next coalition: 43.3% any coalition, 33.9% only a center-left one ([KAP, May 2026](https://dayan.org/file-download/download/public/18837)). In IDI's survey, 86% of Arab respondents back Arab parties in government, against 27% of Jews ([IDI, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). Both figures include Druze.

## Who they are

Identity answers depend on the question. Asked for the single most important component of their identity, few choose "Palestinian":

Offered labels instead, most chose a Palestinian one in 2019. That is the newest survey found that asks the question this way:

<Chart id="palestinian-citizens.identity-label" />

A CBS social survey, reported in May 2025 without its year or wording, found 3% naming "Palestinian" as their primary identity ([JNS](https://www.jns.org/most-arab-israelis-do-not-consider-israeli-identity-important-cbs-finds/)). The three results ask different questions. They do not contradict each other. Asked whether a citizen who feels part of the Palestinian people can be loyal to Israel, 70% of Arab respondents said yes, against 16% of Jews ([IDI, May 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)).

In 2025, 54% of Arab respondents said they feel part of the state and its problems (Muslims 52.5%, Christians 56.5%), down from 66% in 2024. IDI calls the 2024 figure "unusually high" and says 2025 was still one of the highest since its tracking began in 2003. Seventy percent say it is better not to voice political opinions in front of strangers, against about half of Jews. Among Arabs, IDI says, the larger jump came about a decade ago ([IDI, 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). Half of Arab respondents under 55 agree that "it makes no difference who you vote for," against 37% of those 55 and over ([IDI, 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)). Among 18–34-year-olds, belief in Arab-Jewish political partnership rose from 51.6% to 59.3% between November 2025 and May 2026 ([KAP](https://dayan.org/file-download/download/public/18837)).
