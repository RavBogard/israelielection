# Surface brief: /match (Party match)

- **Job and audience:** American readers who want to know where they would sit in Israel's debate. Mode: Operate (a short task), then Read (the results). Spec: docs/planning/2026-10-08-match/SPEC.md.
- **Proof and truth:** every list's answer comes from Compare's matrix rows (`matrixRows`), the same shades, and the same record and unstated basis. Seats come from `listSeats` and blocs from `blocSeats`. The page recommends no list (Daniel, 2026-10-08).
- **Focal moment: "Your Knesset."** The results lead with the 120-seat grid redrawn from the polling average, each list's seats in its party colour at full strength when it is close to the reader, fading as the match falls. The 61 rule stays heavy. The caption gives the seats that lists matching 70% or more hold in the average, against 61. This is the one bold element, and it joins the match to the parliamentary arithmetic.
- **Quiz:** one question per screen. The options are a vertical stack of stance rows in Compare's ramp shades, in scale order, so the reader sees that they are picking a place along a debate. After a pick, the question's letters-strip shows which lists sit on each answer: the ballot letters of the 15 lists gathered under each option. That is the only motion the reader triggers. Importance is a three-option segmented control. The progress is 7 square marks, which is a real sequence.
- **Results order:**
  1. The seat grid.
  2. The ranked slips: the ballot-slip vocabulary laid out horizontally, with a match bar, a percentage and coverage.
  3. A per-list breakdown in a disclosure: for each question, a ramp track with the reader's mark and the list's mark.
  4. "If you were voting in Israel": facts on the top three lists, then the fixed explainer.
  5. Method in a fold.
- **States:**
  - Fewer than 4 answered: the results are disabled, and the button says why.
  - A list with too little on record is grouped below the ranked lists.
  - A deal-breaker clash carries a flag line on the slip.
  - A list shown as "below" the threshold keeps the dashed "out" edge on its slip.
- **Anti-goals:** no "you are a ..." persona, no confetti, no recommendation verbs, no cards of icons, no centred hero.
