## 1. What is at stake

### 1a. The map: Areas A, B and C

The Oslo II interim agreement (1995) split the West Bank into three zones. They were meant to last five years and are still in force.

| Area | Share of West Bank | Security control | Civil control |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (the Palestinian cities) | about 18% | Palestinian Authority | Palestinian Authority |
| B (towns and villages around them) | about 22% | Israel | Palestinian Authority |
| C (everything else, all settlements) | about 60% | Israel | Israel |

Source: B'Tselem, "What is Area C?" (page dated 2013–14; the shares have not changed) https://www.btselem.org/area_c/what_is_area_c. Between 200,000 and 300,000 Palestinians live in Area C (same page; the International Crisis Group gives the same range, https://www.crisisgroup.org/rpt/middle-east-north-africa/israelpalestine/252-sovereignty-all-name-israels-quickening-annexation-west-bank). The total West Bank population is "more than 3 million", about 85% Palestinian, per Israeli and Palestinian CBS figures for end-2023 as given by Peace Now https://peacenow.org.il/en/settlements-watch/settlements-data/population (summary).

Worth noticing: nearly every 2026 plan, from the right's "sovereignty" to B'Yachad's "Area C," concerns Area C.

### 1b. How many settlers

The figure depends on whether East Jerusalem is counted and whose register is used.

- **541,085** Jews in "Judea, Samaria and the Jordan Valley" on 1 Jan 2026, 5.32% of Israel's population, growing about twice as fast as Israel overall in 2025 (JNS, 2026-02-20, from population-registry data; https://www.jns.org/exclusive-judea-samaria-jewish-population-grew-at-twice-israels-overall-rate-in-2025/ (summary — I saw the headline figures but not the full table)).
- **540,327** in about 150 communities, January 2026, up 10,623 during 2025 (Yesha Council, via class doc 05 and Amit Segal's post https://x.com/AmitSegal/status/2027262144824201559; secondhand, not verified).
- **Over 750,000** settlers including East Jerusalem, across 147 settlements plus outposts; about 15% of the West Bank population including East Jerusalem (EU EEAS 2025 settlement report, published 2026-07-06, https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026/documents/2025%20Report%20on%20Israeli%20settlements%20in%20the%20occupied%20West%20Bank.pdf (summary)). For end-2024 the EU gave 503,732 West Bank + 233,600 East Jerusalem (class doc 05).
- Peace Now counts **146** official settlements excluding East Jerusalem as of 1 Aug 2026 (https://peacenow.org.il/en/settlements-watch/settlements-data/population).
- Who the settlers are: 36% Haredi, 36% religious-Zionist, 28% secular (Yesha Council via ToI, May 2023 — over three years old; class doc 05). The four largest places — Modi'in Illit, Beitar Illit, Ma'ale Adumim, Ariel — hold 42.6% of West Bank settlers; the first two are Haredi cities (class doc 05).

I could not open a 2025 or 2026 CBS table directly (see "Stale or missing").

With East Jerusalem the EU counts 63,311 units in 2025, against 28,872 in 2024 and 30,682 in 2023 (EEAS, from Peace Now and Ir Amim).

### 1d. E1

E1 is the open land between Ma'ale Adumim and Jerusalem. Building there would link Ma'ale Adumim to Jerusalem and, its opponents say, cut the northern West Bank off from the southern and separate East Jerusalem from both. US administrations held it frozen for about two decades.

- **August 20, 2025:** final planning approval for about 3,401 units. Smotrich: the plan "practically erases the two-state delusion"; "Every settlement… is another nail in the coffin of this dangerous idea" (ToI 2025-08-20, class doc 11b). NIS 3 billion committed to E1 infrastructure (Peace Now 2025 summary; EEAS dates the commitment to Sept 2025).
- **December 2025:** tenders published (EEAS).
- **August 18, 2026:** tender for 1,234 units in seven complexes opened; **bids close October 19, 2026** — eight days before the election (Peace Now, https://peacenow.org.il/en/e1-tender-published-and-open-for-bidding).
- **Court:** a petition is pending in Jerusalem District Court. The court refused to dismiss it on 2026-06-15 and ordered the state to respond by 2026-09-01 (same page).
- **September 28, 2026:** Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara reversed her own decision to push the tender deadline back by a month. Smotrich: "The tender must open and close, with the winner announced and construction beginning, on the date originally set for it." He said her aim had been to delay it until a new government could cancel it. Ma'ale Adumim mayor Guy Yifrach: "After more than 30 years of waiting, the time has come to build… E1 is moving forward." (Arutz Sheva, https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/433778 (summary)).
- Peace Now: "Building in E1 will ruin the possibility of reaching an end to the conflict and a peace arrangement based on two states."

Worth noticing: E1 is the one West Bank item with a deadline inside the campaign, and two anti-Netanyahu lists (B'Yachad, Yisrael Beiteinu) support building it.

### 1e. Knesset annexation ("sovereignty") votes, 2025–26

- **July 23, 2025 — non-binding motion, 71–13.** It calls the West Bank an "inseparable part of the Land of Israel" and urges the government to "apply Israeli sovereignty, law, judgment and administration" to the areas of Jewish settlement. For: Likud, Religious Zionism, the other coalition parties, the Haredi parties, and opposition party Yisrael Beiteinu. Against: the Arab parties and the Democrats. Yesh Atid and Blue and White did not vote. Democrats MK Gilad Kariv called it "a smokescreen for abandoning the hostages." The motion has no legal force. (ToI, https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-votes-71-13-for-non-binding-motion-calling-to-annex-west-bank/)
- **October 22, 2025 — two bills, passed during VP Vance's visit.** (1) "Application of Israeli Sovereignty in Judea and Samaria, 2025" passed **25–24**; the text applies Israeli law "to all areas of settlement in Judea and Samaria." Noam's Avi Maoz: "we are correcting a historical wrong that is long overdue." (2) A Ma'ale Adumim sovereignty bill passed **32–9**. (JPost, https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-871299; CBS News, https://www.cbsnews.com/news/israel-west-bank-annexation-bills-trump-objection-vance-visit/). Netanyahu told Likud MKs to abstain. Likud's statement: "another provocation by the opposition aimed at damaging our relations with the United States… sovereignty will be achieved not through a showy law for the record, but through proper work on the ground" (CBS). Trump, as quoted by CBS: "I will not allow Israel to annex the West Bank. It's not going to happen."
- **Reading stage:** CBS and JNS call these preliminary readings; JPost calls the 25–24 vote a "first reading" sent to the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee. These are two different stages (see "Source disagreements").
- **2026:** no further floor vote found before the Knesset dissolved on 17 July 2026 (class doc 02). Status of the bills after dissolution not confirmed.
- **July 2025:** all Likud ministers signed a letter urging Netanyahu to "apply sovereignty." No documented response from him (Ynet 2025-07-02, class doc 11a).

### 1f. Moving civil powers to Smotrich: "annexation without a law"

The biggest changes came through orders and cabinet decisions, not Knesset votes.

- **Feb 23, 2023:** under the coalition deal, a Defense Ministry memorandum gave West Bank civil affairs to an "additional minister" — Smotrich — while security stayed with the Defense Minister. He set up a Settlement Administration over land, planning, building, infrastructure and demolitions. The coalition agreement commits the government to "work toward the formulation and promotion of a policy whereby sovereignty is applied to Judea and Samaria." (International Crisis Group report 252, https://www.crisisgroup.org/rpt/middle-east-north-africa/israelpalestine/252-sovereignty-all-name-israels-quickening-annexation-west-bank (summary).) June 2023: PM/Defense Minister sign-off on each planning stage dropped (same).
- **May 29, 2024:** Central Command head Yehuda Fox delegated Civil Administration powers (land, planning, registration, regional councils) and a civilian, Hillel Roth, became deputy head. Smotrich, recorded June 9, 2024: "We created a separate civilian system… There is a minister. There is an administration which is like a government ministry." Lawyer Michael Sfard: it "transfers vast areas of administrative power from the military commander to Israeli civilians." (ToI, https://www.timesofisrael.com/drawing-annexation-claims-idf-hands-many-west-bank-powers-to-civilian-ally-of-smotrich/.) In the same recording: the system sits in the Defense Ministry "so they won't say we are doing annexation" (ToI, https://www.timesofisrael.com/expansion-of-west-bank-rule-appears-to-shift-creeping-annexation-into-higher-gear/).
- **Feb 8 and 16, 2026, security cabinet:** land registries published; Jordanian-era ban on non-Muslim land purchase repealed; enforcement extended into Areas A and B; Hebron permits moved to Israel's defense establishment; Area C land-registration process approved. Smotrich: "We are leading a de facto sovereignty revolution in Judea and Samaria." The Foreign Ministry called them "administrative measures." (ToI, same piece; class doc 11b.)
- Yashar and the Democrats would return the Civil Administration to the army (class doc 11c).

### 1h. The Palestinian Authority

- **Money:** Israel has transferred none of the clearance revenues it collects for the PA (about 60% of PA income) for about a year; NIS 740 million was withheld in April 2026 alone. Smotrich cites the PA's "actions against the State of Israel in the international arena." PA PM Mohammad Mustafa: "not a single shekel of tax revenues has been transferred to us." Public workers got NIS 2,000 toward delayed January pay. (ToI ~2026-04-27, https://www.timesofisrael.com/finance-ministry-confirms-it-hasnt-transferred-any-tax-revenues-to-pa-for-past-year/ (summary).)
- **Politics:** Abbas re-elected Fatah leader 2026-05-14; pledged elections, no date; last parliamentary vote 2006; Hussein al-Sheikh and Jibril Rajoub named as possible successors (Al Jazeera, https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/14/palestinian-president-abbas-pledges-elections-reform-at-fatah-conference).
- Yisrael Beiteinu wants the PA "dismantled"; RZ wants Oslo abolished (section 3).

### 1i. What "sovereignty" means in Israeli political language

- "Applying sovereignty" (hachalat ribonut) means extending Israeli law, courts and administration to West Bank land — what others call annexation. The right avoids "annexation" (sipuach) because it implies taking someone else's land. Speaker Ohana, July 2025 (paraphrased by ToI): in 1967 "the occupation did not begin; it ended."
- The scope varies: all of Judea and Samaria (RZ, Otzma, Amcha); "all areas of settlement" (the 2025 motion and bill); Area C (B'Yachad, Yisrael Beiteinu); Ma'ale Adumim or the Jordan Valley; "sovereignty through actions, not words" (Reservists; in substance Likud's Oct 2025 line).
- No formal plan gives annexed Palestinians citizenship; under 1% of Jewish annexation supporters favor it (JPPI 2026).
- "De facto sovereignty" (Smotrich, approvingly) and "de facto annexation" (Peace Now, Crisis Group, critically) name the same administrative changes in 1f.

## 4. How an American reader is likely to misread this

1. **"Settlements" are not one thing.** About a third of settlers are Haredi, mostly in Modi'in Illit and Beitar Illit, cities just over the Green Line chosen for cheap housing. Ma'ale Adumim and Ariel are suburbs. Ideological hilltop settlements are different again. Outposts and "farms" are illegal even under Israeli law, but are often legalized later. The EU and Peace Now count East Jerusalem neighborhoods as settlements; Israel and most Israelis do not. Violence comes from a subset: the IDF chief says perpetrators "do not represent the settlements," while Yesh Din documents security forces present at 16 of nearly 30 mass attacks.
2. **Area A/B/C.** Without the map, "sovereignty over Area C" (B'Yachad, Yisrael Beiteinu) sounds like "sovereignty over Judea and Samaria" (RZ, Otzma), and "separation" (Democrats, Yashar) sounds like "two states." They are different plans.
3. **The two-state question is mostly absent from the campaign.** The 2026 contest is about October 7, the draft, the courts and cost of living. The main challengers reject a Palestinian state as Likud does. Netanyahu's "Do you want a Palestinian state? Or… a Jewish state?" (JPost/Reuters 2026-09-29) attacks opponents who do not propose one. Even in 2020, only 3.5% named annexation a top-two priority (Geneva Initiative). No 2026 salience poll found.
4. **"Annexation" is mostly not happening by a vote.** The bills are symbolic or stalled. The change has come through planning approvals, the 2024 delegation order and the 2026 land decisions (1f).
5. **"Left" and "right" don't map.** Anti-Netanyahu parties back E1 and Area C sovereignty; Yisrael Beiteinu voted for the 2025 motion. Pro-Netanyahu Haredi parties call the issue "not central."
6. **Terms are positions.** "Judea and Samaria" / "West Bank" / "occupied West Bank"; "sovereignty" / "annexation"; "farms" / "outposts"; "nationalist crime" / "Jewish terror" / "settler violence."

## Source disagreements

- **Settler population:** 541,085 (JNS, registry, 1 Jan 2026) vs 540,327 (Yesha) vs EU "over 750,000" (includes East Jerusalem); EU end-2024 West Bank only 503,732. Settlement counts: Peace Now 146, EU 147, Yesha "about 150."
