## 1. Size, growth, geography

### How big the group is
- **CBS, September 2026 (5787 New Year release):** of Jews aged 20+, secular 39.6%, traditional-not-so-religious 21.8%, traditional-religious 14.0%, religious 12.4%, Haredi 11.5%. (Ynet 2026-09-09, https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/sjc9lpcufl — opened; the article does not state the age range; class memo 06 gives "Jews 20+" from the same release via VIN News.)
- **CBS, September 2025:** secular 42.7%. (via class memo 06, JPost article-868315; verified: false.)
- **CBS 2009:** secular 41.4%. (via class memo 05, CBS tec25; verified: false.)
- **IDI Israeli Democracy Index 2025** (fieldwork May 2025, 1,205 Jews and others): secular 43.6% of Jews 18+. IDI weights its sample to CBS, so it is not an independent estimate. (Appendix 3, p. 322, https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf — opened.)
- **Pew:** Hiloni 49% (2016 report, fieldwork 2014–15); 45% (2025 report, 2024 fieldwork). Pew's 2025 switching study: Hilonim keep 93% of those raised Hiloni. (via class memos 02 and 05; verified: false for the 2025 figures.)
- **Taub Center** (2024 Social Survey sample, cited in Dec 2025 demography report): "secular" 61.7% of the Jewish sample. This category evidently lumps secular with non-religious traditional and is not comparable. (https://www.taubcenter.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Demography-2025-ENG-7.pdf — opened, p. 16.)

What is worth noticing: by every measure the secular are the largest single Jewish group (about 4 in 10 Jewish adults), but not a majority, and the CBS series dropped 3.1 points in one year (2025 → 2026). One-year moves that large in a survey series usually reflect sampling or method, not real change. The long-run picture (41.4 in 2009, 39.6 in 2026) is flat to slightly down as a share of adults, while the Haredi share rises. As a share of the whole population (Arabs and children included) the secular are roughly 29% [arith, memo 05: 39.6% × Jews ~73.6% of residents].

### Growth and fertility
- Taub Center (Dec 2025, Figure 4, using Hleihel 2025 CBS-based data): total fertility of secular/traditional-non-religious women about 1.8 in 2014, about 1.6 in 2023; religious about 3.0 → 2.7; Haredi about 6.0 → 5.5. "Haredi Jews had around four more children than their non-religious counterparts in both 2014 and 2023." Projected completed fertility for secular women: about 1.6 children for cohorts reaching 40–44 in the mid-to-late 2030s. Values read from a chart, so approximate. (https://www.taubcenter.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Demography-2025-ENG-7.pdf — opened.)
- First-grade intake 2025–26 (Education Ministry via ToI 2025-08-31): state-secular stream 66,185 of 179,501 first-graders, 36.9% [arith]; in 2000 secular-stream first-graders were 51,680. The ToI headline: religious first-graders (state-religious 29,470 + Haredi 42,751 = 72,221 [arith]) outnumbered secular-stream ones for the first time. (via class memo 02, https://www.timesofisrael.com/for-the-1st-time-religious-1st-graders-in-israel-outnumber-secular-ones-by-thousands/; verified: false.) Note: the state-secular stream includes many traditional families, so this overstates secular children.
- Emigration is the other half of growth: see section 4.

### Where they live
- No CBS table of religiosity by district or city was found that we could open (CBS site blocked automated access). The IDI 2025 Jewish sample by district: Center 29.2%, Tel Aviv 21.7%, South 14.1%, Haifa 10.6%, Jerusalem 10.2%, North 8.9%, Judea and Samaria 5.3% — but not cross-tabbed with religiosity. (DI 2025, p. 322 — opened.)
- Proxy used on this page: localities with the most secular voting profile. The Israel Hofsheet "liberal cities" index (via JPost 2025-12-10, memo 05; verified: false) ranks Hod HaSharon, Givatayim, Tel Aviv and Ramat HaSharon at the top.
- Kibbutzim of the (secular) Kibbutz Movement: 241 localities, population 173,911 in 2022, of whom 166,627 Jews [arith from CBS localities file `bycode2022.xlsx`, settlement type 330 = kibbutz, organizational affiliation code 15 = Kibbutz Movement; religious kibbutzim (code 3) excluded; https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/bycode2022.xlsx — downloaded]. That is under 2% of Israel's population.
- Populations 2022 (same CBS file): Tel Aviv-Yafo 474,530 (22,863 Arabs); Haifa 290,306 (35,170 Arabs); Herzliya 106,741; Kfar Saba 101,556; Ra'anana 80,260; Hod HaSharon 65,614; Givatayim 61,924; Ramat HaSharon 48,181.

### Education and income
- Pew (fieldwork 2014–15): "Fully 45% of Hiloni adults hold a college degree," versus 23% of Masortim, 22% of Datiim, 13% of Haredim; 15% of Hilonim did not finish high school versus 49% of Haredim. (https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/education-values-and-science/ — opened.) Over 10 years old.
- No current income-by-religiosity figure for secular Jews was found. Nearest available: IDI Haredi yearbook 2025 compares Haredim with all non-Haredi Jews: gross monthly household income 2022 NIS 14,816 vs NIS 24,466; poverty 2023 33% vs 14%. (https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf — opened.) This is not a secular figure.
- Israel Tax Authority study (via ToI 2026-08-06): emigrants' average income rose from about NIS 125,000 (2015–19) to about NIS 200,000 (2024), roughly 50% above the population average. (https://www.timesofisrael.com/rising-exodus-of-top-taxpayers-to-cost-israel-billions-in-lost-revenue-study/ — opened.) Relevant because the leavers are disproportionately secular (section 4).

## 2. Voting pattern 2019–2022

### Survey-based (who secular voters chose)
IDI, Ariel Finkelstein, "Voting Patterns of Jews in the 2022 Elections by Religious Self-Definition," 2026-06-28; 20 Viterbi Center surveys Nov 2022–Oct 2023, 12,322 Jewish respondents, 10,694 reporting a vote. (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/64807 — opened.)
- Secular vote 2022: Yesh Atid 39%, Likud 20%, Labor + Meretz 16%, National Unity 13%. "74%" of secular voters chose opposition parties.
- 2021 comparison: Yesh Atid 31%, Likud 17%, Labor + Meretz 19%; "National Unity" (Blue and White + New Hope combined) 18%.
- Composition: 78.5% of Yesh Atid's voters are secular; National Unity 54%; Likud 32%; RZ–Otzma 14%.
- Not published in the article: secular shares for Yisrael Beiteinu, RZ–Otzma, Shas, Arab parties; 2019–2020 figures.

Tel Aviv's low figure is partly a registry effect (many registered residents live abroad or elsewhere), not necessarily low engagement — not verified.

What the shift looks like across five elections:
1. 2019–2020: these places voted Blue and White by 45–60%. The secular center consolidated behind Gantz–Lapid.
2. 2021: Blue and White split; the vote scattered across Yesh Atid, Labor, Meretz, Blue and White and New Hope. Labor led in the kibbutzim.
3. 2022: re-consolidation behind Yesh Atid (33–42%), with National Unity second or third. Left lists shrank.
4. Throughout: Likud holds a steady 15–23% in secular cities (6–8% on kibbutzim). Herzliya, Kfar Saba and Ra'anana lean further right than Tel Aviv/Givatayim/Ramat HaSharon; Ra'anana has a sizable religious and Anglo population (RZ–Otzma 9.9% in 2022).
5. Haifa is a weaker proxy: Arab and Russian-speaking neighborhoods pull it toward Yisrael Beiteinu (8.1%) and Hadash–Ta'al (5.7%).

Method and limits: Localities are a proxy for a group, not the group. Tel Aviv-Yafo includes Jaffa (Arab) and south Tel Aviv; every city includes traditional and religious voters. Soldiers', diplomats' and other double-envelope votes are not assigned to localities. Kibbutz list = CBS settlement type 330 with Kibbutz Movement affiliation (code 15), 238 matched in each election file. Ballot-letter-to-party mapping per election is in the script.

### 2026 polling by this group
- Not found: no published 2026 poll breaks vote intention down by religiosity (searched IDI, which publishes toplines only in its data files; IDI's 2024 voting-trends review has no religiosity split, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/58374 — opened).
- Indirect evidence: IDI Democracy Index 2025: political self-placement of secular Jews: Left 23.8%, Center 37.7%, Right 37.2% (p. 322 — opened). IDI: "secular Jews in Israel are evenly divided, for the most part, among the three political camps" (p. 28).
- The 2022 secular vote's main vehicles have changed: Yesh Atid now runs inside B'Yachad (Bennett-led); National Unity's voters are courted by Yashar (Eisenkot); Labor and Meretz run as the Democrats. Polls (late Sept–Oct 1, 2026): Yashar 21–23 seats, B'Yachad 7–13, Democrats 8–11 (memo 11c). Yashar's stated targets are "Route 4" commuter suburbs — Petah Tikva, Kiryat Ono, Holon — not the most secular cities (memo 11c, ToI 2026-07-01).

## 3. Attitudes on the six issues
All IDI unless noted. "Secular" = self-defined secular Jews.

**Haredi draft.** Sept 14–18, 2025 (n = 1,000; 800 Hebrew-language): "Draft all young Haredi men, with the exception of a small number of outstanding yeshiva students": secular 71%, traditional non-religious 49%, traditional religious 31.5%, national religious 30.5%, Haredi 8%. "Draft those… enrolled at 'dropout yeshivot'": secular 23.5%. "Leave things as they are": secular 4%, Haredi 68%. (Graph 13, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61845 — opened, values read from the chart labels.) The draft is the one issue on which secular opinion is close to unanimous.

**Courts and the judicial overhaul.**
- Trust in the Supreme Court: secular 66.5% (2023), 58.5% (2024), 66% (2025); Haredi 11 → 7 → 3; national religious 16 → 15 → 19. (DI 2025 Table 2.6, p. 65.)
- "Decisions that are opposed to basic democratic values… are not democratic, even if they are passed by… a Knesset majority": secular 72.5%; Haredi 26.5%. On the secular right, 57% take this view versus 36% of the right overall. (DI 2025 Table 3.8, p. 149.)
- Constitution "quite or very important": secular 80%. (DI 2025 Table 3.9.)
- Jan 2023, rated the overhaul "very good" or "pretty good": secular 16%, traditional non-religious 22%, traditional religious 48%, Orthodox and Haredi 66%. (Itamar Yakir, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/49867 — opened.)
- Trust profile (DI 2025 Table 2.1): secular trust IDF 87%, Shin Bet 75%, Supreme Court 66%, Attorney General 65%, government 9%, Knesset 8%, parties 5%. Haredim: government 45%, Supreme Court 3%.

**War and hostages.** No religiosity breakdown found for the hostage-deal or end-the-war questions; IDI reports these by political camp. Sept 2025: hostages should be the main war goal: Left 94%, Center 80%, Right 48%; "time has come to end the war": Left 94.5%, Center 75%, Right 48.5% (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61845). Since secular Jews split roughly into thirds across the camps, the secular figure is likely near the Center figure — inference, not data. Aug–Sept 2026: October 7 is "a central factor" in the vote for about three-quarters or more of secular and traditional-non-religious Jews vs about a quarter of Haredim (n = 605 Jews; https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177 — opened; exact secular figure not printed in the text).

**West Bank / settlement.**
- Security forces treat violent settlers "too leniently": secular 66.5%, traditional non-religious 43%, national religious 20.5%, Haredi 12.5% (Nov 30–Dec 3, 2025, n = 604 Jews; https://en.idi.org.il/articles/62468 — opened).
- Support Jewish settlement in the Gaza Strip: secular 21% (Nov 2024) and 21% (Aug 2025); Haredi 64 → 75 (Graph 6, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61601 — opened, chart labels).
- Palestinian right to a state: Jews by camp only (Sept 2025): Left 57%, Center 24%, Right 8% (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61845). No religiosity split.
- Related, Jewish–Arab: support bringing Arab parties into government: secular 44%, traditional non-religious 20%, Haredi 5% (DI 2025 p. 215); government should encourage Arab emigration: secular 34.5% — the only group where it is a minority (DI 2025 p. 208–209).

**Religion and state.**
- Most acute tension is religious–secular: secular 20% (2024), 23% (2025); secular rank it second after right–left (DI 2025 Table 4.9).
- Practice: 47% of secular Jews fast on Yom Kippur (28% fully, 19% partly); 20% attend a service, 7% a non-Orthodox one (IDI, July–Aug 2026, n = 1,002 Jews; https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66240 — opened). Secular is not the same as non-observant.
- Not found with a religiosity split from 2024–26: civil marriage, Shabbat public transport. (Check issue-religion-state research.)

**Economy and cost of living.** No secular-specific economic attitude found. DI 2025: the most important vote factors for Jews overall are security (20%), religion and state (19%), economy/cost of living (19%) (p. 225–226); not split by religiosity. IDI "Stay or Go" (April 2025): cost of living and "the lack of a good future for their children" are the dominant reasons for considering leaving (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/62287 — opened).

**Mood.** Israel's overall situation "good or very good": secular 9%, Haredi 42% (DI 2025 Table 1.1). Optimistic about Israel's future: secular 35% (2024), 38.5% (2025), lowest of all groups (Table 1.16).

## 4. Lived texture

**The 2023 protest movement.** Weekly Saturday-night rallies on Kaplan Street, Tel Aviv, through 2023: estimates of 60,000–150,000 in February, 260,000+ on March 18 (ToI), ~200,000 at the Knesset April 27 (JPost/JTA) — as compiled on Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israeli_judicial_reform_protests (opened; underlying press reports not individually opened, verified: false). No survey of protester demographics was found that we could open. In 2026 the protests are much smaller: about 300 at Habima on Aug 1, 2026 (https://www.timesofisrael.com/at-tel-aviv-rally-daughter-of-couple-slain-on-oct-7-says-government-is-defiling-our-dead/ — opened); the High Court capped a wartime rally at 1,000 in Tel Aviv in April 2026 (ToI topic page). "Kaplan" has become shorthand for the group: in Sept 2026 Heritage Minister Amichay Eliyahu said, "I don't want to draft someone from Kaplan to enlist in combat [duty], and in the end they won't storm into battle." (https://www.timesofisrael.com/far-right-minister-panned-for-opposing-idf-combat-duty-for-anti-government-protesters/ — opened.)

**Reservist identity.** The secular self-image as the group that serves meets a mixed record in the data:
- Oct–Dec 2023: 11.2% of secular men 21–44 and 11.2% of traditional men served in an average month; religious men 15.2%. 2025: religious 4.3%, traditional 2.6%, secular 1.7%, Haredi 0.3%. "Secular men were still the largest group of male reservists." (Finkelstein & Portal, IDI 2026-08-20, CBS Labor Force Survey, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65427 — opened.)
- Performed reserve duty during the war (Jews 18–54, DI 2025): secular 18.4%, traditional non-religious 26.4%, traditional religious 18.3%, national religious 16.0%, Haredi 3.3%; never served in the IDF: secular 13.5%, Haredi 69.5% (DI 2025 p. 323).

**The relocation debate.**
- IDI April 2025 (720 Jews): "a particularly large share of secular Jews (39%) are considering leaving"; traditional non-religious 24%, traditional religious 19%, national religious 14%, Haredim 3%. Left "over 40%," Center 35%, Right 19%, "secular Jews on the right – 34%." (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/62287 — opened.) ToI: "Among younger secular Jewish Israelis, 60% said they would consider leaving… among those with a high income and a foreign passport, the figure jumped to 80%." (https://www.timesofisrael.com/some-1-in-4-israelis-considering-leaving-the-country-poll-finds/ — opened.)
- DI 2025 hypothetical (if offered citizenship in the US or another Western country): prefer to remain in Israel: secular 56%, traditional non-religious 79%, Haredi 92% (DI 2025 p. 51).
- Actual departures (CBS via memo 06): 55,300–59,400 left in 2023, ~82,700 in 2024, ~69,500 in 2025; no CBS religiosity breakdown of leavers exists.

**Voices (pages opened; wording exact).**
- Ruby Chen, father of Staff Sgt. Itay Chen, answering Eliyahu: "My son, hero of Israel, protested at Kaplan for a better country, and charged with his tank into the hellscape and fell in battle." (ToI, Sept 16, 2026, URL above.)
- Izhar Shay, father of Staff Sgt. Yaron Shay: his son "both demonstrated at Kaplan, and didn't hesitate to storm dozens of Nukhba terrorists." (same article; partial quote as printed.)

But it's no longer possible to be part of what's happening here." (ToI, Deborah Danan, 2026-08-14, https://www.timesofisrael.com/driven-by-prolonged-conflict-and-ideological-shift-more-israelis-seek-quiet-life-abroad/ — opened; ToI says he told "the broadcaster," so the original interview is secondhand.)
- Shir Matias, daughter of Deborah and Shlomi Matias of Kibbutz Holit, at Habima: "The government defiles our dead, ignores our suffering and dodges establishing a state commission of inquiry." (ToI 2026-08-02, URL above.)

## 5. The problem with "Ashkenazi" (framing note for Daniel)
- The CBS records origin by the person's or father's country of birth. Once a person's father is Israel-born, the CBS has no origin to record. The newest CBS continent-of-origin breakdown seen is for 2015, when the Israel-born-of-Israel-born-father group was 44.1% of Jews (memo 05; verified: false). CBS April 2026: ~81% of Jews are Israel-born (memo 06, Ynet 2026-04-19; verified: false). So official statistics can no longer say how many Israelis are "Ashkenazi."
- Self-identification fills the gap: IDI 2025: Ashkenazi 39.8%, Mizrahi 35.2%, mixed 15.3%, FSU immigrant 5.3%, Ethiopian 0.6% (DI 2025 p. 322 — opened). Pew 2016: Ashkenazi 45%, Sephardi/Mizrahi 48%; "Fully two-thirds of Ashkenazim identify as secular (Hiloni) Jews, compared with about three-in-ten of Sephardim/Mizrahim (32%)." (https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/identity/ — opened.) From Pew's numbers, roughly 60% of Hilonim were Ashkenazi in 2014–15 [arith, rough] — meaning about 4 in 10 were not.
- A sixth of Jewish adults now call themselves "mixed." Many Russian-speakers are secular and Ashkenazi by descent but vote and live as a separate community.
- "Ashkenazi" in the title therefore names a stereotype and a political argument ("First Israel," the elite) as much as a demographic. Using it is a framing choice; see the brief.

## Source disagreements
1. Secular share: CBS 39.6% (2026) vs CBS 42.7% (2025) vs IDI 43.6% (2025, weighted to CBS) vs Pew 45% (2024). Different questions, ages and years; CBS's one-year drop of 3.1 points is unexplained.
2. Secular fertility: Taub chart ~1.6 (2023, secular plus traditional-non-religious combined); memo 05 cites Taub "~1.7" for secular women. Same report, different measure (period TFR vs completed fertility); use 1.6 with the label.
3. Emigration consideration, Haredim: IDI 3% vs ToI's retelling 4%. ToI also rephrased the IDI result as shares "among those weighing emigration," which reads as a composition, not a rate. The IDI page says it is a rate ("a particularly large share of secular Jews (39%) are considering leaving"). Use IDI.
4. Reserve service: IDI CBS-based labor-force data (2025) puts secular men's monthly rate below religious and traditional men; DI 2025 self-report puts secular "performed reserve duty during the war" (18.4%) below traditional non-religious (26.4%) and near national religious (16.0%). The common claim that secular Israelis carried the reserve burden is not supported as a rate; it is supported as a headcount ("still the largest group of male reservists").
5. Protest crowd sizes: estimates vary by outlet (e.g. March 11, 2023: 150,000–240,000).
