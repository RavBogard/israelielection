# Brief: secular ("Tel Aviv") Israel

## Findings
1. **About 4 in 10 Jewish adults are secular** (CBS 2026: 39.6%; IDI 2025: 43.6%), about 29% of all residents (our estimate: the Jews-20+ share applied to the whole population, not a published figure). Fertility ~1.6 vs ~5.5 for Haredim (Taub, 2023, read from a chart; Taub's 1.6 covers secular and traditional non-religious women together. The religious-Zionist page uses JPPI's 2.0 for secular women alone — different source and category, so the two figures are not in conflict).
2. **Split across camps, but voted opposition.** Secular self-placement: Left 24, Center 38, Right 37 (IDI 2025). 74% voted for the opposition in 2022 (IDI).
3. **One main vehicle each cycle.** In the most secular places Blue and White took 43–60% in 2019–20, the vote scattered in 2021, Yesh Atid took 33–42% in 2022 (CEC, our calculation). Likud steady at 15–23% there.
4. **Draft: near-unanimous.** 71% want all young Haredi men drafted, except a few outstanding yeshiva students; 4% want no change (IDI Sept 2025).
5. **Courts: the widest gap.** Trust in Supreme Court: secular 66%, Haredi 3% (IDI 2025).
6. **Most likely to leave.** 39% considering it (IDI Apr 2025); 60% of younger secular Jews.
7. **Reservists: headcount yes, rate no.** Still the largest group of male reservists, but in 2025 served at 1.7% a month vs religious 4.3%, traditional 2.6% (IDI, CBS data).

## Framing choices
1. Title "Secular Ashkenazi / 'Tel Aviv' Israel" **or** "Secular Israel (hilonim)"? CBS can't count Ashkenazim past the second generation; ~4 in 10 Hilonim weren't Ashkenazi even in 2014–15 (Pew, unverified); 15% of Jews say "mixed."
2. Keep "Tel Aviv" **or** drop it? Tel Aviv-Yafo gave the Netanyahu bloc a quarter of its vote.
