# American lens — brief for ruling

## Findings that carry the page
1. "Pro-Israel" is an American category: AIPAC, J Street and JVP all aim at U.S. policy.
2. Israel's left-right axis is the conflict, not the economy (CHES-Israel expert survey, 2021–22). Shas and UTJ are hawkish and economically left.
3. Class runs the other way from the American stereotype. 2021: 46% of Likud voters below-average income; 46% of Yesh Atid voters above-average (IDI).
4. The two-state gap opened because Israelis moved. Same Pew wording, different Pew surveys: U.S. Jews 61% (2013 U.S. Jewish survey) → 63% (2019–20 survey); Israeli Jews 43% (2014–15 Israel religion survey) → 16% (2025, 2026 Israel surveys).
5. Vote considerations, Jews: security 39%, economy 38%, draft 27%; Arabs: crime 53.5% (IDI, Sept 2026).
6. The center is hawkish: 29% of centrists think coexistence possible (Pew 2025; unverified, not in the page text); top issue cost of living (IDI 2026).
7. U.S. Jews 64% favorable to Israel, white evangelicals 65%; 56% of U.S. Jews little/no confidence in Netanyahu (Pew, Mar 2026).
8. The gap now covers antisemitism (chart C).
