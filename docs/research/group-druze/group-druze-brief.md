# Druze — brief for ruling

**Findings**
1. About 150,000 Druze at end 2022, 1.6% of Israelis (CBS 2023 release); about 154,000 and 1.5% at end 2024 (CBS 2026 via Hebrew Wikipedia, unverified). Fertility is 1.66 (same secondhand source, unverified; CBS's 2023 release gave 2.00 for 2021), below the Jewish rate. Most live in Carmel and Galilee villages. Another 25,000–29,000 live in four Golan villages, mostly as residents, not citizens.
2. Golan citizenship surged after Majdal Shams (2024) and the fall of Assad: 3,750 applications in 2025, against about 400–600 a year before (ToI: 438 in 2022, 406 in 2023). About 40% now hold citizenship, up from about 20% in 2022 (ToI).
3. About 90% of the Druze vote goes to Zionist parties, mostly opposition ones. In 2022: National Unity 30, Yisrael Beiteinu 18, Meretz 15, Likud 12. The Netanyahu bloc got about 13%.
4. The vote follows Druze candidates' home villages: Beit Jann gave Meretz 62%, Daliyat gave National Unity 55%. Turnout fell from 77% (1996) to 51% (2022).
5. One Druze MK was elected in 2022; there are three now (ToI, May 2026; not re-opened). 2026 slots: Amar (YB no. 7, unverified), Abu Rokon (Yashar no. 13, unverified), Mari (Blue and White no. 7, unverified), Salalha (Democrats no. 18). No Druze in Likud's top 40.
6. In IDI 2025, Druze are the most pro-state Arab group: trust in the IDF 84%, optimism 81%. They also have the highest share saying the Court intervenes too much (71%). Subsample about 31 people (8.4% of 364 Arab respondents).
7. 2018: about 90,000 rallied against the Nation-State Law.
