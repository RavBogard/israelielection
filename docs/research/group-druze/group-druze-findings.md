# Group page: the Druze — research record

Slug: group-druze. Compiled 2026-10-05. Every figure carries its source inline. "Computed" means I calculated it myself from the Central Elections Committee (CEC) locality files; the method is given in section 2. `verified: false` means I could not open the primary page and took the figure from a secondary source.

Builds on class doc 05 (Druze 153.0k for 2024 via Wikipedia; Golan Druze 29,364, about 20% naturalized by mid-2022; about 90% of the Druze vote to Zionist parties, from IDI 2022) and class doc 11d (Druze votes for the Joint List: 11.6% in 2015, about 2.9% in April 2019, 5.6% in September 2019, from Dayan/Brake). What changes here: the Golan citizenship figure is now out of date (it rose sharply in 2025); the Druze share of the Joint List vote in April 2019 should be about 3.7%, not 2.9% (that list did not run in April 2019, so the comparable figure is Hadash–Ta'al plus Ra'am–Balad); and there is a newer CBS release (2026) that I could not open directly.

## 1. Size, growth, geography

**Population.**
- CBS: about **150,000 Druze at the end of 2022** (preliminary). That is 1.6% of Israel's population and 7.4% of the Arab population. In 2021, about 81% lived in the Northern District and 19% in the Haifa District. Median age 29.6 (2021). Growth 1.2% (2021). Total fertility 2.00 (2021), down from a peak of 7.92 in 1964. Source: CBS media release 129/2023 (2023-04-23), copy hosted by the Jewish Virtual Library, https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Society_&_Culture/druze-stats2023.pdf
- CBS via TPS (2024-04-17): 152,000 (dated 2022 in the article). 23.8% are aged 0–14. 43.5% of high-school graduates start a bachelor's degree within 8 years. 83.8% of households have an employed member. https://tps.co.il/articles/druze-community-has-grown-tenfold-since-israels-founding/
- CBS 2026 release (`verified: false`; the CBS site refused our connection, so these figures come from Hebrew Wikipedia's citation of it): **about 154,000 at the end of 2024** (one line gives 154,369), 1.5% of the population and 7.2% of Arab citizens. About 125,400 of them are citizens. Growth 1.0% in 2024, compared with Muslims 1.8% and Jews 1.0%. Total fertility **1.66 in 2024**, down from 4.1 (1990), 3.1 (2000), 2.5 (2010) and 2.1 (2017). 84.3% of households have an employed member (2024). 6,200 Druze students in higher education (2023/24). 43.2% start a bachelor's degree within 8 years (2025), against 34.4% for the Arab school system as a whole. https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/דרוזים_בישראל, citing https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/Pages/2026/האוכלוסייה-הדרוזית-בישראל-לקט-נתונים-לרגל-חג-הנביא-שועייב-2026.aspx
  - Worth noticing: Druze fertility is now below the Jewish rate. Growth has slowed to the Jewish rate.

**Localities.** Largest Druze populations in 2021 (CBS 2023 release): Daliyat al-Karmel 17,500; Yarka 17,300; Maghar 13,500. The Carmel villages are Daliyat al-Karmel and Isfiya (Haifa District). The Galilee villages include Yarka, Julis (seat of the spiritual leadership), Beit Jann, Hurfeish, Kisra-Sumei, Yanuh-Jat, Sajur, Ein al-Asad and Peki'in, plus the mixed towns of Maghar, Abu Snan, Rameh and Shefa-Amr. CBS shares of Druze in mixed towns, from English Wikipedia's table of 2017 CBS figures (`verified: false`): Isfiya 76%, Peki'in 78%, Abu Snan 30%, Rameh 31%, Shefa-Amr 13.9%. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_in_Israel

**Golan Druze and citizenship.** Four villages: Majdal Shams, Mas'ade, Buq'ata, Ein Qiniyye. The Golan was annexed by Israeli law in 1981. Most residents hold permanent residency, not citizenship. Only citizens can vote for the Knesset.
- Mid-2022: about 4,300 citizens out of about 21,000 Golan Druze. Citizenship requests approved: 239 in 2021 and 206 in the first half of 2022, up from 4 in 2010. Data from the Population and Immigration Authority, obtained through a freedom-of-information request. Source: Shomrim, https://www.shomrim.news/eng/druze-gloan
- End of 2024: about 6,000 citizens out of 29,000+ (20.45%). Applications: 438 in 2022 (419 approved), 406 in 2023 (389), 352 in the first 11 months of 2024 (318). Source: Times of Israel, 2025-01-06, https://www.timesofisrael.com/taboo-no-more-one-in-five-golan-druze-now-holds-israeli-citizenship/
- 2025–26: **3,750 applications in 2025**, against "500–600" a year before that. 6,779 applications in total from 2020 to 2026. Nearly 1,000 people naturalized in January–February 2026. About 38% now hold citizenship in Majdal Shams and about 37–43% in the other three villages. Source: Times of Israel, Tal Schneider, 2026-03-15, https://www.timesofisrael.com/after-years-of-hesitation-more-druze-of-the-golan-heights-seek-israeli-citizenship/
- Eligible voters registered in the four villages (computed from CEC files): 1,780 (April 2019), 2,098 (2021), **2,755 (2022)**. Turnout among them was 17–23%. The 2026 roll will be much larger because of the 2025 surge. That is an open question for election night.

**Conscription.** Druze men have been conscripted by law since 1956 (English Wikipedia, citing Zeedan 2019, https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4698/9/1/1). Druze women and religious men are exempt (Hebrew Wikipedia). Druze conscription was resisted in 1954–56. Sheikh Amin Tarif opposed it at the time (Wikipedia, citing Pappé 2006). The Druze Herev battalion was closed in 2015, and Druze soldiers now serve throughout the IDF.
- Service rate: "over 80 percent of Druze men enlisting" (Georgetown Journal of International Affairs, Eve Kheir, 2025-04-21, https://gjia.georgetown.edu/conflict-security/the-druze-community-in-israel-a-model-of-minority-integration/). I found no primary IDF figure. Pew (fieldwork Oct 2014–May 2015): about six in ten Druze men surveyed had served (45%) or were serving (15%). That is a different measure, since it covers men of all ages (https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2016/03/21/5-facts-about-israeli-druze-a-unique-religious-and-ethnic-group/).
- Career soldiers: Druze are about 3% of career soldiers while being about 2% of the population (Times of Israel, 2026-05-05; `verified: false` as to the IDF source behind it).

**Casualties in the current war.** I did not find a sourced total of Druze soldiers killed since October 7, 2023 (see "Stale or missing"). Named cases on pages I opened: Lt. Col. Alim Abdallah of Yanuh-Jat, deputy commander of the 300th Brigade, killed on the Lebanon border on 2023-10-09 (Wikipedia, citing Times of Israel); and Lt. Col. Salman Habaka, named by Georgetown/Kheir as one of the community's "heavy losses."

**Socioeconomic indicators.** CBS 2023 release (data for 2022): employment rate 50.8% (Muslims 42.6%); labor-force participation 64.2% for men and 40.2% for women; 81.2% of households have an employed member; 39.7% of adults hold a matriculation certificate and 14.8% an academic degree. IDI's 2024 Arab Society report says only that matriculation rates are "particularly high in Druze schools" and does not break out other figures (https://en.idi.org.il/arab-society/2024/?chapter=65056). IDI 2016 found the highest average income of the three Arab religious groups among Druze respondents. I could not obtain the CBS socioeconomic cluster rankings of Druze localities.

## 2. Voting pattern 2019–2022

**Method (computed).** Source files: the CEC locality-results CSVs for the 21st–25th Knesset (https://media21.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv through https://media25.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv). I added up the ten localities that the Moshe Dayan Center study treats as 95%+ Druze: Daliyat al-Karmel, Yarka, Beit Jann, Hurfeish, Kisra-Sumei, Yanuh-Jat, Peki'in, Julis, Sajur and Ein al-Asad (Shanan and Ilat, 2021-03-18, https://dayan.org/he/content/5714). Party letters were matched to lists using each election's CEC national-results page. Checks: my non-Zionist shares match Dayan's published figures (April 2019 3.7% vs 3.75%; 2020 11.5% vs 11.5%), and my 2022 shares are within about one point of IDI's. This method leaves out Druze in mixed towns and cities, and the Golan villages, which I report separately.

Longer turnout trend (Dayan Center): 76.8% in 1996, 78.9% in 1999, 64.2% in 2003, 64.9% in 2006, 53.2% in September 2019 (57.1% in 2020, per Hebrew Wikipedia's summary). Non-Zionist share: 9.58% (1996), 17% (2013), 15% (2015), 3.75% (April 2019), 11.5% (2020).

IDI (Rudnitzky, 2022-12-25, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/47986): in 2022, about 90% of the Druze vote went to Zionist parties: National Unity 30.4%, Yisrael Beiteinu 18.3%, Meretz 14%, Likud 10.6%. Turnout averaged 48.7% across 2021–22, against 55.9% across 2013–2020. IDI called Druze representation "an all-time low," with one Druze MK elected, and described "political marginalization and deepening crisis of orientation."

**What the numbers show.**
1. The Druze vote is overwhelmingly for Zionist parties, but it is not a right-wing bloc vote. In 2022, Netanyahu's bloc (Likud, Religious Zionism, Shas, UTJ) took about 13% in the core localities. Opposition-side Zionist parties took about 75%.
2. The vote follows the hometowns of Druze candidates. In 2022, Beit Jann gave Meretz 61.6%; Meretz's no. 4 candidate, Ali Salalha, is from Beit Jann. Daliyat al-Karmel gave National Unity 54.9%; Akram Hasson, no. 16, lives there. Sajur gave Jewish Home 47.1%. Yarka gave Likud 34.4%. The Dayan study describes the same pattern back to 1996: "when nearly every party positioned a Druze candidate in a realistic spot, it captured the most support in his village."
3. Turnout is falling and is now below the national rate. In 2021 and 2022 it was close to the Arab-sector rate.
4. Isfiya and the mixed towns vote differently. In Isfiya, which has a Christian minority, the Joint List took 30.6% in 2020 and Balad 11.2% in 2022. In Maghar, Abu Snan and Rameh, the leading lists in 2022 were Ra'am or Hadash–Ta'al. These towns' figures cannot be read as Druze votes.
5. The Golan's few voters lean toward the center and the right: Blue and White 55% in 2020; Likud 37% in 2021; Yesh Atid 24%, National Unity 19%, Likud 15.5% and Yisrael Beiteinu 15.5% in 2022.

**Druze Knesset members.**
- 2022 election: only **Hamad Amar** (Yisrael Beiteinu, no. 6) was elected (IDI). Ali Salalha (Meretz no. 4) missed out when Meretz fell below the threshold. Akram Hasson (National Unity no. 16) missed out when the party won 12 seats.
- Later in the 25th Knesset: **Afif Abed** (Likud, from Yarka) replaced Yoav Gallant on 2025-01-05. **Akram Hasson** (New Hope, Daliyat) replaced Gideon Sa'ar on 2025-07-10. Source: Wikipedia list of 25th Knesset members and the MKs' own pages. Times of Israel (2026-05-05) confirms the current three: Abed, Amar and Hasson.
- Earlier, from pages I opened: Mufid Mari (Blue and White, 2021–22, from Hurfeish); Ali Salalha (Meretz, July 2021–2022). In 2018, three Druze MKs (Hasson of Kulanu, Amar of Yisrael Beiteinu, and Saleh Saad of Zionist Union) together filed the first High Court petition against the Nation-State Law (Hebrew Wikipedia, citing Haaretz 2018-07-22; `verified: false`). Five Druze MKs were elected in 2009 (Wikipedia).

**2026 lists (Druze candidates).** Source: Wikipedia, "Party lists for the 2026 Israeli legislative election," read 2026-10-05. Positions are `verified: false` until checked against CEC filings.
- Yisrael Beiteinu: **Hamad Amar, no. 7**.
- Yashar (Eisenkot): **Kamil Abu Rokon, no. 13**. He is a retired major general and former COGAT head, from Isfiya.
- Blue and White: **Mufid Mari, no. 7**.
- The Democrats: **Ali Salalha, no. 18**. Amir Khnifess, no. 25, may be Druze but this is unverified.

- Brit Achim ("Brothers' Covenant"), a Druze party launched 2026-05-04 by Col. (res.) Wajdi Sarhan, did not file a list. Times of Israel launch story: https://www.timesofisrael.com/former-idf-commander-wajdi-sarhan-launches-first-druze-political-party-in-decades/. Sarhan's withdrawal (Ynetnews, https://www.ynetnews.com/article/bfi2ue5qp): large parties did not want a joint run, and with small parties "the conditions for a joint and equal run agreement were not created."

## 3. Attitudes on the six issues

**Sample warning, to print on the page.** No large Druze-only survey exists. Druze make up about 8–12% of the Arab subsample in IDI surveys. For a total Arab sample of about 360, that is roughly 25–45 respondents. IDI itself says it "limited the use of" the religion breakdown "due to the small proportion of Christians and Druze in the Arab sample." Pew's 2014–15 survey had 439 Druze respondents, the largest sample I found, but it is old.

**IDI Israeli Democracy Index 2025.** Fieldwork 2025-05-04 to 05-28. Arab sample n=364, by telephone in Arabic. https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf
- War and security: trust in the IDF, Druze **84%** (64% in 2024), against Muslims 28% and Christians 34% (Jews 83.5%). "The state provides security for its citizens": Druze 67%, Christians 44%, Muslims 28.5%. "Safer for Arabs to live in Israel than elsewhere": Druze 68%, Muslims 52%, Christians 46.5%. Trust in the Shin Bet: Druze 42%, Christians 28%, Muslims 24%.
- Courts and the overhaul: trust in the Supreme Court, Druze 61%, Muslims 38%, Christians 37%. Yet **71% of Druze agree the Court "intervenes too much in government decisions"** (Christians 62%, Muslims 54%). Support for a constitution: Druze 83.5%, Christians 91%, Muslims 77%. Trust in the Attorney General: Druze 51%. Israeli democracy rated good or very good: Druze 36%; poor or very poor: 55%.
- Belonging: proud to be Israeli, Druze 77%, Christians 56%, Muslims 30%. Feel part of the state: Druze 68%. Optimistic about Israel's future: **Druze 81%**, higher than any Jewish group, against Muslims 42% and Christians 47%.
- Economy and welfare: "the state manages to ensure citizens' welfare," Druze 58%, Christians 32%, Muslims 31%. Trust in the government: Druze 29%. Trust in the Knesset: Druze 19%.
- Social trust: "Israelis can always count on other Israelis to help," Druze 46% (2022), 90% (2024), 74% (2025).
- Haredi draft, West Bank, religion and state: IDI 2025 gives no Druze breakdown on these.

**IDI 2016, attitudes of Arab citizens.** Fadi Omar. Fieldwork 2016-05-03 to 05-23. n=362 Arabs, of whom about 12% were Druze. https://www.idi.org.il/media/8292/ (PDF)
- Most important identity: religious (Druze) 54%, Israeli 37%, Arab 5%, Palestinian 0. "Not one of the Druze respondents named Palestinian identity as their primary identity."
- Proud to be Israeli: 83%, close to Jews (86%). Trust in the IDF 81%, the government 48%, the police 43%, political parties 14%.
- Only 2% of Druze respondents would accept a Jewish spouse. This reflects the religion's ban on marrying out, not a view of Jews.

**Pew 2014–15.** 439 Druze respondents. https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/israels-religiously-divided-society/
- West Bank: 66% of Druze say settlements hurt Israel's security (Muslims 61%, Christians 79%).
- Jewish state and democracy: majorities of Druze, as of Muslims and Christians, say Israel cannot be both at the same time.
- 71% say they are ethnically Arab (Muslims 99%, Christians 96%). 49% say religion is very important in their lives.

**Nation-State Law surveys.** I found no Druze-specific opinion poll on the 2018 Nation-State Law that I could open. The evidence is behavioral: the petition, the officers' protest and the rally (section 4). Afif Abed, now a Likud MK, told Ynet in January 2025 that he does "not see a problem with the Nation-State Law" (Ynet headline, cited on English Wikipedia; `verified: false`).

**2008 Tel Aviv University (Yusuf Hassan).** 94% of Druze define themselves as "Druze-Israeli" (Ynet, 2008-03-04, http://www.ynetnews.com/articles/0,7340,L-3514638,00.html). This is old and the sample size was not on the page.

## 4. Lived texture

**The 2018 Nation-State Law protest.** On 2018-07-25, about 100 Druze reserve officers protested the law. Brig. Gen. (res.) Amal As'ad: "After we built this home together with the Jews, the Nationality Law excludes the Druze community from that home." Brig. Gen. (res.) Imad Fares: "The feeling is it was decided to bury us outside of the graveyard." (Ynetnews, https://www.ynetnews.com/articles/0,7340,L-5316576,00.html)

On 2018-08-04, the Druze leadership organized a rally in Rabin Square. Ynet: "כ-90 אלף דרוזים, לצד מתנגדים אחרים לחוק… גדשו את הכיכר" ("some 90,000 Druze, alongside other opponents of the law, filled the square"; the estimator is not named). https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-5322446,00.html
- Sheikh Mowafaq Tarif: "ומעולם לא ערערנו על זהותה היהודית של המדינה" ("we never questioned the Jewish identity of the state"); "בתי הקברות הצבאיים יעידו על כך" ("the military cemeteries will testify to it"); "למרות הנאמנות הבלתי מסויגת שלנו למדינה, המדינה לא רואה בנו שווים" ("despite our unreserved loyalty to the state, the state does not see us as equals").
- Shchiv Shanan, father of a Druze police officer killed in the 2017 Temple Mount attack: "נשארתי דרוזי ישראלי גאה, אבל בני הפך לחלל סוג ב'" ("I remained a proud Druze Israeli, but my son became a second-class fallen soldier").

Netanyahu said he was "touched by the sentiments of our brothers and sisters in the Druze community" (JPost, 2018-07-29, quoted on Wikipedia; `verified: false`). Talks broke down when he walked out after a Druze leader would not withdraw the word "apartheid" (Wikipedia). The High Court upheld the law in July 2021.

**"Blood covenant" (ברית דמים, brit damim).** This is the common Israeli phrase for the Jewish–Druze military bond (English Wikipedia). I did not find it in any article I opened from 2018 or 2025. A 2008 Walla headline (not opened) quotes the prime minister of the day saying "no more blood covenant, but a covenant of life." The phrase is the Jewish-Israeli frame for the relationship. The Druze speakers I could verify talk instead about loyalty, sacrifice, equality and "our Israeliness."

**Majdal Shams, 2024-07-27.** A rocket hit a soccer field and killed 12 children, most of them aged 10–16, and wounded 42–44 people. Israel and Western governments blamed Hezbollah and an Iranian-made Falaq-1 rocket. Hezbollah denied it. AP's weapons experts said the evidence points to a rocket from Lebanon and raised the possibility that the hit was an accident. Per the local council (via CNN, cited on Wikipedia), none of the 12 dead held Israeli citizenship. Residents' statement: "We reject the shedding of a single drop of blood" in retaliation (The National, 2024-07-31, https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2024/07/31/majdal-shams-residents-golan-heights/). Council head Dolan Abu Saleh: "All of the Majdal Shams family lean towards peace. We do not want war or retribution." Four of the dead were his relatives. Netanyahu was met with chants of "murderer," and Smotrich was berated. On 2024-07-30, Israel killed Hezbollah commander Fuad Shukr in Beirut. The citizenship surge followed this strike and the fall of Assad in December 2024.

**Syria, 2025.** April–May 2025: attacks on Druze near Damascus. Israeli Druze protested by blocking roads. Amar and Tarif jointly urged protesters to go home (JPost, 2025-05-01, cited on Wikipedia; `verified: false`).

July 2025: fighting broke out in Suwayda from July 13 between Druze and Bedouin armed groups, and then Syrian government forces moved in. Israel struck government forces in Suwayda on July 15–16, then the General Staff complex in Damascus and the area near the presidential palace on July 16. Defense Minister Katz: Israel "will not allow harm to the Druze in Syria." On July 15–16, dozens and then several hundred to 1,000+ Israeli Druze crossed the fence near Majdal Shams (Wikipedia; Ynet 2025-07-15). The Syrian Observatory counted 516 dead by July 17; the Syrian Network for Human Rights counted 169 for July 13–17 (`verified: false`). Wikipedia also records reprisals by Druze groups against Bedouin after government forces withdrew.

Voices from the Ynet report of 2025-07-15 (https://www.ynetnews.com/article/by11g11e4ull):
- MK Hamad Amar: "The prime minister and defense minister have repeatedly promised to protect our brethren."
- MK Akram Hasson: "Al-Sharaa's messages are not sincere."
- Wafa al-Shaar of Mas'ade: "There's guerrilla warfare in the streets."
- Golan civilian official, March 2026 (ToI): "The Druze have tied their fate to the fate of the State of Israel, and everyone saw that Israel took a stand and chose a side."

**Religious leadership.** Sheikh Muwaffaq (Mowafaq) Tarif, born 1963, of Julis, has been spiritual leader since 1993. He succeeded his grandfather Amin Tarif, who led from 1928 to 1993. In January 2025 he met US officials, including Sen. Ted Cruz, to seek protection for Syrian Druze, and he urged intervention after the 2025 killings. He received the Presidential Medal of Honor in 2025. Suwayda's Sheikh al-Hinnawi publicly thanked him in August 2025. (English Wikipedia, Muwaffaq Tarif; and the Suwayda article.)

**Political voice, 2026.** Wajdi Sarhan, launching Brit Achim: "No more settling for crumbs. This time, we are asking for everything." He described the model of Druze politicians running inside the big parties as having failed the community (ToI, 2026-05-05). The party then withdrew.

## Source disagreements

- **Population:** CBS 2023 release gives 150,000 for end-2022. TPS (citing CBS) gives 152,000, also dated 2022. Hebrew Wikipedia (citing CBS 2026) gives about 154,000 for end-2024, but dates one figure to "April 2025." The Wikipedia infobox gives 119,400 in Israel plus 29,364 in the Golan.
- **Golan Druze population:** Shomrim (2022) 21,000; ToI (2025) 29,000+; Hebrew Wikipedia about 25,000 (2025). CBS's 125,400 citizens out of 154,000 implies about 29,000 non-citizens, which does not fit 20–40% Golan naturalization.
- **Golan citizenship share:** about 20% (Shomrim 2022; ToI January 2025) against about 37–43% by village (ToI March 2026). Both may be right, given the 2025 surge. ToI March 2026 says applications ran "500–600" a year before 2025, while ToI January 2025 gives about 400 a year for 2022–24.
- **Service rate:** "over 80%" (Georgetown, with no primary source given) against about 60% of Druze men ever served or serving (Pew sample). These measure different things.
- **Druze vote 2022:** IDI gives National Unity 30.4, Yisrael Beiteinu 18.3, Meretz 14.0, Likud 10.6. My Dayan-10 computation gives 30.1, 18.4, 14.9, 11.7. A set including Isfiya and excluding Peki'in gives 31.7, 16.7, 14.6, 11.4. Differences of 1–2 points come from which localities are counted.
- **Druze MKs elected in 2022:** IDI says one (Amar). The Wikipedia 25th-Knesset page says "three Druze lawmakers." Three is correct only after the 2025 replacements.
- **Identity:** Pew finds 71% say they are ethnically Arab. IDI 2016 finds 5% name Arab as their *most important* identity. The questions differ: one asks about ethnicity, the other about priority among identities.
- **IDI 2016 fieldwork:** English Wikipedia says 2015; the report says May 2016.
- **Majdal Shams wounded:** 42 (Wikipedia) against 44 (The National).
- **Wajdi Sarhan:** ToI calls him Col. (res.); Ynetnews calls him Brig. Gen. (res.). Withdrawal date: Wikipedia says 8 August, the Ynet page says 8 September 2026.
