## 1. What is at stake

### Where the war stands (as of early October 2026)

- **Gaza.** The ceasefire under the US-backed plan (Trump's 20-point plan, later UN Security Council Resolution 2803) took effect **October 10, 2025** ([Wikipedia, Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war), opened). Phase 2 (governance and demilitarization) began **January 14, 2026** ([UK House of Commons Library, 2026-02-17](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10492/), opened). Wikipedia describes the present as a "de jure ceasefire… De facto limited war." Israel carries out near-daily strikes ([ToI, 2026-05-05](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-wont-hold-israel-to-truce-terms-if-hamas-doesnt-okay-disarmament-offer/), opened). On Sept 29, 2026 Israel killed Izz al-Din al-Beik, head of Hamas's armed wing in northern Gaza ([Al Jazeera, 2026-09-29](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/29/israeli-forces-kill-hamas-commander-izz-al-din-al-beik-in-gaza-attack), headline seen; detail via [Next Century Foundation index, 2026-10-03](https://nextcenturyfoundation.org/gaza-information-index-3-october-2026), opened, secondary). On Sept 28 Israeli security officials were reported to be weighing an expanded operation ([ToI liveblog 2026-09-28](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-september-28-2026/), opened).
- **Territory.** Israeli-controlled Gaza grew from about **53%** at the October 2025 ceasefire to **nearly 70%** by August 2026 ([Chatham House, 2026-08-03](https://www.chathamhouse.org/2026/08/agreeing-disarm-hamas-trying-call-israels-bluff-gaza), opened). The Danish Refugee Council gives 58%→~70% (via [NCF 2026-10-03](https://nextcenturyfoundation.org/gaza-information-index-3-october-2026), secondhand).
- **Disarmament deadlock.** The Board of Peace (chaired by Trump; envoy Nickolay Mladenov) gave Hamas a framework on April 3, 2026 requiring it to "hand over all of its weapons" over eight months; Hamas missed the April 11 deadline and in early May "largely bucked" the demand ([ToI, 2026-05-05](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-wont-hold-israel-to-truce-terms-if-hamas-doesnt-okay-disarmament-offer/), opened). In early August Hamas accepted a "decommissioning" roadmap on conditions, including Israeli withdrawal to the Yellow Line ([Chatham House](https://www.chathamhouse.org/2026/08/agreeing-disarm-hamas-trying-call-israels-bluff-gaza)). On Aug 4 the Board said no Israeli withdrawal until "the complete decommissioning of weapons in the Strip"; Netanyahu's office said the published version "does not reflect Israel's positions," and Smotrich demanded a new cabinet vote ([Al Jazeera, 2026-08-04](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/board-of-peace-says-no-israeli-withdrawal-from-gaza-before-hamas-disarms), opened). Class doc 11a: Netanyahu on Aug 9 said "Israel rejects the 15-point document" and no withdrawal "until Hamas is genuinely disarmed" (Al Jazeera 2026-08-09; not reopened). Chatham House's read: each side demands a sequence the other will not accept.
- **Iran.** Israel and the US struck Iran on **February 28, 2026**; Supreme Leader Khamenei was killed ([House of Commons Library, 2026-07-02](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10637/), opened). Israel's name for the campaign is "Operation Roaring Lion" (used in [IDI's March 2026 survey](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30641/israeli-voice-index-march-2026-eng-data.pdf), opened). A pause began April 8; a US–Iran memorandum was signed June 17; Trump called it "over" on July 7 and it lapsed Aug 17 with no deal. Full-scale fighting is paused but the war is "far from resolved" ([Al Jazeera, 2026-08-17](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/17/us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding-expires-how-and-why-it-fell-apart), opened). Israeli deaths in the Iran war: 40 soldiers, 1 contractor, 31 civilians ([Wikipedia, 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war), opened; secondary, not confirmed against an Israeli official source).
- **Lebanon (northern front).** Hezbollah resumed fire March 2; Israel invaded southern Lebanon March 3, 2026 ([Wikipedia, 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)). An Israel–Lebanon ceasefire took effect April 16 and was extended repeatedly ([Wikipedia, 2026 Israel–Lebanon ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Israel%E2%80%93Lebanon_ceasefire), opened). Netanyahu says forces will stay in a southern Lebanon "security zone" as long as "Israel's security needs require it" ([PBS/AP, 2026-06-19](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/israel-and-hezbollah-renew-ceasefire-after-u-s-and-iran-call-off-talks-over-fighting-in-lebanon), opened). Al Jazeera says Israel holds "roughly one-fifth of Lebanon" ([2026-08-17](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/17/us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding-expires-how-and-why-it-fell-apart)). Late September: IDF paratroopers still operating in the town of Mansouri; Lebanon's PM met Rubio on a withdrawal timeline ([ToI liveblog 2026-09-28](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-september-28-2026/)). Status of displaced northern Israeli residents in 2026: not found.

### The hostages (the issue is closed as a rescue question, open as an accountability question)

- **251** people were abducted on Oct 7, 2023. **166 came home alive; 85 were returned dead** — of those, **44 were already dead when taken and 41 were taken alive and killed in captivity** ([ToI, 2026-10-03](https://timesofisrael.com/ex-hostage-point-man-says-israel-could-have-returned-all-oct-7-captives-a-year-earlier), opened).
- Release phases: 4 freed Oct 20–23, 2023; 105 in the Nov 24–30, 2023 truce (81 Israelis, 23 Thai, 1 Filipino); 8 rescued by the IDF; 30 in the Jan–Mar 2025 deal; the last **20 living hostages on Oct 13, 2025** ([Wikipedia, Gaza war hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_war_hostage_crisis), opened).
- The last body, police officer **Ran Gvili**, was recovered **Jan 26, 2026**, after 843 days. Netanyahu: "We promised, and I promised, to bring everyone back, and we brought everyone back, to the very last one" ([ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/all-of-them-home-now-body-of-last-hostage-ran-gvili-found-in-gaza-returned-to-israel/), opened). His mother Talik Gvili is No. 9 on Likud's list (class doc 11a).
- The live argument now is **whether a deal could have come sooner.** Maj. Gen. (res.) Nitzan Alon, the IDF's former hostage point man, told the Washington Institute (reported Oct 3, 2026): "I'm pretty sure we could and should have shortened the war, brought all the captives home maybe a year earlier," and that Netanyahu "also had his political and personal considerations" ([ToI](https://timesofisrael.com/ex-hostage-point-man-says-israel-could-have-returned-all-oct-7-captives-a-year-earlier), opened).

### The human cost on the Israeli side

- Official figures, Oct 7, 2023 – Oct 4, 2026 (Defense Ministry and National Insurance Institute): **1,318 security personnel** killed (1,153 IDF soldiers incl. 43 civilian security squad members; 116 police; 11 Prison Service; 9 Shin Bet; 5 firefighters; 24 disabled veterans who died of wounds) and **1,029 civilians** killed (778 on Oct 7 itself). 87,286 people recognized as victims of hostile action. More than 7,150 people joined bereaved families, including 880 orphans ([Arutz Sheva/INN, 2026-10-04](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/434051), opened; outlet is right-leaning, the figures are attributed to official bodies).
- Defense Ministry before Memorial Day 2026: 170 soldiers killed since the previous Memorial Day ([ToI, 2026-04-16](https://www.timesofisrael.com/170-soldiers-added-to-list-of-israels-fallen-since-last-memorial-day/), opened).

### Reservist burden

- Nearly 300,000 reservists activated after Oct 7; average **136 days a year**; some called up seven times; 41% of reservists surveyed by the employment service said they were fired or left their jobs; 66% of the wounded are reservists; government authorized up to 450,000 call-ups in May 2025 ([ToI, 2025-05](https://www.timesofisrael.com/as-idf-raises-reservist-call-up-cap-to-450000-weary-troops-decry-low-haredi-enlistment/), opened; date per class doc 05).
- 2026: the IDF promised a 70-day cap; "Most reserve units have already exceeded 100 days of reserve duty this year" ([JPost analysis, 2026-09-25](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/defense-news/article-909671), opened).
- Who serves (IDI, from administrative data): share of Jewish men 21–44 doing reserve duty in an average month, 2025 — **religious 4.3%, traditional 2.6%, secular 1.7%, Haredi 0.3%**. In 2023: religious 15.2%, secular and traditional 11.2% each. Secular men remain the largest single group of reservists in absolute numbers; Arab citizens about 1% of reservists ([IDI, Finkelstein & Portal, 2026-08-20](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65427), opened). This is the bridge from the war issue to the Haredi draft issue.

### The commission-of-inquiry fight

- A **state commission of inquiry** under the 1968 law is set up by the government, but its members are appointed by the **Supreme Court president**, it must be headed by a judge, and it has court-like powers to compel witnesses ([IDI, Blander & Lavi, 2025-12-24](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/62785), opened).
- The government's alternative (bill P/6380/25) has members chosen by Knesset/political actors, possibly co-chairs from both camps, and topics set by a ministerial committee headed by the PM ([same](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/62785)). Netanyahu opposes a state commission because the Supreme Court president, he says, is biased against him, and wants the inquiry to cover the 1993 Oslo Accords, the 2005 Gaza disengagement and the 2023 protest movement (paraphrase in [ToI, 2026-07](https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-passes-controversial-politically-appointed-oct-7-probe-in-1st-reading/), opened; no direct quote found).
- Timeline: first reading passed **59–0, opposition boycotting** (ToI dates it Monday July 7, 2026; JPost says July 6). Revised version: 80-of-120 vote to appoint; failing that, coalition and opposition three members each plus four bereaved-family observers; can operate with three members. Lapid: "The opposition will not be part of a fake show whose sole purpose is to whitewash and prevent the investigation." The October Council (bereaved families): "This disgraceful bill is not intended to uncover the truth, but to bury it alongside our loved ones" ([ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-passes-controversial-politically-appointed-oct-7-probe-in-1st-reading/), opened).
- July 23: government told the High Court it could not finish the bill before the election recess, "due to the constraints of the Knesset's schedule," and asked the court to leave the question to voters ([JPost, 2026-07-23](http://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-903419), opened).
- **Sept 23, 2026**: the High Court deferred ruling. It accepted the Attorney General's position that a state commission is warranted but should be decided by the next government; the government must respond by **Jan 15, 2027** ([ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/high-court-delays-decision-on-october-7-state-commission-until-after-election/), opened).
- The fight is visible on the street: a "Sukkah of the Failure" event in Tel Aviv on Sept 28 with bereaved families calling for a state commission ([ToI liveblog 2026-09-28](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-september-28-2026/)).

## 2. What Israelis think

### Hostages first vs. fighting on (2024–2025)

The IDI asked repeatedly which war goal mattered more, "toppling Hamas, or bringing home all the hostages." Hostages first: about **50% in January 2024 → 68% in March–April 2025** (Hamas first 25%). Jews by camp, March–April 2025: left 91%, center 80.5%, right 52% (right Hamas-first 38.5%) ([IDI, fieldwork 2025-03-31 to 04-06, n=598 Jews + 150 Arabs](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/59019), opened). The page gives the Jan 2024 point as approximate.

Other IDI points on the same theme (wording differs each time, so these are not one series):
- May 2024, Jews only, deal vs. Rafah operation: 56% deal, 37% Rafah; right 55% Rafah; Likud voters 48.5% Rafah vs 44% deal; RZ voters 83% Rafah; women 63.5% deal vs men 49% ([IDI 54052, fieldwork 2024-05-01/06](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/54052), opened).
- Jan 2025, deal for all hostages in exchange for ending the war: 57.5% overall; Jews 53% comprehensive, 13% partial, 27% military pressure; Arabs 81.5% comprehensive ([IDI 57957, 2025-01-05/08](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/57957), opened).
- Aug 2025, deal including ceasefire and full IDF withdrawal: 67% overall, Jews 62%, Arabs 81%; Jews on the right 47% yes, 44% no; majorities of Likud, Shas and UTJ 2022 voters yes, RZ voters majority no ([IDI 61601, 2025-08-24/28](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61601), opened).
- Sept 2025, "time has come to end the war in Gaza": 66%, up 13 points from Sept 2024 (so ~53% then); Jews 60%, Arabs 93%. Among Jews favoring ending it, the main reason was the hostages (50.5%); 2% of Jews cited reducing harm to Gazans ([IDI 61845, 2025-09-14/18, n=1,000](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61845), opened).

What is worth noticing: even the Israeli right moved toward "hostages first" over 2024–25, and the Jewish case for ending the war was almost entirely about the hostages, not about Gaza's civilians.

### After the ceasefire (Dec 2025 – Sept 2026): skeptical, not dovish

INSS National Security Survey (online, iPanel, ~805 Hebrew + ~150 Arabic respondents each wave):
- Dec 2025: 54% support moving to Phase B; 58% believe the situation requires a return to fighting ([INSS Dec 2025](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-december-2025/), opened).
- Jan 2026: 40% say only the IDF can disarm Hamas; 23% say disarmament is impossible; Jews 48% vs Arabs 11% on "only the IDF" ([INSS Jan 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-january-2026/), opened).
- Feb 2026: 51.5% say moving to Phase B does not serve Israel's interests ([INSS Feb 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-february-2026/), opened).
- May 2026: 42% think Israel has won or will win against Hamas, 49% not; 59% dissatisfied with enforcement of the Gaza ceasefire ([INSS May 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-may-2026/), opened).
- **Sept 24, 2026 (three-year special survey)**: only **27%** say Israel won in Gaza (Lebanon 38%, Iran 23%); 61% satisfied with the IDF's management of the Gaza war vs **34%** with the government's; 59% think an Oct 7-type attack from Gaza could recur (up from 52% a year earlier) ([INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/three-years-survey/), opened; fieldwork dates not on the page).

Iran and Lebanon: in March 2026, 77.6% of Jews and 19.2% of Arabs backed continuing the Iran campaign ([IDI March 2026 data](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30641/israeli-voice-index-march-2026-eng-data.pdf), opened); in April 61% opposed the Iran ceasefire ([INSS Apr 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-april-2026/)); in June 57% opposed full withdrawal from southern Lebanon ([INSS June 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-june-2026/), opened).

### Who is blamed for Oct 7

- Oct 24–26, 2023: 63% said responsibility should be examined after the war (left 50, center 67, right 77); on who to trust to run the war, 55% IDF heads, 7% Netanyahu ([IDI 51198](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/51198), opened).
- Feb 25–28, 2025: 87% say Netanyahu should accept responsibility; 72.5% say he should resign now (48%) or after the war (25%). Jews on the right: 56.5% resign now or later, 25.5% now ([IDI 58648](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/58648), opened).
- Sept 2025: 64% resign now (45%) or after the war (19%) ([IDI 61845](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61845)).
- Trust gap, July 23–26, 2026 (Jewish sample on most items): IDF 73%, Chief of Staff Zamir 59%, Netanyahu 31%, Defense Minister Katz 30%, government 25%. Netanyahu: coalition voters 68%, opposition voters 6% ([INSS July 2026 full report](https://www.inss.org.il/INSS_National_Security_Survey_July_2026_EN_FIXED_v16.html), opened). 56% think political decisions are not mainly based on security considerations ([same](https://www.inss.org.il/INSS_National_Security_Survey_July_2026_EN_FIXED_v16.html)).
- I did not find a 2026 poll that asks directly "who is most responsible": Netanyahu, the army, Shin Bet, or the protest movement. The Netanyahu camp's argument (the protests and the "conception" of earlier governments share blame) is visible in what he wants the inquiry to cover, not in a poll I could open.

### How much it matters to the vote

Aug 31–Sept 3, 2026: **68% of Jewish Israelis** call Oct 7 "a central factor in deciding which party to vote for" (Arabs 32%). Foreign affairs and security is the top consideration for Jews at 39%, economy and cost of living 38%. By camp: right, security first (47%); center, economy first, security fourth (25%); left, religion and state first, security third ([IDI 66177](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177), opened).

### Jewish–Arab gap on Gaza's civilians

July 27–31, 2025: 78% of Jews and 22.5% of Arabs said Israel makes efforts to avoid causing suffering to Gazans; 79% of Jews said they were not troubled by reports of famine and suffering, while 86% of Arabs were ([IDI 60357](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/60357), opened).

## 4. How an American reader is likely to misread it

1. **Assuming the Israeli debate mirrors the American one.** In the US the Gaza argument is about Palestinian civilian deaths, aid, and whether the war is just. In Israel the mainstream argument was about the hostages versus "total victory," and now about whether the government's management failed. Among Jews who wanted the war ended in Sept 2025, 2% cited harm to Gazans; half cited the hostages ([IDI 61845](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61845)). 78% of Jews thought Israel makes efforts to avoid civilian suffering ([IDI 60357](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/60357)). The Israeli "left" on this issue is mostly a hostages-and-accountability position, not a humanitarian-Gaza one; the latter is voiced mainly by Arab parties and a small Jewish left. Palestinian citizens of Israel hold views much closer to American critics (93% for ending the war in Sept 2025).
2. **Missing that the hostage-families movement was the central domestic protest.** The Hostages and Missing Families Forum was founded Oct 13, 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostages_and_Missing_Families_Forum), opened); weekly Saturday rallies at Hostages Square. After six hostages were found executed, organizers estimated about **500,000** people rallied on Sept 1, 2024, "the largest rallies since Oct. 7," and the Histadrut called a general strike for Sept 2 ([ToI liveblog 2024-09-01](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-september-1-2024/), opened). Families were themselves split: the Tikva Forum opposed deals that ended the war ([ToI topic page](https://www.timesofisrael.com/topic/tikva-forum/)). Both camps now have candidates on lists (Talik Gvili on Likud; Zvika Mor on RZ; Ohad Ben Ami and Shir Siegel with Yashar).
3. **Reading "the war is over."** There is a ceasefire on paper; Israel holds about 70% of Gaza, strikes almost daily, holds a zone in southern Lebanon, and the Iran war is paused, not ended. Most Israelis do not think Israel won in Gaza (27% say it did).
4. **Missing the inquiry fight.** For many Israeli voters the war question in 2026 is accountability: who decides who investigates Oct 7. It is tied to the courts fight because the dispute is over whether the Supreme Court president picks the panel. Three-quarters say they want a state commission when asked yes/no, including half of coalition voters ([INSS Sept 2026](https://www.inss.org.il/publication/three-years-survey/)).
5. **Assuming "anti-Netanyahu" means "dovish."** Every major opposition list except the Joint List and Ra'am opposes the Board of Peace terms as too soft on Hamas, and opposes a Palestinian state. The opposition attacks Netanyahu on responsibility and management, not on whether to fight.

- **Hostages returned alive:** ToI (2026-10-03) says 166 alive + 85 dead = 251. Wikipedia's phase breakdown sums to 168 alive (105+5+8+30+20) plus 85 dead. The difference may come from how the four pre-war captives and non-Oct-7 cases are counted. Use ToI's 166/85/44/41.
