## 1. What is at stake

### The basic arrangement
- Israel has no civil marriage or divorce. Jews marry and divorce through the state Chief Rabbinate and rabbinical courts; Muslims, Christians and Druze through their own state-recognized religious courts. Pew's 2016 report states it plainly: "Israel does not allow civil marriage; Jewish marriages must be sanctioned by Orthodox rabbis." (Pew, 2016-03-08, https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/israels-religiously-divided-society/)
- The arrangement goes back to the 1947 "status quo" letter and the Ottoman/British millet system. (Background; not separately sourced in this pass — needs a citation before publishing.)
- Who it leaves out: Israelis the Rabbinate does not consider Jewish, mostly immigrants from the former Soviet Union and their children who came under the Law of Return's grandchild clause. ToI: "hundreds of thousands of Israelis who are not considered Jewish" by the Chief Rabbinate (ToI 2025-07-09, https://www.timesofisrael.com/bill-to-limit-law-of-return-shot-down-despite-support-from-ultra-orthodox-mks/). Class doc 05: "Others" (neither Jewish nor Arab) 4.7–4.9% of the population (Taub Dec 2025) **[not re-opened]**; 71.7% of 2020 FSU immigrants not Jewish under halacha (Knesset RIC via ToI 2022-11-17) **[not re-opened]**.

### Workarounds for marriage
- **Abroad (Cyprus, Prague, etc.):** the state registers civil marriages performed abroad. 9,550 couples registered marriages performed abroad in 2019 (ToI 2023-03-07, https://www.timesofisrael.com/israel-must-recognize-online-marriages-conducted-via-utah-supreme-court-rules/).
- **Utah online marriage:** On March 7, 2023 the Supreme Court ruled the Population and Immigration Authority must register marriages performed online through Utah. Deputy President Uzi Vogelman: "Registrars are not entitled to make decisions on complex legal questions when presented with a legal public document." About 1,200 couples had used it since 2020. Shas MK Moshe Arbel: "a sad joke at the expense of all of Israel's religious and traditional citizens." (ToI 2023-03-07, same URL). I found no later law reversing it (see Stale/missing).

### Conversion
- March 1, 2021: the Supreme Court held that people who "convert in Reform and Conservative communities in Israel should be recognized as Jews for the purpose of the Law of Return," 8 of 9 justices (Sohlberg wanted a 12-month delay). The court said the ruling had "no religious implications" — it covers citizenship only, not marriage through the Rabbinate. (JPost 2021-03-01, https://www.jpost.com/breaking-news/israel-to-recognize-reform-conservative-conversion-for-law-of-return-660592)
- The Rabbinate keeps the monopoly on conversions that count for marriage. Shas has pushed since at least 2018–2020 for a law giving the Rabbinate sole authority over all conversions; Deri revived it in June 2020 (ToI 2020-06-18, https://www.timesofisrael.com/interior-minister-revives-efforts-for-orthodox-control-over-jewish-conversion/). I did not find that it passed in the 25th Knesset.

### "Who is a Jew" and the grandchild clause
- The 1970 amendment to the Law of Return extends eligibility to anyone with one Jewish grandparent (and spouses), whether or not they are Jewish under halacha. About 500,000 people have immigrated under it since 1970 (JTA 2025-07-03, https://www.jta.org/2025/07/03/israel/knesset-bill-would-narrow-eligibility-for-israel-citizenship).
- July 9, 2025: Noam MK Avi Maoz's bill to remove the clause was rejected 18–54 in a preliminary reading; Shas and UTJ MKs voted for it while boycotting the coalition. Explanatory note: "the law is being exploited by many who have severed all ties with the Jewish people and their traditions, and in effect empties the law of its original intention." ITIM's Rabbi Seth Farber called it "simply a provocation." (ToI 2025-07-09, URL above)
- JTA notes similar bills were filed earlier by Shlomo Karhi (Likud), Bezalel Smotrich and Simcha Rothman (RZ) (JTA 2025-07-03). Since 1990s FSU immigration is the main group affected, this issue runs straight into Yisrael Beiteinu's base.

### Kashrut
- July 15, 2026: Knesset passed, 46–41, a law restoring the Chief Rabbinate's monopoly over kosher certification, reversing the 2022 (Bennett–Lapid government, Matan Kahana) reform that let private Orthodox bodies certify. Championed by Shas. Opponents: it "will eliminate competition and ultimately increase costs for and harm businesses and consumers." ToI reports it was part of a package in which the Haredi parties got the Torah study Basic Law and the draft-arrest freeze in exchange for backing judicial and media bills. (ToI 2026-07-15, https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-passes-law-restoring-chief-rabbinates-monopoly-over-kosher-certification/)
- 2019 baseline opinion: 63% of Jews favored ending the Rabbinate's kashrut monopoly (IDI, Aug 2019, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/28411).

### Rabbinical courts' reach
- Nov 17, 2025: a two-year interim law, 53–38, lets rabbinical courts rule on child support ("expenses for their upbringing, education and medical care") with both parties' consent, answering a 2025 High Court ruling that they lacked that authority. Reported as traded for Haredi support of Karhi's media bill. Farber (ITIM): "It is unacceptable that such significant matters be decided so dramatically based solely on which panel a couple 'happened' to appear in front of." (ToI 2025-11-17, https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-passes-law-expanding-rabbinical-courts-powers-to-arbitrate-civil-disputes/)
- March 24, 2026: the Rabbinical Courts (arbitration) law passed 65–41 on second and third readings: rabbinical and Sharia courts may arbitrate civil money disputes when both parties consent in writing; excludes criminal, administrative and personal-status matters. Gafni had first filed it two days after the 2006 High Court ruling that barred this. Gafni: "when two people come and wish to settle monetary disputes according to Torah law, with the full consent of both sides, what is wrong with that?" Lapid: "Mark this day. Today is the day the status quo died—dead, buried, eliminated, canceled." (VIN News 2026-03-24, https://vinnews.com/2026/03/24/knesset-passes-rabbinic-courts-law-restoring-jurisdiction-over-arbitrated-monetary-matters/; consent and Sharia-court scope also JPost 2026-03-24, http://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-890990)

### Shabbat: transit and commerce
- National buses and trains do not run on Shabbat, except in Haifa, Arab localities and some mixed areas. Since 2015–2019, municipal and civic services have grown: Shabus (Jerusalem), Noa Tanoa (Tel Aviv), and Tel Aviv's free Na'im Busofash (2019) serving Gush Dan "and beyond"; such initiatives run in "more than 15 cities." (Reichman University report, Aug 2025, https://www.runi.ac.il/media/53mmwyif/transportation_on_shabbat.pdf)
- IDI Religion and State report 2022: of 2,913 bus lines, 284 (~10%) run on Shabbat; "In the central region, a de-facto public transportation system runs uninterrupted every weekend." 26% of malls open on Shabbat (20% in 2015); 98% of cinemas, 82% of museums. Economy Ministry Shabbat-work violation files fell from 360 (2018) to 89 (2021). (ToI 2022-09-20, https://www.timesofisrael.com/israels-official-shabbat-rules-being-increasingly-broken-on-the-ground-research/)
- In practice the Shabbat fight is local (municipal by-laws, who pays for weekend buses) and the national question is whether the state runs or funds it. Bennett, Lieberman and Golan all propose "local choice."

### The Western Wall
- January 2016: the cabinet approved the "Kotel compromise": an expanded egalitarian section at the southern (Robinson's Arch) area, one shared entrance, and a public council including Reform and Conservative movements and Women of the Wall, with at least NIS 5 million a year. Frozen June 2017; never implemented. (Wikipedia, Kotel compromise, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotel_compromise — use for dates only; confirm with a news source)
- The High Court later ordered the state to proceed with upgrades to the egalitarian area without further delay (ToI 2026-03-16, https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-committee-advances-bill-expanding-orthodox-control-over-western-wall-plaza/).
- February 2026: Avi Maoz's Western Wall bill would give the two chief rabbis ultimate authority over Jewish holy sites including the egalitarian section, define non-Orthodox worship there as "desecration," and set up to seven years' prison. It passed 56–47 on Feb 25–26, 2026. Netanyahu withdrew formal coalition backing but let coalition MKs vote freely. March 16, 2026: House Committee sent it to the Constitution Committee. (JTA 2026-02-25, https://www.jta.org/2026/02/25/religion/bill-that-would-effectively-bar-egalitarian-prayer-at-the-western-wall-advances-in-israels-knesset; ToI 2026-03-16; JC, https://www.thejc.com/news/israel/knesset-bill-orthodox-western-wall-mcambwxs)
- Reactions: Women of the Wall, "a black day for the State of Israel" (JTA); Rabbi Jacob Blumenthal (Rabbinical Assembly), "a dangerous step toward declaring war on Jewish unity and religious pluralism" (JTA); IMPJ CEO Anna Kislanski: "The State of Israel is about to criminalise non-Orthodox Jewish prayer at the Western Wall" (JC).
- Status after dissolution (July 17, 2026): the bill lapses unless the next Knesset applies the continuity rule. Not confirmed — see Stale/missing.

### Religious budgets, yeshiva funding and the core curriculum
- 2026 budget passed March 30, 2026, 62–55: NIS 850.6 billion. Funding for Haredi educational institutions rose from NIS 4.1 billion to NIS 5.17 billion, including about NIS 800 million added in a late-night amendment. Smotrich: "a budget that takes care of everyone." Lapid: "the greatest theft in the history of the state." (ToI 2026-03-30, https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-approves-2026-budget-israels-largest-ever-sending-billions-to-haredi-institutions/)
- March 10, 2026: cabinet approved NIS 5 billion in coalition funds, of which NIS 2.2 billion for Haredi purposes, including NIS 1.56 billion for yeshivas and kollels, NIS 75m for "recognized but unofficial" schools, NIS 32m for exempt schools. Bennett: "While the people of Israel are fighting on the front lines, this government is busy looting the public purse." (ToI 2026-03-10, https://www.timesofisrael.com/cabinet-okays-1-6-billion-in-coalition-funds-including-715-million-for-ultra-orthodox/) Note: the summary also gave "53.19%" as the Haredi share, which does not match 2.2/5.0 = 44% [arith]. Check the article before using any share.
- How Haredi schools are funded (IDI explainer, 2023-06-16, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/49956): "exempt" institutions must teach 55% of core studies and get 55% funding (42% of Haredi elementary boys); "recognized but unofficial" 75%/75%; the Shas and Independent networks 100%/100%; State-Haredi 100%. State Comptroller 2020: "84% of boys in Haredi secondary schools did not study any core curriculum subjects."
- Enforcement: IDI's Ariel Finkelstein (July 2025): only 6% of network schools had budget cuts for non-compliance; average cut NIS 225,000 (~6% of budget) in 2023–24; schools "still receive the vast majority of their budget while teaching whatever they please." A 2022 study: 76% of Haredi parents said their sons did not learn English. (ToI 2025-07-03, https://www.timesofisrael.com/state-turns-blind-eye-as-haredi-schools-sidestep-core-curriculum-study-reveals/)
- Live court case: whether UTJ/Shas network schools get full funding only if they teach the full core. Education Minister Kisch (Likud), filing Sept 2026, asked to keep proportional funding: an "all-or-nothing rule could instead push schools out of the networks." The ministry reported 498 of 517 network schools taught 96–100% of the core in 2025–26. (JPost Sept 2026, https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-907846) The ministry's self-reported compliance and IDI's estimates disagree sharply — see Source disagreements.

### Torah study Basic Law
- July 13, 2026, 63–52: Basic Law declaring Torah study a "foundational value" of the state and Jewish people; aimed at giving legal cover to yeshiva exemptions. Gafni: "This Basic Law will serve as the state's moral compass." Tropper (Yashar): "draft dodgers are preferred to warriors." The Movement for Quality Government petitioned the High Court immediately. (ToI 2026-07-13, https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-passes-contentious-basic-law-declaring-torah-study-a-constitutional-value/) — Cross-link to the draft page.

### Gender segregation
- July 16, 2026, 52–43: law sponsored by Otzma MK Limor Son Har-Melech letting universities and colleges run gender-segregated master's and doctoral programs, extending a 2021 High Court ruling that allowed limited segregated undergraduate tracks for Haredim. Committee chair Zvi Sukkot (RZ): to "expand freedom of choice." Democrats MK Naama Lazimi: the coalition seeks "to oppress us, erase us and restrict our freedom." (ToI 2026-07-16, https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-passes-law-expanding-gender-segregation-in-academia/)
- Segregation on buses, at public events and in municipal services: not researched in this pass (search budget ran out). Needs a source before the page mentions it.

## 2. What Israelis think

### Who Israeli Jews say they are (self-identification)
CBS, Jews aged 20+, self-definition (secular / traditional-not-so-religious / traditional-religious / religious / Haredi):
- 2009: 41.4 / 26.3 / 13.0 / 11.7 / 8.2 (class doc 05, CBS tec25) **[not re-opened]**
- 2025: 42.7 / 21.5 / 12.0 / 12.0 / 11.4 (class doc 05/06, CBS release 2025-09-17) **[not re-opened]**
- 2026: 39.6 / 21.8 / 14.0 / 12.4 / 11.5 (VIN News 2026-09-09, https://vinnews.com/2026/09/09/israel-enters-5787-with-population-growth-high-birthrate-and-rising-emigration/). VIN: "The figures are based on self-definition."
- Pew 2016 (fieldwork Oct 2014–May 2015, n=3,789 Jews): Hiloni 49%, Haredi 9% (Pew short read, https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2016/03/08/key-findings-religion-politics-israel/); Masorti 29%, Dati 13% **[not re-opened; class doc 02]**.
- Worth noticing: secular is still the largest single label but has dropped below 40%; the two "traditional" categories together (35.8% [arith]) are nearly as large. Adults only — first-grade enrollment is far more religious (class doc 02: 36.9% state-secular, 16.4% state-religious, 23.8% Haredi, 22.9% Arab/Bedouin/Druze, 2025–26).

### Civil marriage
- **JPPI Israeli Society Index, fieldwork Apr 30–May 4, 2026, n=749 (547 Jewish, 202 Arab):** 59% of Jewish Israelis support a civil marriage option; 55% of all Israelis. When the question specifies it includes marriages between Jews and non-Jews, Jewish support falls to 46% (all Israelis 44% support / 50% oppose). By religiosity (general / with intermarriage): secular 91 / 75; traditional-not-religious 54 / 41; traditional-religious 40 / 17; religious 23 / 13; Haredi 15 / 6. Arab respondents: 39% support, 46% oppose. Same-sex marriage option: 59% of Jews support; 75% of Arabs oppose. (https://jppi.org.il/en/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%93-jppi-%D7%9C%D7%97%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%94-%D7%94%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%90%D7%99-2026/) Exact question wording not on the page.
- **IDI, Aug 7–8, 2019, n=760 Jews, ±4%:** 59.5% support civil marriage; secular 84.5%, traditional-non-religious 68%, traditional-religious 41%, national-religious 22.5%, Haredi 4%. (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/28411) Category labels as rendered by the summarizer ("non-religious," "religious") — confirm IDI's exact labels.
- **Pew 2016:** "Should Conservative and Reform rabbis be allowed to conduct marriages?" All Jews 40% yes / 54% no; Haredi 2, Dati 7, Masorti 24, Hiloni 65. (https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/religion-politics-and-public-life/) This asks about non-Orthodox rabbis, not civil marriage — different question.
- **Smith Institute for Hiddush, late July 2025 (advocacy-commissioned; n not given):** 60% support recognizing civil, Conservative and Reform marriages; 61% support "Utah marriages"; 53% would still choose Orthodox marriage if all were legal (Haredi 99%, religious 85%, secular 16%, FSU immigrants 22%). By vote: Likud 56% support, RZ/Otzma 24%, opposition/Bennett 85%. (JPost 2025, https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-863596) Use only as a supplement; Hiddush campaigns on this issue.
- Worth noticing: majority support for civil marriage has been stable around 60% of Jews from 2019 to 2026, but it rests on the secular and the non-religious traditional. Specifying intermarriage drops it below half. And a majority would still marry Orthodox if they had a free choice — support for civil marriage is mostly about choice, not about rejecting the Rabbinate's wedding.

### Shabbat public transit
- **Reichman University Institute for Liberty and Responsibility, Jewish respondents, Apr 2022–Mar 2025** (question: "Do you support or oppose operating public transportation on Saturdays (Shabbat) throughout the country, except in areas with major ultra-orthodox or religious populations?"; "don't know" excluded; n not given). Overall support 67.1–73.8%, rising. Secular 87.6–92.5%; traditional 65.3% (Apr 2022) → 68.4% (Mar 2025); religious 35.5% (Apr 2022) → 42.0% (Mar 2023) → 35.0% (Mar 2024) → 45.0% (Mar 2025); Haredi 11.2% → 16.2%. Left 89.9% → 92.4%; center ~83%; right 47.3% (low, Sept 2022) → 55.3% (Mar 2024) → 51.7% (Mar 2025). (https://www.runi.ac.il/media/53mmwyif/transportation_on_shabbat.pdf, report Aug 2025; text read directly)
- **IDI Aug 2019:** 60% support transit everywhere except religious/Haredi areas; secular 86%; Haredi 97% opposed (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/28411).
- **Pew 2016:** "public transportation should be shut down throughout the entire country on the Sabbath": all Jews 35% favor / 63% oppose; Haredi 96, Dati 85, Masorti 44 favor (52 oppose), Hiloni 94 oppose. (Pew religion-politics chapter, URL above)
- Hiddush, Nov 2019, n=600 Jews (advocacy): 71% support; Likud voters 68% (ToI 2019-12-10, https://www.timesofisrael.com/most-israelis-support-shabbat-buses-including-likud-voters-traditional-jews/).
- Worth noticing: a right-wing majority (just over half) supports Shabbat buses outside religious areas. This is one of the clearest cases where opinion and the governing coalition's policy diverge.

### Separating religion and state, and halacha as law
- **Pew 2016:** "Religion should be kept separate from government policies" vs "government policies should promote religious beliefs and values": all Jews 60% separate / 36% promote; Hiloni 88% separate; Haredi 82% and Dati 80% promote; Masorti split ~50/50. Halacha as "official law of the land for Jews": all Jews 29% yes / 64% no; Haredi 86, Dati 69 yes; Masorti 57 no, Hiloni 90 no. (Pew religion-politics chapter)
- **Pew 2025 (published 2025-01-28; fieldwork believed 2024, not confirmed on page):** 33% of Israeli Jews favor halacha as official law, 60% oppose; Masorti 20% favor, Hiloni 4%; Haredi/Dati ~90% favor (as rendered — confirm whether separate). 82% of Jews say Israel can be both Jewish and democratic; 38% of Muslims. Israeli Muslims: 46% favor sharia as official law for Muslims, 45% oppose. (https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2025/01/28/what-role-should-religion-play-in-muslim-and-jewish-majority-countries/)
- Worth noticing: support for halacha as state law rose slightly, 29% → 33%, 2015 to 2024 (different surveys, same organization; check wording match).

### The Western Wall and kashrut (older data only)
- IDI 2019: 51.5% of Jews support egalitarian prayer in a separate area of the Wall; secular 78%; Haredi 98.5% opposed; national-religious 72.5% opposed. Kashrut: 63% favor ending Rabbinate monopoly (secular 89%, Haredi 95.5% opposed). (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/28411)
- No 2024–26 survey on the Kotel or kashrut found in this pass.

### How much it moves votes
- IDI 2019: religion-and-state was the top voting consideration for 15.5% of Jews, behind economy (36.7%) and security (36.2%). It was "of paramount importance" in vote choice for 67.5% of Haredim vs 17.5% of national-religious and 11% of secular. (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/28411) The asymmetry — intense minority, diffuse majority — is the key to why opinion and policy diverge. No 2026 equivalent found.

## 4. How an American reader is likely to misread it

1. **"Reform vs. Orthodox" is not the Israeli fault line.** Pew 2016: 50% of Israeli Jews say the stream they identify with is Orthodox, 41% none, Reform 3%, Conservative 2% — vs. US Jews Reform 35%, Conservative 18%, Orthodox 10%. Pew: "Conservative and Reform movements do not have a major presence in Israel." (https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/comparisons-between-jews-in-israel-and-the-u-s/) The Israeli categories are hiloni / masorti / dati / haredi — degrees of observance and community, not denominations.
2. **"Orthodox" means two things.** Half of Israeli Jews pick "Orthodox" as their stream, but only 22% are Haredi or Dati (Pew). A secular Israeli who never goes to synagogue still often considers the synagogue he doesn't attend to be Orthodox. And dati (religious-Zionist) and Haredi are politically distinct communities with separate parties.
3. **Secular Israelis are not secularists in the American sense.** Most want civil marriage as an option, yet a majority would still marry Orthodox given free choice (Hiddush/Smith 2025). The ask is choice, not a wall between synagogue and state.
4. **State-funded religion is the default, not an aberration.** The state pays rabbis, religious councils, rabbinical courts, and (in part) yeshivas and Haredi schools; Muslim, Christian and Druze courts are state bodies too. The Israeli argument is mostly about who controls and how much is paid, not whether the state should be involved. The First Amendment frame doesn't map.
5. **Diaspora issues (Kotel, conversion) rank low for Israeli voters.** In 2019 only 15.5% of Jews ranked religion-state first, and the Kotel had bare-majority support (51.5%). American Jewish attention to the Kotel is much higher than Israeli attention.
6. **Opinion majorities don't translate into policy.** ~60% support civil marriage, ~70% Shabbat buses — and the 25th Knesset moved the other way. The reason is coalition math: Haredi parties vote on these issues; most other voters don't.
7. **Arab citizens are part of this too.** Muslims, Christians and Druze also have no civil marriage; Arab respondents in 2026 opposed civil marriage 46–39 (JPPI). The issue is not only Jewish.

## Source disagreements
- **Civil marriage support:** JPPI 2026 59% (Jews) vs IDI 2019 59.5% vs Hiddush/Smith 2025 60% (broader question: civil, Conservative and Reform) vs Ne'emanei Torah Va'Avodah/Smith 2016 68% (n=500, advocacy; ToI 2016-08-10, https://www.timesofisrael.com/68-of-israelis-support-civil-marriage-poll-finds/). Pew 2016's 40% asks a different question (non-Orthodox rabbis).
- **Haredi support for civil marriage:** JPPI 2026 15% vs IDI 2019 4%. Different years and wording; small Haredi subsamples.
- **Shabbat transit:** Reichman 67–74% vs IDI 2019 60% vs Hiddush 2019 71% vs Pew 2015 63% opposed to a national shutdown. Question framing differs (exempting religious areas raises support).
- **Haredi schools and core curriculum:** Education Ministry (via JPost Sept 2026) — 498 of 517 network schools teach 96–100% of core; IDI/Finkelstein (2025) — at least half of Haredi schools don't comply, rising above 50% in grades 7–8; State Comptroller 2020 — 84% of Haredi secondary boys study no core subjects. These measure different school types and grades; the ministry figure covers only the two networks.
- **Western Wall vote, Feb 2026:** ToI calls it a preliminary reading; JC and Wikipedia say first reading. Same 56–47 vote.
- **Coalition funds, March 2026:** NIS 2.2bn of 5bn = 44% [arith]; summary also said 53.19%. Unresolved.
- **Self-identification:** CBS 2026 secular 39.6% vs Pew 2015 Hiloni 49% vs Pew 2024 45% (class doc 05). CBS uses 5 categories and age 20+.
