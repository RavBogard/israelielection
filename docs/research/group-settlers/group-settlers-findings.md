### 1b. Growth vs Israel proper
- Registry series: +2.2% in 2025 against 1.1% for Israel as a whole; +13.8% over five years (Katz PDF). CBS's September 2026 national figure is +1.2% (class doc 02).
- CBS series (Peace Now), annualized: 5.0%/yr 2000–10; 4.4% 2010–15; 3.5% 2015–18; 3.2% 2019; 2.2% 2020; 3.1% 2021; 4.3% 2022; 4.0% 2023 [arith].
- Yesha Council, May 2023: 2.2% in 2022, down from 3.3% in 2021; 10,755 Israelis moved in during 2022 (ToI 2023-05-12, https://www.timesofisrael.com/yesha-settler-umbrella-group-says-over-half-a-million-israelis-live-in-west-bank/).
- The two series disagree on 2022 (2.2% vs 4.3%). See "Source disagreements."
- **Where the growth is (Jan 2021 → Jan 2026, registry):** Ma'ale Adumim bloc +1.7%; Ma'ale Adumim city itself 40,943 → 40,800 (flat); Ariel–Elkana bloc +9.1%; Gush Etzion +7.5%; Modi'in Illit bloc +13.8%; Beitar Illit +15.7%; Binyamin +15.7%; South Hebron Hills +17.9%; Samaria +21.5%; Jordan Valley +25.8%; Eastern Etzion +35.0% (Katz PDF). About 41% of all five-year growth was in the Haredi towns [arith]. Worth noticing: the best-known secular suburb has stopped growing; the growth is Haredi cities and small ideological communities deep in the territory.

### 1d. The places (registry, 1 Jan 2026; Katz PDF)
- Haredi cities: Modi'in Illit 92,339; Beitar Illit 74,760 — the two largest "settlements," both a few km over the Green Line. Beitar Illit's mayor in 2017: "We came here due to the housing distress" (ToI 2017, link above).
- Suburbs ("quality of life"): Ma'ale Adumim 40,800; Givat Ze'ev 25,630; Ariel 22,273; Karnei Shomron 11,199; Oranit 10,212; Shaar Shomron 9,024; Alfei Menashe 8,631; Beit Aryeh 6,115; Har Adar 4,403. Together with Barkan and Ganei Modi'in, 144,291 people, 26.7% [arith].
- The four largest towns hold 230,172, 42.5% of all West Bank settlers [arith] (class doc 05 had 42.6%).
- Everything else — about 199,000 people in 100+ small communities — is mostly national-religious, plus 12,640 in the Jordan Valley's older, largely secular farming villages [arith].
- Naming trap: Modi'in Illit (West Bank, Haredi) is not Modi'in (inside the Green Line, secular). Use "Modi'in Illit" every time.

### 1e. Outposts and farms
- **Peace Now database (downloaded 2026-10-05): 415 outposts listed, of which 243 are "farm outposts."** Founding years: 1–18 a year through 2022; 32 in 2023; 61 in 2024; 92 in 2025; 79 so far in 2026 [arith from the spreadsheet; the list may include outposts later removed or legalized].
- Peace Now's 2025 summary report: 86 new outposts in 2025, 58 of them "agricultural farms"; 54 settlements approved; 27,941 housing units approved (Peace Now, 2026-02-03, https://peacenow.org.il/en/summary-of-2025-in-settlements). The EU repeats these and says the previous record was 26 outposts in 2023 (EEAS 2025). ToI's write-up of the same report gives 27,491 units (https://www.timesofisrael.com/2025-record-year-for-settlement-expansion-construction-and-planning-ngo-finds/) — a digit slip in one of them.
- Land: farm outposts "effectively control more than 1,070,000 dunams — approximately 18% of the West Bank" (Peace Now/Kerem Navot via EEAS 2025).
- The pro-settlement Katz report claims Smotrich "kickstarted 140 Jewish farms reclaiming 700,000 dunams" in two years and approved 69 new towns. Same phenomenon, opposite valuation.
- Legalization: 33 outposts legalized to date, at least 48 in process (Peace Now page, summary); 26 of the 54 settlements approved in 2025 were existing outposts (ToI 2026-02-25).

### 1f. Hilltop youth
- Government count, June 2026: **657 settler minors** in outposts identified for a NIS 50/day stipend program (Binyamin 225, Samaria 129, Jordan Valley 120, Hebron area 99, Gush Etzion 84); part of a NIS 120m program (ToI 2026-06-15, https://www.timesofisrael.com/government-to-provide-50-nis-daily-stipends-for-hundreds-of-hilltop-youth-report/).
- IDF/Shin Bet estimate, Sept 2026: **~300 Jewish extremists involved in attacks, ~70 "hardcore"**; about half live in illegal outposts (ToI 2026-09-09, https://www.timesofisrael.com/as-settler-violence-surges-some-ask-whether-israel-cant-crack-down-or-simply-wont/).
- Older: ~800 core, ~5,000 sympathizers (2009; Wikipedia, unattributed — do not use).
- Ambassador Yechiel Leiter, himself a resident of Eli: "It's a handful of a few hundred people who are staining an entire enterprise — and everyone is silent" (Ynet via ToI 2026-03-25, https://www.timesofisrael.com/israels-us-envoy-says-settler-violence-a-stain-costing-the-nation-friends/).

## 2. Voting pattern 2019–2022 (CEC locality results)

**Method.** Official CEC locality files for the 21st–25th Knessets (https://media21.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv through https://media25.bechirot.gov.il/files/expc.csv). West Bank settlements = CBS locality codes 3400–3899 (124–125 localities each election; Jerusalem is code 3000 and excluded). Grouping is ours:
- **Haredi cities (8):** Modi'in Illit, Beitar Illit, Emmanuel, Kochav Yaakov (the CEC merges it with Haredi Tel Tzion), Matityahu, Ma'ale Amos, Asfar/Metzad, Nachliel.
- **Suburbs (11–13):** Ma'ale Adumim, Ariel, Givat Ze'ev, Oranit, Alfei Menashe, Har Adar, Karnei Shomron, Shaar Shomron (Sha'arei Tikva and Etz Efraim before 2022), Ma'ale Shomron (before its merger), Beit Aryeh, Ganei Modi'in, Barkan.
- **Jordan Valley (24–25 small villages).**
- **Other settlements (~80):** everything else; overwhelmingly national-religious communities (Beit El, Ofra, Shiloh, Kedumim, Efrat, Kiryat Arba, Yitzhar...).
Caveats: soldiers and others who vote by double envelope are counted nationally, not by home locality, so settlement totals slightly undercount settlers; East Jerusalem neighborhoods cannot be separated from Jerusalem in this file; "suburbs" is a judgment call (Karnei Shomron votes 54% RZ–Otzma; Givat Ze'ev votes about 45% Haredi parties).

### 2c. How much of RZ/Otzma's vote comes from over the Green Line
- Settlement localities were **3.9% of Israel's eligible voters** in 2022 (266,958 of 6,788,804) and 4.2% of valid votes.
- **2022: 12.2% of RZ–Otzma's 516,470 votes came from settlement localities** (62,880 votes: 41,956 ideological settlements, 14,972 suburbs, 4,477 Haredi cities, 1,475 Jordan Valley). Jewish Home: 13.0%.
- 2021 RZ list: 17.9%. Apr 2019 URWP: 21.1%. Sep 2019 Otzma: 15.5%; Yamina 17.4%.
- For comparison, 2022: UTJ 14.3%, Shas 5.9%, Likud 3.4%, Yesh Atid 1.3%.
Worth noticing: RZ–Otzma depends on settlers about three times as much as their share of voters, but as the list grew from 5% to 11% nationally, the settler share of its vote fell from ~18–21% to 12%. The 2022 surge came mostly from inside the Green Line (class doc 11b: Ben-Gvir's "traditional Mizrahi voters from the periphery"). These figures exclude East Jerusalem neighborhoods and soldiers' double envelopes, so the true share is somewhat higher.

## 4. Lived texture

**The Yesha Council.** Umbrella of 25 elected local-council heads, founded in the 1980s as successor to Gush Emunim; headquarters in Jerusalem's Ramat Eshkol; chair Israel Ganz (also Binyamin Regional Council head) since May 2024 (Wikipedia, Yesha Council, read 2026-10-05). Stated aims include "to prevent the establishment of a Palestinian state between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea" (same). Ganz on the Oct 2025 bills: "The fact that it is the opposition that pushes for sovereignty is unacceptable" (ToI 2025-10-22, https://www.timesofisrael.com/2-west-bank-annexation-bills-get-initial-nod-with-mks-rebelling-against-pm-as-vance-visits/). On Bennett, June 2026: "Naftali Bennett declared this evening the establishment of a terror state minutes from his home in Ra'anana" (ToI 2026-06-23, https://www.timesofisrael.com/bennett-calls-to-dismantle-illegal-settlement-outposts-draws-right-wing-leaders-ire/).

**The Settlement Division.** A World Zionist Organization body funded by the state that plans and finances settlements. Haaretz documents (reported by ToI 2018-10-22) showed it gave mortgage loans to 26 illegal outposts over 20 years and backed settlers' land claims to banks "even when they didn't" own the land (https://www.timesofisrael.com/for-years-taxpayer-money-reportedly-used-to-fund-illegal-settlements/). No 2025–26 figures found; since 2023 the Settlements and National Missions Ministry (Orit Strock) "channels hundreds of millions of shekels to organisations involved in settlement expansion" (EEAS 2025).

**The Smotrich transfer.** Feb 23, 2023 coalition memorandum: civil powers in the West Bank go to a second minister in the Defense Ministry (Smotrich), who set up the Settlement Administration. May 29, 2024: Central Command head Yehuda Fox delegated Civil Administration powers (land, planning, building, registration, regional councils) and civilian Hillel Roth became deputy head. Smotrich, June 2024 recording: "We created a separate civilian system"; it sits inside the Defense Ministry "so they won't say we are doing annexation." (Sibling issue-west-bank research, ToI links there.) The EU: powers over planning, land, infrastructure, roads, nature reserves, archaeology and enforcement "transferred from the Civil Administration and the military chain of command to Finance Minister Smotrich and the Settlement Administration" (EEAS 2025, read). Feb 8, 2026 security cabinet: land registries opened, Jordanian-era ban on non-Muslim land purchase repealed, enforcement extended into Areas A/B (class doc 11b). A court froze a key step of the land-registration process on Sept 17, 2026 (ToI headline, https://www.timesofisrael.com/court-freezes-key-step-in-governments-controversial-west-bank-land-registration-process/ — headline only).

**The "sovereignty" push.** July 23, 2025 Knesset motion 71–13 (non-binding); Oct 22, 2025 preliminary readings: all-settlements bill 25–24, Ma'ale Adumim bill 32–9; Netanyahu opposed both; Trump: "Israel would lose all of its support from the United States if that happened" (Wikipedia summary of cited reporting; sibling file has CBS quote "I will not allow Israel to annex the West Bank"). Smotrich, Sept 27, 2026 (Ynet podcast via ToI): "I think we need to go to war in Judea and Samaria, to do there what we did in Gaza, to dismantle the Palestinian Authority" (https://www.timesofisrael.com/smotrich-israel-must-go-to-war-in-west-bank-do-there-what-we-did-in-gaza/). At the Sept 27 inauguration of Makeda, Karmei Yehudah, Meged and Ma'ale Einav: "We are continuing the settlement revolution" (ToI 2026-09-28).

**Settler violence and the state's response.**
- UN OCHA attacks causing casualties or damage: 1,189 (2023), 1,420 (2024), 1,828 (2025), with 9 Palestinians killed and 838 injured by settlers in 2025 (EEAS 2025, read). IDF "nationalist crime" incidents: 682 (2024) → 867 (2025); +63% in H1 2026 vs H1 2025 (ToI 2026-07-21 and 2026-09-09).
- Yesh Din: 93.6% of investigation files 2005–2025 closed without indictment; 62.6% of 2026 incidents in Areas A/B (ToI 2026-09-09).
- Defense Minister Katz: "I canceled administrative detention orders against settlers because it's not terror activity" (ToI 2026-09-09).
- IDF chief Eyal Zamir, March 19, 2026: "It is unacceptable that during a multi-front war, the IDF is forced to also contend with a threatening minority from within"; the perpetrators are "rioters who do not represent the settlements" (ToI, https://www.timesofisrael.com/idf-chief-settler-violence-unacceptable-results-in-extraordinary-strategic-damage/, read).
- Cabinet quietly approved an anti-violence plan in March 2026; Netanyahu called the phenomenon a "metastasizing cancer"; Shin Bet head David Zini wanted it made public and the cabinet refused (ToI 2026-07-21, Channel 13 account).
- Rosh Hashanah 2026: five attacks reported; "There were no reports of arrests" (ToI 2026-09-13).
- Abroad: UK sanctions package on settlements announced Sept 8, 2026, with 12 other Western states planning similar steps; Foreign Minister Sa'ar called it "antisemitic" (ToI 2026-09-15).

**Voices (pages opened).**
- Shlomo Ne'eman, Gush Etzion Regional Council head, 2023: "Unfortunately, even after we've crossed half a million residents, we are still prisoners of a military administration that does not correspond to the reality on the ground." (ToI 2023-05-12)
- Rabbi Yaakov Meidan, head of Yeshivat Har Etzion (Alon Shvut): "There are extreme and terrible crimes committed by Jewish youth, and we must fulfill the mitzvah of expelling the evil from our midst." (ToI 2026-07-31)

## Framing choices for Daniel (not resolved here)
1. "Settlers" vs "residents of Judea and Samaria" (Yesha's term) vs "West Bank Israelis."
2. "West Bank" vs "Judea and Samaria" vs "occupied West Bank" — the CEC, CBS and Israeli law use "Judea and Samaria"; the EU, UN and US press use "occupied West Bank."
3. "Occupied" vs "disputed" for the territory's status.
4. Whether East Jerusalem neighborhoods (Ramot, Gilo, Pisgat Ze'ev) count as settlements — changes the headline number from ~540,000 to ~750,000+.
5. Whether Haredi cities belong on a "settlers" page at all; most residents chose cheap housing, not ideology.
6. "Quality-of-life settlers" — a common label, but it implies the others are not; alternative: "suburban."
7. "Outposts" (illegal under Israeli law) vs "farms" vs "young settlements" (settler term).
8. "Settler violence" vs "nationalist crime" (IDF) vs "Jewish terror" (Shin Bet 2023, opposition).
9. "Sovereignty" vs "annexation."
10. "Hilltop youth" — Israeli coinage; some read it as softening.
