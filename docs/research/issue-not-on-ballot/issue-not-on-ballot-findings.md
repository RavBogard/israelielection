# What is not on the ballot: a Palestinian state and the peace process — research record

Researched 2026-10-04/05. Builds on class docs 11a–11d (party profiles, 2026-10-04) and 02/05/06. Every figure below carries its source URL. "Opened" means the page or PDF was read directly in this session; "secondhand" means the figure came through another page and is marked `verified: false` in the JSON.

Note on terms used in this record: "two-state solution" and "Palestinian state" are used as each source used them. Survey questions differ a great deal (see "Source disagreements" and the framing list in the brief). Pew asks whether a Palestinian state and Israel *can* coexist peacefully (a feasibility question). The Peace Index, INSS and the Pulse ask whether people *support* an outcome (a preference question). These should not be charted as one series.

## 1. What is at stake

### How the two-state idea left Israeli electoral politics

- **Oslo era.** Likud's own record is mixed: the IDI party page notes Likud "continued the Oslo Accords and withdrew from most of Hebron, and led the unilateral disengagement plan" (IDI, Likud party page, https://en.idi.org.il/israeli-elections-and-parties/parties/likud/, opened). It also says that from 2009 to 2023 the party "advocated a 'conflict management' approach … and de facto recognized Hamas rule in Gaza" (same page).
- **Camp David and the Second Intifada.** Camp David summit July 11–25, 2000, failed; Taba talks January 21–27, 2001, failed. The Second Intifada ran from September 28, 2000, to February 8, 2005. About 1,053 Israelis and about 4,745 Palestinians were killed by the other side through April 2008. Sharon (Likud) beat Barak (Labor) in February 2001 (Wikipedia, Second Intifada, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intifada, opened; Wikipedia figures cite B'Tselem — use B'Tselem directly if the page cites casualty totals).
- **2005 disengagement.** Israel removed all 21 Gaza settlements and 4 in the northern West Bank; more than 8,000 settlers evacuated, completed September 12, 2005. Hamas won the January 2006 Palestinian legislative election and took control of Gaza in June 2007 (Wikipedia, Israeli disengagement from Gaza, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_disengagement_from_Gaza, opened). For many Israeli voters this is the reference point for "we withdrew and got rockets"; the page should state this as the common Israeli reading, not as the only reading.
- **The left's collapse in the Knesset.** Labor peaked at 49 seats in 1969 and won 4 in 2022; Meretz won 12 in 1992 and 0 in 2022, its first time out of the Knesset (class doc 11c, Democrats section A). The two merged as The Democrats (Golan).
- **Abraham Accords.** Signed September 15, 2020 (Israel, UAE, Bahrain); Morocco and Sudan followed; Kazakhstan (November 2025) and Somaliland (December 2025) added. The UAE deal came with Israel suspending its 2020 annexation plan (Wikipedia, Abraham Accords, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords, opened). Worth noticing: the accords showed Israelis normalization with Arab states could come without a Palestinian state — this is part of why the issue faded.
- **Saudi Arabia.** In September 2024 Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman said "The Kingdom will not establish diplomatic relations with Israel without that," meaning an independent Palestinian state with East Jerusalem as capital (Wikipedia, Israel–Saudi Arabia relations, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Saudi_Arabia_relations, opened; secondhand for the quote — primary is the Saudi Press Agency speech to the Shura Council). A 2025–26 Saudi statement was not found this session.
- **October 7 and after.** Pew's open-ended answers record Jewish Israelis citing October 7 as the reason they no longer think coexistence is possible (Pew, Sept 15, 2026, https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/09/15/many-israelis-and-palestinians-say-they-cannot-coexist-peacefully-and-explain-why/, opened; ToI summary https://www.timesofisrael.com/pew-poll-finds-majority-of-israelis-and-palestinian-say-two-state-coexistence-impossible/, opened).

### The Knesset votes

- **July 17, 2024 (18 July by some outlets' dating).** Knesset passed 68–9 a resolution: "The Knesset of Israel firmly opposes the establishment of a Palestinian state west of Jordan. The establishment of a Palestinian state in the heart of the Land of Israel will pose an existential danger to the State of Israel and its citizens..." It rejected statehood "even as part of a negotiated settlement," unlike a February 2024 resolution that only rejected unilateral recognition. Sponsored by coalition parties with members of Gantz's National Unity; the 9 votes against were Ra'am and Hadash–Ta'al; Yesh Atid and Labor left the chamber (Times of Israel, https://www.timesofisrael.com/knesset-votes-overwhelmingly-against-palestinian-statehood-days-before-pms-us-trip/, opened). Worth noticing: 68 is more than the coalition; centrists (Gantz) voted for it.
- **July 23, 2025.** Non-binding declaration in favor of applying sovereignty to "Judea, Samaria and the Jordan Valley," passed 71–13. Supporters: coalition plus UTJ, Noam, Yisrael Beiteinu and MK Idan Roll; National Unity and Yesh Atid boycotted (Ynet, https://www.ynetnews.com/article/bkxfw9algl, opened).
- **October 22, 2025.** Preliminary reading of Avi Maoz's (Noam) bill annexing all West Bank settlements passed 25–24; Otzma, RZ, UTJ's Agudah faction and Likud's Yuli Edelstein voted for; the rest of Likud stayed out. Lieberman's Ma'ale Adumim bill passed 32–9 (ToI liveblog, https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/rebelling-against-pm-mks-pass-preliminary-reading-of-west-bank-annexation-bill/, opened). Vice President Vance called it "a very stupid political stunt"; Trump told Time (Oct 15): "It won't happen because I gave my word to the Arab countries… Israel would lose all of its support from the United States if that happened" (ToI, https://www.timesofisrael.com/israel-would-lose-all-support-from-the-us-if-it-annexes-west-bank-trump-warns/, opened).

### The recognition wave and Israel's reaction

- As of late September 2025, 157 of 193 UN member states recognized Palestine (Al Jazeera, https://aljazeera.com/news/2025/9/23/which-are-the-150-countries-that-have-recognised-palestine-as-of-2025, opened; same count on Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine, opened). UK, Canada, Australia and Portugal recognized September 21, 2025; France, Luxembourg, Malta, Monaco, Andorra and Belgium at the UN General Assembly September 22–23. Earlier: Ireland, Norway, Spain (May 28, 2024). Al Jazeera counts 20 recognitions since October 2023. The United States, Germany, Italy and Japan had not recognized as of the sources read.
- Belgium attached conditions (last hostage released; Hamas out of governance); on July 6, 2026, Prime Minister De Wever told his government to begin the process (Wikipedia, same page; secondhand — find a Belgian government or Reuters source before publishing).
- Israeli reaction cut across the Zionist spectrum. Netanyahu: "there will be no Palestinian state" (House of Commons Library briefing, Nov 19, 2025, https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10323/, opened; secondhand for the quote). Lapid: "a harmful step, and a reward for terror." Golan: "destructive," "extremely damaging." Herzog: "will not help a single Palestinian." Ben-Gvir: "a prize for the murderous terrorists" (Times of Israel, Sept 22, 2025, https://www.timesofisrael.com/israel-unites-against-hollow-palestine-recognition-but-pm-could-go-too-far/, opened). Worth noticing: the recognition wave did not split Israeli politics; it united the Zionist parties against it.
- Government steps the class docs record after the wave: E1 ~3,400 units approved Aug 20, 2025 (Smotrich: "practically erases the two-state delusion"); Feb 8, 2026 security cabinet measures on land registries and enforcement in Areas A/B (ToI, via class doc 11b; not re-opened). The West Bank issue page covers these; this page should link there rather than repeat.

### Gaza "day after" — where statehood language now lives

- Trump's 20-point Gaza plan (announced Sept 29, 2025; signed Oct 9, 2025), point 20: "While Gaza re-development advances and when the PA reform program is faithfully carried out, the conditions may finally be in place for a credible pathway to Palestinian self-determination and statehood." UN Security Council Resolution 2803, Nov 17, 2025. Board of Peace announced Jan 17, 2026; by May 2026 phase two was stalled over Hamas disarmament (Wikipedia, Gaza peace plan, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_peace_plan, opened; secondhand — the plan text is on whitehouse.gov and should be cited directly).
- Netanyahu, Aug 9, 2026, rejecting a US 15-point document: "as long as I am prime minister, no Palestinian state will arise" (Al Jazeera via class doc 11a; not re-opened).

### What the parties talk about instead

- IDI asked voters (Aug 31–Sept 3, 2026; 605 Jews, 150 Arabs) to name their first and second most important consideration. Jews: foreign affairs and security 39%, economy and cost of living 38%, conscription 27%, religion and state 26%, party leader 23%, judicial reform 18%. Arabs: crime in Arab society 53.5%, economy 46%. 68% of Jews and 32% of Arabs say October 7 is a central factor in their vote (IDI, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177, opened). **The Palestinian issue was not offered as a separate option.** It may sit inside "foreign affairs and security." This is itself evidence for the page's thesis, but also a limit on it: the survey cannot show the issue is unimportant if it never asked.
- Campaign framing: Netanyahu's Sept 18, 2026 video: "Do you want a Palestinian state? (or) do you want a Jewish state?" (JPost, https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-910068, opened). Worth noticing: the one place a Palestinian state appears in the campaign is as an accusation against the center-left, which denies it.

## 2. What Israelis think

### Pew: "can Israel and an independent Palestinian state coexist peacefully?" (feasibility)

Question: "Do you think a way can be found for Israel and an independent Palestinian state to coexist peacefully with each other?" Face-to-face. "It depends" volunteered.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 50 | 38 | 46 | 42 | 74 |
| 2014 | 40 | 45 | 37 | 50 | 64 |
| 2017 | 44 | 37 | 40 | 43 | 61 |
| 2023 | 35 | 46 | 32 | 48 | 41 |
| 2024 | 26 | 50 | ~20 (chart read, uncertain) | 55 | 49 |
| 2025 | 21 | 50 | 16 | 58 | 40 |
| 2026 | 17 | 56 | 16 | 64 | 23 |

Sources: Pew report PDF Sept 15, 2026, chart "Jewish and Arab Israelis have lost confidence in the two-state solution" (https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/09/pg_2026.09.15_coexistence_report.pdf, opened); 2026 fieldwork Israel April 5–May 6 per JPost / March 30–May 6 per Pew page for both samples, n=1,001 Israel (https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/09/15/many-israelis-and-palestinians-say-they-cannot-coexist-peacefully-and-explain-why/, opened). 2025 fieldwork Feb 5–March 11, 2025, n=998 face-to-face (https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/06/pg_2025.06.03_israel_report.pdf, opened). The 2023 point is spring 2023, before October 7.

- 2026: Arab citizens — 46% declined to answer; of the rest, 23% can, 26% cannot. Pew says Jewish views are "largely unchanged" from 2025 and the overall drop from 21% to 17% is "not changed significantly." It is the first time more than half (56%) say "cannot."
- 2025 breakdowns (Pew 2025 report, opened): left 54%, center 29%, right 7% say "can." Jewish religious groups: Hilonim (secular) 25%, Masortim (traditional) 11%, Haredim and Datiim (combined) 9%.
- 2025 Pew, related: 70% call the status of Jerusalem a major obstacle, 52% call settlements a major obstacle; 44% say settlement building helps Israel's security vs 35% hurts. On Gaza after the war: 33% say Israel should govern (40% in 2024), 16% let Gazans decide, 10% PA without Abbas, 6% PA with Abbas, 2% UN, 1% Hamas.

### Peace Index (Tel Aviv University): support for four outcomes (preference)

Question: "Regarding each of the following solutions to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, please state to what extent you support or oppose each of them." Each option rated separately, so shares do not sum to 100. Online, iPanel. Conducted by Dr. Nimrod Rosler and Dr. Alon Yakter.

| Option (Peace Index wording) | Jews Sept 2023 | Jews Nov 2025 | Jews Mar 2026 | Arabs Sept 2023 | Arabs Mar 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "The creation of an independent Palestinian state alongside the State of Israel" | 38 | 20 | 23 (22.7) | 69 | 71 |
| "Annexation of the occupied territories and the creation of one state under Israeli rule with limited rights for Palestinians" | 30 | 38 | 38 (37.7) | 12 | 12 |
| "Continuing the existing situation" | 28 | 30 | 31 (31.1) | 15 | 12 |
| "A binational state between the Jordan and the Mediterranean Sea with full and equal rights for Jews and Palestinians" | 11 | 9 | 8 (7.5) | 43 | 51 |

- Sept 2023: fieldwork Aug 29–Sept 5, 2023, n=612 (https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/2023-09.pdf, opened; bar values read from chart). Five weeks before October 7.
- Nov 2025: fieldwork Nov 6–13, 2025, n=610 (https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/2025-11.pdf, opened). Two-state "returns to its lowest recorded level (20%)"; annexation "the most popular option" at 38%, though half of Jews oppose it. Status quo back to pre-Oct 7 levels.
- Mar 2026: fieldwork March 23–28, 2026, n=607 (504 Jews, 103 Arabs), ±4.2% (press release https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/2026-03.pdf and findings https://en-social-sciences.tau.ac.il/sites/socsci-english.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/social/peaceindex/2026-03-findings.pdf, both opened). Jews on a Palestinian state: 5.0% very much support, 17.7% moderately, 13.3% moderately oppose, 56.5% very much oppose, 7.5% don't know. Arabs: 41.7% very much, 29.1% moderately.
- Mar 2026 by Jewish political camp (opinion-holders): two-state right 3%, moderate right 14%, center 44%, left 86%; annexation right 64%, moderate right 49%, center 23%, left 3%. Coalition voters: 8% two-state, 52% annexation; opposition voters: 49% two-state, 21% annexation.
- Support for negotiations with the PA among Jews: 47% in Sept 2023; 30% Nov 2025; 26% Mar 2026. Arabs 78% → 67%. Only 13% of Jews think negotiations would lead to peace. 48% of Jews think the status quo is the most likely outcome; 78% say continuing the conflict harms Israel.
- Worth noticing: the most likely outcome in Jewish eyes (status quo) is not the most supported one, and the most supported one among Jews (annexation with limited rights) is opposed by about half of them.

### INSS National Security Index, June 2026 (single choice)

Fieldwork June 16–22, 2026; n=957 (805 Hebrew, 152 Arabic); ±3.17%. All Israelis: separation from the Palestinians 27%, two-state solution 25%, "full annexation without granting civil rights" 25%, unsure 10%. Jews: separation 32%, annexation 31%. Arabs: two states 57%, one binational state 21%. Coalition voters: 50% annexation; opposition voters: 40% separation, 36% two states. Expanding the Abraham Accords in a framework that includes a Palestinian state: 42% support, 41% oppose; Jews 36%, Arabs 66%; opposition voters 61%, coalition 20% (https://www.inss.org.il/publication/inss-survey-june-2026/, opened).

- Worth noticing: when "separation" is offered as its own option (it is the Democrats' word), it is the plurality choice among Jews. The two-state number depends heavily on whether a softer "separation" option is on the list.

### Long-run series, 1994–2018 (secondhand)

Jewish Virtual Library reproduces a chart labeled as IDI's "Support for the two-state solution among Jewish and Arab Israelis, 1994–2018": 1994 47.2%, 1995 36.9%, 1997 51.0%, 1999 52.3%, 2000 49.8%, 2002 57.5%, 2003 65.0%, 2006 54.5%, 2007 70.3%, 2008 68.8%, 2009 62.6%, 2012 61.6%, 2015 52.4%, 2017 46.9%, 2018 46.8% (https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/israeli-polls-regarding-peace-with-the-palestinians, opened; original IDI source not found; verified: false). INSS (Zipi Israeli, April 2017) says support was "approximately 70 percent" among Jews 2003–2013 and 59% in Nov–Dec 2016 (https://www.inss.org.il/publication/national-security-index-public-support-arrangement-based-two-state-solution/, opened). Worth noticing: support peaked in the mid-2000s, after the Second Intifada and around disengagement — not in the Oslo years. Do not chart the 1994–2018 series until IDI's original is found.

### Palestinian citizens of Israel

Every source puts Arab citizens far above Jews on two states: Peace Index 71% (Mar 2026), INSS 57% (June 2026), Pulse 72% (July 2024, secondhand). Pew 2026 is the outlier: 23% "can," but 46% did not answer — up from earlier years. Arab citizens' first voting concern is crime (53.5%, IDI Sept 2026), not the Palestinian question. Arab binational-state support: 43% (Sept 2023) → 59% (Nov 2025) → 51% (Mar 2026) in the Peace Index.

### What Israelis say they want instead

Among Jews the post-Oct 7 alternatives are, roughly in order: annexation with limited rights (Peace Index 38%; Pulse 42% in 2024; INSS 31%), separation without a state (INSS 32%), the status quo (Peace Index 31%; also the most expected outcome). A binational state with equal rights stays under 10% among Jews. In Gaza the plurality wants Israeli governance (Pew 2025: 33%).

## 4. How an American reader is likely to misread it

1. **Assuming the election is a referendum on two states.** No Zionist list running on Oct 27 campaigns for a Palestinian state. A Netanyahu loss would not mean a peace process; a Yashar- or Bennett-led government has said a state is "not on the agenda" or opposed.
2. **Assuming "left" and "right" mean what they do in the US.** In Israel left/right is mostly about the Palestinians and territory; Bennett and Eisenkot are anti-Netanyahu but not "left" on this axis.
3. **Assuming young Israelis are more dovish.** The Pulse found 18–34-year-old Jews least supportive (20% in 2022 vs 47% for 55+).
4. **The American Jewish gap.** Pew 2020 (fieldwork Nov 2019–June 2020): 63% of US Jews said a way can be found for Israel and an independent Palestinian state to coexist peacefully (https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/05/11/u-s-jews-connections-with-and-attitudes-toward-israel/, opened); Orthodox Jews mostly said no; Democrats about 7 in 10 yes. AJC 2019 (April 10–May 7, 2019, n=1,006): 64% of American Jews favored "a two-state solution through the establishment of a demilitarized Palestinian state on the West Bank," 26% opposed (https://www.ajc.org/news/survey2019, opened). Pew Feb 13–25, 2024 (1,941 Jews): 46% of US Jews call two states the best possible outcome; about 22% one country under an Israeli government (https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2024/03/21/majority-in-u-s-say-israel-has-valid-reasons-for-fighting-fewer-say-the-same-about-hamas/, opened; 22% read from chart). Pew May 4–17, 2026: 83% of US Jews view the Israeli people favorably, 47% the Israeli government (https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/07/09/americans-views-of-israelis-have-grown-increasingly-negative-but-views-of-palestinians-have-held-fairly-steady/, opened). Compare Israeli Jews: 16% "can coexist" (Pew 2026) and 23% support (Peace Index 2026).
5. **Reading "no Palestinian state" as "support for annexation."** Among Jews, opposition to a state is far larger than support for any single alternative; status quo and separation draw large shares, and many Israelis expect the status quo whatever they prefer.

## Source disagreements

- **Pew 2026 vs ToI on the trend.** Pew: 17% in 2026 is "not changed significantly" from 21% in 2025. ToI calls 17% "the lowest support level." Use Pew's wording.
- **Pew 2026 fieldwork dates.** Pew page: March 30–May 6 (covering both samples; Israel from April). JPost: Israel April 5–May 6. Not a real conflict; cite Pew.
- **Arab citizens' two-state view.** Pew 2026 23% "can" (46% no answer) vs Peace Index 71% support (Mar 2026), INSS 57% (June 2026), Pulse 72% (2024). Different question types (feasibility vs preference) and very different non-response.
- **Two-state support among Jews, 2024–26:** Pulse 21% (July 2024); Peace Index 20–23% (Nov 2025–Mar 2026); INSS 25% overall (June 2026, Jewish figure not given); Pew 16% "can" (2026). Consistent within a band of about 16–25%, with question differences.
- **Annexation figure among Jews:** Peace Index 38% ("limited rights"); Pulse 42% ("without equal rights"); INSS 31% ("without granting civil rights"), with separation offered alongside. The share falls when "separation" is an option.
- **Pulse confederation among Jews, July 2024:** PSR 12% vs ToI 20%. Use PSR.
- **Pulse Arab two-state 2024:** ToI 72%; PSR page as read gives 69% for the detailed package, not the general question. Unresolved.
- **Knesset vote date:** ToI dates the vote July 17, 2024; Al Jazeera's URL is July 18. Vote was late on the 17th (Israel time) per ToI.
- **Recognition list in Wikipedia** merges 2024 and 2025 dates in one passage; use Al Jazeera's 2025 list.
