## 1. What is at stake

### The short version
Israel drafts most Jewish men at 18 (32 months) and most Jewish women (24 months). Druze and Circassian men are drafted too. Arab citizens who are not Druze are not drafted. Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) men have been able, for most of the state's history, to defer service year after year while studying full-time in yeshiva, until the deferral became a de facto exemption. The fight is over whether that arrangement continues, in what form, and who enforces it. Since October 7, 2023, it has stopped being an argument about principle and become an argument about manpower: the army says it is short of soldiers, and reservists have carried the gap.

### Legal history, 1948–2026
- **1948:** Ben-Gurion agreed with Agudat Yisrael's Yitzhak-Meir Levin to exempt full-time yeshiva students under the principle of *Torato umanuto* ("his Torah is his trade"). About 400 students a year were covered at first. [Wikipedia, Haredi conscription in Israel, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haredi_conscription_in_Israel — secondary source]
- **1977:** After Likud's first win, the Likud–Agudat Yisrael coalition deal lifted the cap on the number of exemptions. [same]
- **1998:** The High Court ruled that the defense minister lacked authority to grant sweeping exemptions on his own; the Knesset had to legislate. [IDI, Dagan/Cohen/Fuchs, 2026-01-21, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/63168]
- **2002: the Tal Law.** Allowed yeshiva students to defer to 23, then take a "decision year" and choose a shortened service or work. By 2005, of 41,450 covered students, 1,115 took the decision year and 31 later enlisted (figure attributed to Justice Minister Tzipi Livni). [Wikipedia, above — secondary] **2012:** the High Court struck the Tal Law down as ineffective. [IDI 63168]
- **2014:** Yesh Atid–driven amendment set Haredi enlistment targets with criminal sanctions if missed. **2015:** the next coalition (with Haredi parties back in) delayed the sanctions. **September 12, 2017:** the High Court struck down the 2014–2015 amendments as discriminatory and gave the Knesset a year. [IDI 63168; Wikipedia]
- **2018–2023:** Repeated extensions. Lieberman's 2018 bill stalled; his refusal to join a Netanyahu government in 2019 over the draft set off five elections in under four years (class doc 02). The last legal deferral expired **June 30, 2023**. [IDI 63168]
- **April 1, 2024:** The High Court ordered a halt to state funding for yeshivas whose students were draft-eligible. About 1,257 yeshivas with 49,485 students faced cuts. [ToI 2024-04-01, https://www.timesofisrael.com/the-high-courts-yeshiva-funding-ruling-goes-into-effect-today-what-does-it-mean/]
- **June 25, 2024:** A unanimous nine-justice panel ruled there is "no legal basis for avoiding the recruitment of yeshiva students" and that the state must begin drafting them, gradually if necessary, and may not fund yeshivas of draft-eligible students. The court wrote: "In these days, in the midst of a severe war, the burden of inequality is more acute than ever." The ruling cited 63,000 eligible men. [ToI 2024-06-25, https://www.timesofisrael.com/in-historic-ruling-high-court-says-government-must-begin-drafting-haredi-men-into-idf/]
- **July 2024 onward:** The IDF began sending draft orders. By April 2025: 10,000 orders since July 2024, 205 enlisted from those orders, 1,096 arrest warrants (IDF Personnel Directorate to the Knesset). [ToI liveblog 2025-04-02, https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/just-2-of-haredi-call-ups-have-enlisted-since-high-court-struck-down-their-exemptions/]
- **January 2025:** Defense Minister Katz presented an outline with targets rising to 50% of the cohort by 2032. [Wikipedia — secondary]
- **July 14–15, 2025:** Both UTJ factions quit the government over the stalled draft law; Shas ministers left soon after (staying in the coalition). [Wikipedia, 2026 Israeli legislative election, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Israeli_legislative_election — secondary]
- **July 23, 2025:** Likud removed Yuli Edelstein as chair of the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee (29–4 in the Likud faction) and installed Boaz Bismuth. Edelstein's bill aimed at about 50% of each Haredi cohort within five years, with sanctions. Lapid: "Bismuth was elected to promote evasion, and this is spitting in the face of fighters." [ToI 2025-07-23, https://www.timesofisrael.com/amid-haredi-draft-controversy-likud-votes-for-mk-boaz-bismuth-to-head-key-knesset-panel/]
- **October 31 / November 27, 2025: the Bismuth bill.** Targets of 8,160 / 6,840 / 7,920 / 8,500 in years 1–4, then "no less than 50 percent of the annual enlistment cohort" from Haredi institutions; full-time yeshiva students keep yearly deferrals to 26, when sanctions lapse; sanctions (driver's licenses, travel, scholarships, tax credits) apply only ages 23–26; civilian service capped at 10% of recruits. A defense official called it "essentially a bluff" leaving "the exemption mechanism in the hands of the rabbis." [ToI 2025-11-27, https://www.timesofisrael.com/bismuth-presents-bill-exempting-yeshiva-students-from-idf-draft-loosening-sanctions/] IDI's reading of the October outline: year-one target 4,800, about 5% of a draft-eligible pool of ~100,000; "does not establish an equal obligation for conscription." [IDI, Ravitsky Tur-Paz, 2025-10-31, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61994]
- **November 19, 2025:** An expanded High Court panel ordered the government to adopt an "effective enforcement policy" within 45 days, including civil-economic sanctions and criminal proceedings. The government missed the January 4, 2026 deadline; a contempt motion followed. The State Attorney's Office told the court: "This conduct poses real danger to democratic rule in the State of Israel, including the principle of separation of powers." [IDI 63168]
- **March 2026:** The bill was shelved during the US-Israeli war with Iran. [VIN News 2026-05-17, https://vinnews.com/2026/05/17/haredi-parties-push-knesset-dissolution-as-draft-exemption-bill-returns-to-committee/]
- **April 26, 2026:** The High Court ordered personal sanctions on individual draft evaders: no "target price" discounted housing (about NIS 500,000 per apartment), no daycare and after-school subsidies, no municipal-tax or transit discounts. IDI: "the responsibility for non-enlistment no longer rests solely on Haredi institutions, but also on the draft evaders themselves." [IDI 2026-04-26, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/64062]
- **May 2026:** Rabbi Dov Landau (Degel HaTorah): "We no longer have trust in Netanyahu" (class doc 11a, not reopened); he urged MKs "not to get drawn into political games" and to dissolve the Knesset. [VIN News 2026-05-17]
- **June 2, 2026:** The dissolution bill passed first reading 106–0 after Netanyahu told the Haredi parties the bill lacked votes. [JPost 2026-06-02, https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-898048]
- **July 2026:** The coalition passed a law shielding yeshiva students from arrest and proceedings for draft evasion (58–54, July 14; class doc 11b, ToI, not reopened). It also kept regular service at 32 months (43–12), rejecting the IDF's request for 36. The Knesset dissolved July 16–17; election set for October 27. [Ynet 2026-07-17, https://www.ynetnews.com/article/h1nremv4gl; JNS 2026-07-17, https://www.jns.org/news/israel-news/knesset-votes-to-dissolve-formally-sets-oct-27-election-date]
- **September 3, 2026:** The High Court unanimously voided the arrest-ban law for procedural defects; eight of nine also held it unconstitutional in substance (Justice Mintz did not join that part). Sohlberg: "Knesset members did not heed the good advice they received." The ruling cited about 73,664 Haredi draft evaders and 219 arrests (about 0.3%). [JPost 2026-09-03, http://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-907553]
- **September 27–29, 2026:** Rallies in Bnei Brak and Jerusalem welcomed about 160 men released from military prison; gift bags read "The welcoming ceremony for prisoners of the Torah who had the privilege of sitting in prison." TV crews were attacked in Bnei Brak. [Ynet 2026-09-29, https://ynetnews.com/article/bydixytqzl]

**On the dissolution:** The draft fight drove the coalition apart, but the 25th Knesset was close to its full term anyway; Wikipedia notes the dissolution efforts "would have only moved the election date forward by a few weeks." [Wikipedia 2026 election] Class doc 02 says the 25th Knesset served a full term, the first since 1988. The page should say the draft *shaped* the end of the government and the campaign, not that it forced an early election by months.

### Numbers

**Draft-age Haredi men (sources disagree because they count different things):**
- 63,000 yeshiva students subject to the draft (High Court, June 2024). [ToI 2024-06-25]
- ~66,000 eligible Haredi men 18–24 not enlisted (IDF, April 2025). [ToI 2025-04-02]
- ~80,000 Haredi men 18–24 eligible and not enlisted (ToI, July 2025 and July 2026). [ToI 2025-07-23; ToI 2026-07-29, https://www.timesofisrael.com/idf-chief-tells-haredi-commanders-army-must-grow-as-data-shows-uptick-in-enlistment/]
- ~100,000 draft-eligible pool (IDI, Oct 2025). [IDI 61994]
- 73,664 estimated Haredi draft evaders (High Court, Sept 2026). [JPost 907553]
- Annual Haredi male cohort reaching 18: ~14,000. [JPost 2026-07-29, https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-904035; JPost article-885107]
- Haredim are 23.5% of all Israelis reaching draft eligibility (2025), projected 25% in 2030, 33% in 2040, 40% in 2050. If patterns hold, only 45% of 18-year-old men would serve by 2050. [IDI, Malach/Yakir/Portal, "Haredim in Israel 2050," https://en.idi.org.il/articles/63385]

[ToI liveblog 2026-05-31, https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/ag-over-1800-haredi-men-enlisted-to-idf-in-six-month-period-in-2025-far-short-of-security-needs/]

**Draft orders vs. response (a different, much smaller number):**
- 2024 draft year: 24,000 initial orders; 2,738 attended; 798 enlisted plus 432 with dates set. [IDI 63168]
- By early June 2025: 19,000 summonses; 996 reported (~5%); 232 conscripted (1.2%). [IDI, Ravitsky Tur-Paz, 2025-06-25, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/59892]
- 2025 draft year: 53,741 notices; 15,938 attendance dates passed; 701 reported. [IDI 63168]

The gap matters: most Haredi enlistees are men already outside full-time yeshiva who volunteer into Haredi tracks. The men who receive draft orders mostly do not come.

**Money:**
- Yeshiva/kollel sector budget (2022): NIS 5.3 billion, of which about NIS 1 billion from the state. [ToI 2024-04-01]
- Monthly stipend for married kollel students: NIS 700, with a coalition deal (Nov 2022) to raise it to NIS 1,300, costing NIS 1.5 billion more a year. [ToI 2022-11-11, https://www.timesofisrael.com/likud-religious-allies-said-to-agree-on-hiking-stipends-for-yeshiva-students/] Current (2026) amount not found.
- After the 2024 ruling: NIS 480 million a year in yeshiva funding revoked; about NIS 9,500 a year lost per kollel student. [IDI 59892]
- Housing: at least 6,500 Haredi households had received "target price" benefits totaling NIS 3.25 billion. [IDI 64062]
- IDI: personal sanctions moved enlistment "from 1,700 to 2,800 annually." [IDI 64062]

**The burden on everyone else:**
- Share of men 21–44 in reserve service in an average month: Oct–Dec 2023 secular 11.2%, traditional 11.2%, religious 15.2%; 2025 religious 4.3%, traditional 2.6%, secular 1.7%, Haredi 0.3%. [IDI, Finkelstein/Portal, 2026-08-20, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65427]
- Average reserve duty: 94 days in 2024, 78 in 2025; 37% of reservists are parents (IDF data). [ToI liveblog 2026-06-09, https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/average-reserve-duty-in-2025-was-78-days-37-of-reservists-are-parents-biggest-number-comes-from-tel-aviv/]
- The IDF says it is short about 12,000 soldiers, 6,000–7,500 of them combat. [IDI 63168]

## 2. What Israelis think

### Toplines and trend (Jews unless stated)
- **IDI, Jan → Nov 2024 (n=756, ±3.56%):** support for conscripting Haredim among non-Haredi Jews rose from 67% to 84.5%; support for the current exemption fell from 22% to 9%. Likud voters 74.5%, Religious Zionism voters 79%. [ToI 2024-12-31, https://www.timesofisrael.com/survey-public-support-for-conscripting-ultra-orthodox-has-surged-throughout-war/; IDI press release https://en.idi.org.il/press-releases/57753]
- By religiosity (Nov 2024, "policy should change given security needs"): secular 90%, traditional non-religious 80%, traditional religious 66%, religious (national-religious) 64.5% (up from 41% in January). National-religious support for conscription doubled, 36.5% → 72.5%. [IDI 57753]
- Haredim (Nov 2024): 61% want the current exemption kept; 20% support enlistment in some form (11% comprehensive, 9% quotas); 19% unsure. [IDI 57753]
- **IDI, July 2025:** 85% of non-Haredi Jews support penalties and loss of benefits for draft-eligible Haredim who do not serve, including 73% of Likud voters and 77% of Religious Zionism voters. [cited in IDI 61994; original survey page not opened]
- **INSS, June 10–13, 2025 (n=805 Jews, ±3.5%):** universal conscription 41%; service by IDF needs (military or civilian) 37.5%; Jews-only mandatory service 10.5%; full exemption 8%; 89% want the status quo changed; 71% say exemptions reduce motivation for combat roles. [INSS, https://www.inss.org.il/publication/idf-conscription/]
- **IDI, Nov 2–6, 2025 (604 Jews, 152 Arabs):** Jews — draft all except outstanding yeshiva students 49.5%; draft only those not really studying ("dropout yeshivot") 31%; keep current voluntary system 15%. Secular and traditional non-religious majorities chose "all except outstanding"; national-religious and traditional-religious leaned to "dropouts only"; about 75% of Haredim chose the status quo. Arabs — 26% / 23% / 35%. "Very similar" to September 2025. [IDI, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/62072]
- **JPPI Israeli Society Index, July 2026:** 46% of all Israelis (50% of Jews) favor tougher sanctions; 31% conscription only with Haredi leadership agreement; 11% say it is futile. Likud voters 75% prefer agreement; center 73% and left 79% prefer sanctions; Yisrael Beiteinu voters 84% sanctions. Haredim: 45% agreement-based conscription, 15% sanctions. [JPost 2026-07, https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-902297; INN, https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/430026] Sample size not published in either article.
- **Hiddush / Smith Institute:** Aug 2024 (n=800 Jews): 83% favor full service (51%) or quotas (32%); 17% accept the Haredi position; sanctions backed by 96% of secular, 78% of Likud voters, 65% of RZ/Otzma voters. [https://hiddush.org/article-23675-0-Hiddush_Annual_Religion_and_State_Index_2024_.aspx] 2025: 80% require Haredi service, 75% punish evaders. [https://hiddush.org/article-23687-0-2025_Religion_and_State_Index.aspx] Sept 2026 (n=800): 84% draft yeshiva students in some form, 16% full exemption, 66% back the court's sanctions; nearly two-thirds want a coalition without Haredi parties or one that rejects their religious demands. [JPost 2026-09-07, https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-907870] Hiddush is an advocacy organization for religious freedom; the pollster is independent but the question framing is Hiddush's.

### How much it moves votes
- IDI Israeli Voice Index, Aug 31–Sept 3, 2026 (n=755): 11.4% of Jews name a party's position on conscription as the biggest influence on their vote; 12.0% as second biggest; 0.0% of Arabs. [https://en.idi.org.il/media/32364/august-voice-index-data.pdf]
- So: near-consensus that Haredim should serve, but for most voters it is one issue among several. Among right-wing voters, support for "conscription" coexists with a preference for doing it by agreement rather than sanctions (JPPI).

### What Haredim say
- Survey: majority for the status quo (61% Nov 2024; ~75% Nov 2025, IDI). A minority (15–20%) accept enlistment or sanctions. JPPI's 45% "with leadership agreement" suggests openness when the rabbis sign off.
- Haredi voters' priorities: 41% put religion-and-state first (17% of others); 36% vote for the same party no matter what (7% of the public); only 28% give their political leadership high marks (June 2026 Voice Index). IDI's authors list the draft-law failure, cracks in rabbinic consensus, and the growth of a "modern Haredi" sector as current fractures. [IDI, Lafair/Malach, 2026-08-03, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65364]
- Leaders' words after the April 2026 sanctions ruling: Goldknopf, a "blow to the heart of the Jewish identity of the State of Israel"; "no sanction will stop the voice of the Torah." Gafni: "The State of Israel is gradually losing, step by step, its identity as a Jewish and democratic state." Eichler: the court is "declaring a governmental war against the children of Israel." [ToI liveblog 2026-04-26, https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/haredi-leaders-assail-oppressive-courts-order-to-sanction-draft-evaders/]
- Protest slogan at anti-draft rallies: "Better to die than enlist." [JPost 904035]
- At the same time, enlistment has roughly doubled since 2023, mostly through Haredi tracks such as the Hasmonean Brigade. IDF chief Zamir to Haredi commanders: "Your integration into the IDF is vital to the security of the State of Israel." [ToI 2026-07-29]
- Shas's own campaign ad claimed "more than 30,000 conscripts and career soldiers voted for Shas" (pulled by court order); Deri, Sept 3: "The army doesn't want haredi soldiers, get that into your head" (class doc 11a; JPost page now returns 404 — not verified).

## 3. What each party says (2026)

From class docs 11a–11d unless noted; Ynet's Sept 16 questionnaire re-opened and confirmed. [Ynet 2026-09-16, https://www.ynetnews.com/article/sjph72wyzx]

- **Likud:** No written position; "Likud chose not to respond" to Ynet's draft questions (verified). In office, it ousted Edelstein, backed Bismuth's bill, and voted for the July 2026 arrest-ban law. Karhi (2025): "a historic conscription law that will conscript about 17,000 young Haredi men." [ToI 2025-07-23]
- **Shas:** Protect full-time yeshiva students; Ynet: accepts that the court rules out broad exemptions for those not studying (verified). Deri told Netanyahu that without two laws protecting yeshiva students "there was nothing to discuss" (class doc). Wants Torah study enshrined as a basic value.
- **UTJ:** Asher, Sept 28: "will not support or enter any government without regulating the status of Torah learners" (class doc, Matzav). Ynet: opposes a universal draft and the enlistment of yeshiva dropouts (verified). Degel slogan "The right to be Haredi."
- **Religious Zionism–Zehut:** Voted for the arrest ban; Smotrich: "arrests don't bring ultra-Orthodox to the army… they just make Jews fight one another" yet "haredim should be enlisted"; platform: a Basic Law for service members, "first in equality." Declined to answer Ynet on sanctions (verified). Two RZ figures (Sofer, Solomon) left over the issue.
- **Otzma Yehudit:** Backed the arrest ban; pitches Haredi tracks in police and Border Police "with full observance of Shabbat, kashrut, prayers and Torah study" (verified, Ynet).
- **Amcha Yisrael (Winter):** "We will not join a government that does not pass, before it is formed, a law that puts an end to draft evasion and requires everyone to report for service… Full stop." "No sector is above the law." [JPost 2026-09-02, https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-907373, verified] Still recommends Netanyahu.
- **Yashar (Eisenkot):** National service for all; deferrals capped at 3% of each cohort (~2,200 of ~70,000); end rabbis' ability to grant exemptions "through a lawyer"; "revoke any state incentives granted to those who evade service." [JPost 2026-09-24, https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-909543, verified] Target ≥10,000 Haredi recruits a cycle (class doc).
- **B'Yachad (Bennett/Lapid):** "Every citizen… will enlist in the IDF or perform national civilian service… without exemptions — that applies to Arabs and to the ultra-Orthodox alike" (verified, Ynet). Bennett: "I am talking about 0 exemption." End stipends for married yeshiva students.
- **The Democrats (Golan):** "The obligation to serve the state should apply to all citizens, within a national and equal framework" (verified, Ynet); Golan earlier called mass Haredi conscription "impractical"; party says universal service as "a process."
- **Yisrael Beiteinu:** "No exemptions, no quotas and no deals" (verified, Ynet); everyone at 18 including Arabs; "the draft law will be the first we advance."
- **The Reservists (Hendel):** Universal reporting with military first; broad benefit loss for non-enlistees (verified, Ynet); "Those who do not serve cannot vote or be elected to the Knesset" (class doc); draft law before a government is sworn in.
- **Joint List:** Opposes all compulsory enlistment and sanctions (verified, Ynet) — position is about Arab citizens, not Haredim specifically.
- **Ra'am:** Opposes drafting Arab citizens; declined to address Haredi enlistment (verified, Ynet). Proposes voluntary civilian service for Arab youth.
- **Noam, Blue and White:** No 2026 position found in this pass. Blue and White's IDI page carries no draft position. Gantz pushed draft legislation in earlier governments; not re-sourced here.

## 4. How an American reader is likely to misread it

1. **Reading it as church-state separation.** Americans hear "religious exemption" and think of the First Amendment. In Israel the core complaint is not that the state funds religion (most Israelis accept that) but that one group does not share a duty almost everyone else carries: the phrase is "equal burden" (*shivyon ba'netel*). National-religious Israelis, who are the most observant Zionists, are now among the strongest supporters of drafting Haredim (IDI: 36.5% → 72.5% in 2024) and carry the highest reserve load (IDI 2026).
2. **Missing the reservist burden.** Since Oct 7, the same men have been called up again and again: 94 days on average in 2024, 78 in 2025; religious men 21–44 serving at 4.3% a month in 2025 vs Haredi men at 0.3%. The anger is about families and jobs, not theology.
3. **Assuming Haredi parties are one bloc.** Shas (Sephardi, with many non-Haredi traditional voters) and UTJ (Ashkenazi) differ; UTJ itself now runs as three factions, and Degel's rabbi removed Gafni from the slate. Shas fields a reserve lieutenant colonel at no. 6. Only 28% of Haredim rate their leadership highly.
4. **Assuming Haredim never serve.** About 4,000 enlisted in 2025–26, more than double pre-war. But that is still under a third of the annual Haredi cohort, and almost none of the men who received draft orders came.
5. **Assuming "the right" wants exemptions and "the left" wants a draft.** Hawkish right-wing parties (Amcha, Yisrael Beiteinu, the Reservists) make the draft their first condition; Likud voters say Haredim should serve but prefer agreement over sanctions.
6. **Assuming the election was called early over the draft.** The draft broke the coalition, but the Knesset was nearly at term anyway.
7. **Assuming Arab citizens are in the same debate.** Arab citizens (except Druze) are not drafted; some parties (Yisrael Beiteinu, B'Yachad) want universal service including Arabs; Arab parties oppose it.
8. **American Haredi connection.** After funding cuts, Haredi rabbis raised money abroad, "particularly among Haredi communities in the United States" (ToI 2024-04-01). Some American readers' own communities are part of the story.

## Source disagreements
- **Size of the draft-eligible pool:** 63,000 (court 2024) vs 66,000 (IDF 2025) vs 80,000 (ToI) vs ~100,000 (IDI 2025) vs 73,664 evaders (court 2026). Different definitions (yeshiva-enrolled vs. all 18–24 vs. all deferred to 26).
- **Pre-war baseline:** ~1,800 (ToI, IDF) vs ~1,700 (JPost, IDI 64062).
- **2024–25 enlistment:** 2,800 (IDF via ToI) vs 2,940 (IDI 61994) vs ~2,700 (IDI 59892) — and 798 enlisted from draft orders (IDI 63168). Total Haredi enlistment and response-to-orders are different measures; the page must not mix them.
- **Average reserve duty:** IDF says 78 days in 2025 (94 in 2024); IDI's Jan 2026 article says about 110 days annually (unclear year or population).
- **Annual Haredi cohort:** ~14,000 (JPost) vs Eisenkot's "annual cohort is approximately 70,000" (that is the whole national cohort, not Haredim).
- **2026 enlistment projection:** ≥3,300 (JPost 885107, Jan 2026) vs ~4,000 preliminary (IDF via ToI, July 2026).
- **Surveys differ by framing:** "support conscription" (IDI 84.5%) vs "tougher sanctions" (JPPI 46%) vs "universal conscription" (INSS 41%). These are not contradictions; they ask different things.
- **Wikipedia's 2026 election article** gives 84.5% as "January 2025"; IDI's fieldwork was November 2024, published Dec 31, 2024/Jan 2025.
