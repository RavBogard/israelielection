# Religious Zionists — brief for ruling

## Findings that carry the page
1. One Jewish adult in eight: CBS 12.4% (2026); IDI 12.9% (2025; its category "national religious / Haredi leumi" includes Hardal); Pew 13% (2014–15). Broader claims (16–22%) take in traditional Jews.
2. Growth is in children: state-religious schools take 21.3% of Jewish first-graders (2025–26), up from 19.6% in 2000; dati fertility about 3.9 vs secular 2.0 (JPPI 2023, years not stated). The secular page uses Taub's ~1.6, which covers secular and traditional non-religious women together — a different source and category.
3. Reserve burden: national-religious men are 14.7% of men 21–44 but were 27.4% of male reservists in 2024; in 2025, 4.3% of religious men served in an average month vs 1.7% of secular men and 0.3% of Haredi men (IDI/CBS; the 2025 source category is "religious" by CBS self-definition, close to but not the same as national-religious).
4. 2022: 45% voted for the RZ–Otzma–Noam joint list (from 30% in 2021, as Yamina's 27% vanished); Likud steady at about 23%. Most of that list's voters (57%) were not national-religious.
5. 2026 (Dialog/Ynet, Sept 2026, n=508): RZ–Zehut 30, Likud 17, Otzma 12, Winter 6, centrist lists ~12 in all (Yashar 4, B'Yachad 3, Reservists 3, Yisrael Beiteinu 1, Blue and White 1), undecided 13; 23% feel "politically homeless."
6. Courts: 19% trust the Supreme Court; 75% say it intervenes too much. Security: 87% supported continuing the Iran operation (Roaring Lion, March 2026); confidence that the state protects citizens rose 32% → 65% (2022–25) while it fell among secular Israelis.
7. The draft splits them from their own government: IDI (Jul–Aug 2026) says only a minority back the 2026 law halting arrests of Haredi evaders (no national-religious figure printed), but INSS (Jul 2026) puts support among "religious" respondents at 56%; the two surveys disagree. 37% say the government did not represent them on it.
