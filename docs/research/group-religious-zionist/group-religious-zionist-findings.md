## 1. Size, growth, geography

### How big is the group? Three answers, depending on who counts and how
- **CBS (official, self-definition, Jews 20+), Sept 2026:** "religious" 12.4% of Jewish adults; Haredi 11.5; traditional-religious 14.0; traditional-not-so-religious 21.8; secular 39.6. [class doc 06, via VIN News 2026-09-09, https://vinnews.com/2026/09/09/israel-enters-5787-with-population-growth-high-birthrate-and-rising-emigration/]. CBS 2025: religious 12.0%; CBS 2009: 11.7% [class doc 05]. The CBS category is "religious" (dati), not "national-religious"; it is the closest official proxy.
- **IDI Israeli Democracy Index 2025 (survey sample, Jews 18+, fieldwork May 4–28, 2025, n=1,205 Jews):** "National religious / Haredi leumi" 12.9% of the Jewish sample (p. 321 sample profile). https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf
- **Pew (fieldwork Oct 2014–May 2015, n=5,601 adults, 3,789 Jews):** Datiim 13% of Israeli Jews, 10% of all adults. https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/israels-religiously-divided-society/
- **IDI reserve study (CBS Labor Force Survey):** national-religious men are 14.7% of Jewish men 21–44 (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/57912, Jan 9, 2025). A younger age band gives a bigger share because the group is younger.
- **Broad estimates:** JPPI 2025 says Religious Zionists are "16–17% of the general population" [class doc 05, https://jppi.org.il/en/annual-assessment-of-the-jewish-people-2025-5785/s/8/]. A 2024 Reset DOC essay (Claudia De Martino) says "approximately 22 percent of the Jewish population" identifies with religious Zionism broadly; no primary source given (https://www.resetdoc.org/story/growing-weight-religious-zionism-israel-war-gaza/). Stephen Flatow (president, Religious Zionists of America) writes 20–22%, unsourced (https://www.sdjewishworld.com/2026/02/23/religious-zionists-sacrifice-their-lives-in-the-idf-well-beyond-their-proportion-in-israels-population/). These bigger numbers fold in traditional-religious Jews who identify with the movement without calling themselves dati.

What is worth noticing: the core group is stable at about one Jewish adult in eight (12–13%) for 15 years in CBS data. The growth shows up in children, not adults.

### Growth: school enrollment and fertility
- **First grade, state-religious stream (Education Ministry via ToI, Aug 31, 2025):** 2000: 16,639; 2015: 23,083; 2025–26: 29,470. Secular state: 51,680 / 66,059 / 66,185. Haredi: 16,525 / 29,967 / 42,751. https://www.timesofisrael.com/for-the-1st-time-religious-1st-graders-in-israel-outnumber-secular-ones-by-thousands/
  - [arith] State-religious share of Jewish first-graders (secular + state-religious + Haredi): 19.6% (2000), 19.4% (2015), 21.3% (2025). Share of all first-graders incl. Arab, Bedouin, Druze: 16.4% (class doc 02).
  - Caveat: state-religious schools also enroll children of traditional families; enrollment is not the same as family identity.
- **Total fertility rate by religiosity (CBS, as cited in JPPI Annual Assessment 2023):** Haredi 6.45; dati 3.88; traditional-religious 2.82; traditional-not-so-religious 2.34; secular 2.0. Dati range over four decades 3.7–4.3. https://jppi.org.il/en/%D7%94%D7%A2%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%94-%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%AA-2023/s/5.4/ — the exact data years are not stated on the page (likely 2019–2021 three-year average, Hleihel method). verified: true for the numbers, data year uncertain.
- Taub Center (Weinreb, Dec 2025) confirms "a slow and moderate reduction in fertility over the 2014–2023 period… at all levels of religiosity"; the numbers by religiosity are in charts only. https://www.taubcenter.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Demography-2025-ENG-6.pdf

### Sub-streams
- **Torani / Hardal (Haredi-leumi):** IDI surveys offer "national-religious" and "Torani/Hardal" as separate answers. In IDI's pooled 2022-vote data the Hardal group is "a relatively small group" (Finkelstein, June 28, 2026, https://en.idi.org.il/articles/64807). An earlier fetch of that page gave a Hardal subsample of 254; I could not confirm that number in the page text (footnote not rendered) — verified: false. The CBS has no Hardal category. No size estimate from a primary source was found.
- Yagil Levy (Open University) on the Hardal: "constitutes the ideological core of the settlement movement, controls a dense network of hesder yeshivas and religious pre-military academies"; "A Hardal minority, comprising only a few percentage points of the Jewish population, leverages its influence over the military through a structural threat." (K. La Revue interview, Feb 5, 2026, https://k-larevue.com/en/2026/02/05/on-the-evolution-of-the-idf-interview-with-yagil-levy/)
- **Datlash (those raised dati who leave):** Pew's 2025 switching study (2024 fieldwork) found only about 60% of those raised Dati or Masorti still identify that way; Hilonim keep 93% [class doc 05; I could not reopen the Pew page — verified: false]. No 2024–26 primary study on datlashim specifically was found.
- **Liberal / moderate stream:** No survey measures it directly. Indirect signs: Jewish Home (Shaked) took 10.5% of non-Hardal national-religious voters in 2022 and opposition lists 12.5% (IDI 64807). The IDI 2025 Democracy Index found national-religious respondents the most likely to say no party represents them, and suggests why: "The latter result may be attributable to the lack of a religious Zionist party in the last elections that was not associated with the extreme Right." Organizations: Ne'emanei Torah Va'Avodah (founded 1978; education, gender equality, opposes Rabbinate coercion) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ne%27emanei_Torah_Va%27Avodah — Wikipedia only).

### Where they live
- Primary geographic data by religiosity and locality was not found (CBS site blocks automated reading). What is sourced:
  - Settlements: Yesha Council composition of West Bank Jewish population 36% Haredi / 36% religious-Zionist / 28% secular (via ToI 2023-05-12) [class doc 05]. West Bank Jewish population 540,327–541,085 on Jan 1, 2026 (Yesha/JNS) [class doc 06].
  - The locality vote shares below (Beit El, Eli, Kedumim, Efrat, Kiryat Arba) are the best evidence available on the site of where the movement's vote is concentrated.
  - Jerusalem neighborhoods, Petah Tikva, Modi'in area, hesder towns (e.g. Ma'ale Adumim, Yeruham, Sderot, Kiryat Shmona): no sourced population figures found. Givat Shmuel is a mixed city near Bnei Brak with a large religious-Zionist population; this is common description, not a sourced figure.

### Over-representation in combat, officers and reserves
- **Reserves (strongest current data — CBS Labor Force Survey):**
  - IDI (Finkelstein & Porat Hirsh, Jan 9, 2025): national-religious men were 14.7% of men 21–44 but 21.9% of male reservists in late 2023 and 27.4% in 2024; among men 35–44, 2.5 times their population share. https://en.idi.org.il/articles/57912
  - IDI (Finkelstein & Portal, Aug 20, 2026): share of men 21–44 serving in an average month — Oct–Dec 2023: religious 15.2%, secular 11.2%, traditional 11.2%; 2025: religious 4.3%, traditional 2.6%, secular 1.7%, Haredi 0.3%. Women 18–44, 2023: secular 2.0, traditional 1.6, religious 0.7. https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65427
  - JPost's report on the same study says 4.1% for 2025 and "31% served in the reserves" vs "15% of Israel's population" (https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-906272). The IDI page says 4.3%. Use IDI.
- **Officers (older data):**
  - Maariv (Ben Caspit) via INN, Aug 26, 2007: about 50% of new combat officers and 40% of cadets at the officer school (Bahad 1) wear a kippah. https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/123492 — press report, IDF figure not published.
  - CS Monitor, Apr 17, 2015, citing the 2013 book *Between the Yarmulke and the Beret* (ed. Reuven Gal): Orthodox men were 2.5% of infantry officer course graduates in the early 1990s and "more than 25 percent" by the 2010s; Bnei David (Eli) says over 40% of its 2,600 graduates became officers. https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Middle-East/2015/0417/In-Israel-s-army-more-officers-are-now-religious.-What-that-means
  - Tablet (Hacohen & Butler, Feb 27, 2026): "42% of cadets in the ground forces officer-training courses were graduates of religious schools" (2011); June 2012, a quarter of infantry officer course graduates came from yeshiva-style academies that are 4% of the annual intake. https://www.tabletmag.com/sections/israel-middle-east/articles/israel-theology-power-religious-zionism
  - Levy (2026): settlers were 13% of combat company commanders (late 2000s); secular middle-class soldiers were about two-thirds of fatalities in 1982 and about one-third in the current war (https://k-larevue.com/en/2026/02/05/on-the-evolution-of-the-idf-interview-with-yagil-levy/).
- **Fallen soldiers:** No primary dataset by religiosity was found. Amit Segal estimated over 60% of October 2024's fallen were religious Zionists (ToI, Nov 1, 2024, https://www.timesofisrael.com/as-religious-zionist-idf-casualties-rise-so-does-resentment-of-haredi-exemption-bill/). Flatow's "approximately one-third" is unsourced. Reset DOC: settlers 5% of population, 16% of casualties, unsourced. Recommendation in brief: do not chart fallen-soldier shares.

What the table shows:
- In the ideological settlements (Beit El, Eli, Kedumim) the vote consolidated behind Smotrich's list as Bennett's Yamina vanished: Beit El 71→83, Eli 56→75.
- Efrat (Gush Etzion, many English speakers) is the moderate end: Bennett's lists led there in three of four cycles to 2021, and in 2022 Shaked's Jewish Home still took 15.4% and National Unity 6.9%.
- Kiryat Arba (Ben-Gvir's home) gave Otzma 23.2% running alone in Sept 2019, and Likud is consistently strong there (21–45%) — a more traditional, more Mizrahi electorate than the hilltop settlements.
- Givat Shmuel, inside the Green Line, looks like a mixed right-of-center suburb: in 2022 RZ and Likud tied at 24 with Yesh Atid and National Unity together at 27.

### The joint list and the 2026 split
- 2021: RZ+Otzma+Noam joint list, 5.12% (CEC), 6 seats. 2022: 10.84% (CEC), 14 seats; split in the Knesset on Nov 20, 2022 into RZ 7, Otzma 6, Noam 1 [class doc 11b].
- 2026: Smotrich refused a renewed joint run: "the Religious Zionist public can and should run on its own as a big, strong, significant party" (JPost 2026-08-13/14) [class doc 11b]. RZ formed a technical bloc with Feiglin's Zehut on Sept 1, 2026. Otzma runs alone. Ofer Winter's Amcha Yisrael (launched Aug 25, 2026) targets the same pool. Netanyahu urged a merger: "Every time we went together, we won" (ToI, Aug 27, 2026, https://www.timesofisrael.com/netanyahu-urges-ben-gvir-smotrich-to-unite-as-poll-shows-winters-new-party-surging/). Noam's 2026 status not confirmed in this pass.

## 3. Attitudes on the six issues
Main source: IDI Israeli Democracy Index 2025 (fieldwork May 4–28, 2025; 1,205 Jews; national-religious subsample about 155 [arith from 12.9%], so roughly ±8 points). https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf

**Courts / judicial overhaul**
- Trust in the Supreme Court (national religious): 16% (2023), 15% (2024), 19% (2025). Secular: 66.5 / 58.5 / 66. Haredi: 11 / 7 / 3. (Table 2.6)
- Trust in the Attorney General: 11 / 10 / 14. Secular 53 / 50 / 65. (Table 2.18)
- "The Supreme Court intervenes too much in government decisions": agree — national religious 75%, Haredim 93.5, traditional religious 71, traditional non-religious 54.5; 68% of secular disagree. (text, p. ~150)
- Israel should have a constitution: 51% important, 24% not, 25% don't know (lowest after Haredim). (Table 3.9)
- July 2026 Voice Index: "In the national religious public, the majority support both the gender segregation law and the attorney general law" (AG opinions no longer binding); only a minority support the law halting arrests of Haredi draft evaders. Exact figures in chart image only. https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65483

**Haredi draft**
- July 2026 IDI: national-religious minority support the arrest-freeze law (above). Jews overall: 25.3% support, 71.5% oppose (data PDF, https://en.idi.org.il/media/31733/july-2026-index-data.pdf) [arith: 17.3+8.0; 14.3+57.2].
- Dialog/Ynet Sept 2026: 15% name draft equality as top issue; 32% vs 37% on whether the government represented them (above).
- March 2026 IDI on the state budget decision: national religious 34% "responsible decision," 37% "political deal that prioritizes sectoral interests," 15% both (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/63856).
- Reserve burden data (section 1) is the factual background to the anger.

**War and hostages**
- Support for continuing Operation Roaring Lion (Iran, March 2026): national religious 87%; secular 67.5% (https://en.idi.org.il/articles/63856).
- Trust in the IDF: 87 / 83 / 84 (2023–25); trust in Shin Bet fell 65→48 (2024→25) (Tables 2.4, 2.21).
- "Israel ensures the security of its citizens": national religious 32% (2022) → 65% (2025); secular 52 → 30. (Table 2.29) — a reversal worth noticing: the group closest to the government grew more confident while the secular grew less.
- Hostage-deal attitudes by religiosity: IDI 2025 articles show religiosity charts but numbers are images only. Not captured.

**West Bank, Gaza and annexation**
- Jewish settlement in Gaza (IDI, Sept 3, 2025): support highest among Haredim, lowest among secular; national religious level "remained unchanged" since Nov 2024; numbers in chart image only. https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61601
- Government should encourage Arab citizens to emigrate: national religious 68% (DI 2025). Separation of Jews and Arabs to preserve Jewish identity: 61%. Support including Arab parties in government: 13%. (DI 2025 ch. 5)
- Pew 2014–15: 68% of Datiim say settlements help Israel's security, 13% hurt; about 71% favor expelling or transferring Arabs (https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2016/03/08/israels-religiously-divided-society/). Old.
- Violent settlers treated too leniently/harshly (July 2026): by political camp only, not religiosity (65483).

**Religion and state**
- DI 2025: the most common (not majority) national-religious answer is that Israel's democratic component is too strong (relative to the Jewish one). Exact share in chart only.
- Pew 2014–15: 69% of Datiim favor making halakha state law for Jews. Old.
- Religious–secular tension named as the most acute: national religious 7% (2024) → 14% (2025); right–left tension 57% (DI 2025, Table 4.9 and text).
- Prefer parties with equal women's representation: national religious 35%; oppose barring all-male parties from running: 74% (IDI, 62950).

**Economy and cost of living**
- No 2025–26 economy question broken out by religiosity found in English IDI material. "Israel ensures the welfare of its citizens": national religious 26% (2022) → 38% (2025); secular 22 → 8 (Table 2.32). Sense of belonging to the stronger groups in society: 75 / 72 / 71 (2022 / 2024 / 2025) — the highest of any group (Table 4.13).

**Other markers useful for the page**
- Optimistic about Israel's future: 88.5% (2024) → 76.5% (2025). Trust in the government 49 → 39 → 36; Knesset 35 → 27 → 20 (2023–25). Social solidarity rated high (7–10): 48%, highest of any group. Trust in the media: 9%.

## 4. Lived texture

**From Mafdal to Smotrich and Ben-Gvir.** The National Religious Party (founded 1956 from Mizrachi and Hapoel HaMizrachi) sat in every government to 1992, ran in a "historic alliance" with Labor and focused on religion's place in public life; it held 11–12 seats until 1977 (12 seats), fell to 6 in 1981 and 3 in 2006, and merged into Jewish Home in 2008 (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Religious_Party — Wikipedia only). After 1967 the movement's centre moved to settlement (Rabbi Zvi Yehuda Kook; Gush Emunim). Tkuma split off in 1998 and became Smotrich's party [class doc 11b]. The 2019–21 cycles were a contest between Bennett's more liberal-economic, mixed list (New Right/Yamina) and the URWP/RZ line; the CEC table shows Yamina's vote moving to RZ once Bennett governed with Ra'am and the left in 2021–22.

**Ben-Gvir's different base.** IDI's Finkelstein: Smotrich draws "mainstream Religious Zionists," Ben-Gvir "traditional Mizrahi voters from the periphery" (ToI 2026-04-15) [class doc 11b]. The IDI data agree: the 2022 joint list was only 43% national-religious.

**The liberal religious-Zionist minority.** About a quarter of non-Hardal national-religious voters chose opposition lists or Jewish Home in 2022 (IDI). Efrat is the clearest place to see it in votes. In the Dialog poll, a Givat HaRoeh resident (Binyamin) said he feels "much closer to Elisaf Peretz and Chili Tropper," and Sarah Eliash, a founder of Kedumim, said she wants "a party that will demand a Zionist unity government" (Ynet, Sept 4, 2026; quotes rendered by a fetch tool from Hebrew — check against the original before publishing).

**Post-Oct 7 reservist leadership and the draft.** The reserve burden (above) produced a movement inside the movement: over 2,000 religious-Zionist women formed "Partners in Service" (Shutafot La'Sherut) demanding equal conscription; Rabbi Yitzhak Shilat: "There is absolutely no halachic or moral justification for exempting part of the nation from military service"; Rachel Goldberg, writing to MKs ten days before her husband Capt. Avraham Yosef Goldberg was killed in Lebanon: "Where is the sense of shared obligation?" (ToI, Nov 1, 2024, link above). Two RZ figures (Ofir Sofer, Moshe Solomon) left over the exemption (Ynet 2026-07-26) [class doc 11b]. Ofer Winter (Bnei David graduate, former Givati and 98th Division commander) built a party on "No sector is above the law. Those who serve will receive." Settler anger: "I don't have a political home today"; Smotrich "sold this country to the ultra-Orthodox" (ToI 2026-08-07) [class doc 11b].

**Voices (Ynet/Dialog, Sept 4, 2026; check exact Hebrew).**
- Aviad, 29, Ofra (grew up in Petah Tikva): "The gravest thing in the government's conduct, in my eyes, is the Haredim and the draft."
- Liat Zamir, 28, Jerusalem, tour guide: "I relied on Ben-Gvir and Smotrich on security" — she says they did not meet expectations.
- Moti Cohen, 34, Petah Tikva, lawyer: "Smotrich represents the values of religious Zionism best for me."
- Eitan Neuman, Tekoa: "I don't think people like Eisenkot will clash head-on with the settlement enterprise."

**A religious-Zionist critique from inside.** Tablet (Aviad Hacohen and Menachem Butler, Feb 27, 2026): "the halachic tradition permits and regulates force; it does not sanctify power as such."

## Source disagreements
- Group size: CBS 12.4% (Jews 20+, "religious") vs IDI sample 12.9% vs Pew 13% vs JPPI 16–17% of total population vs Reset DOC/Flatow 20–22% (unsourced). Different categories and bases.
- Reserve service 2025 monthly rate: IDI 4.3% vs JPost's report of the same study 4.1%.
- Officers: "about 50% of new combat officers" (Maariv 2007) vs "more than 25%" of infantry officer graduates (Gal, 2013) vs "42% of ground forces cadets were religious-school graduates" (Tablet, 2011 figure). Different measures (kippah-wearers, religious schooling, graduates vs cadets).
- Fallen soldiers: "over 60%" (Segal, one month) vs "one-third" (Flatow) vs settlers 16% (Reset). None primary.
- Likud's share of national-religious voters 2021: IDI "around 23%."
- RZ polling: Lazar/Zman late Aug had RZ below threshold; after the Zehut merger polls show 5–7 seats.
