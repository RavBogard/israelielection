## 1. Size, growth, geography

### How many
- IDI's 2025 yearbook counts **1,452,350 Haredim, 14.3% of Israel's population**, up from about 750,000 in 2009. Growth is "around 4.2% per year." 57% are under age 20 (31% in the general population). [IDI Statistical Report on Ultra-Orthodox Society 2025, exec. summary](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf); release date Dec 14, 2025 per [The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/headlines/israel-democracy-institute-report-finds-stalled-integration-of-ultra-orthodox-men-as-community-growth-accelerates/).
- The Haredi Institute for Public Affairs (Machon, a Haredi-run research body) counts fewer: **about 1.26 million, 12.5%** of the population and 16% of Jews, late 2024. [Machon annual report 2024, English](https://machon.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/english-digital-1-1-1.pdf); [Ynet summary, 2024-12-30](https://www.ynetnews.com/jewish-world/article/h1cch618jg).
- CBS self-identification of Jews aged 20+: 11.5% Haredi in 2026, up from 8.2% in 2009 (class doc 05; adults only, so it undercounts a very young population).
- What is worth noticing: the gap between 1.26M and 1.45M is a definitions gap (who counts as Haredi: self-ID, school system, place of residence), not a disagreement about the trend. Both agree the share is rising fast.

### Fertility and age
- Total fertility: **7.5 births per woman in 2003–05, 6.5 in 2021–23** (non-Haredi Jewish women: 2.2). [IDI 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf)
- Machon puts 2023 fertility at **6.1**, which Ynet reports as "a 43-year low." By stream (2023): Hasidic 7.0, Lithuanian 6.4, Sephardi 5.2. [Machon 2024](https://machon.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/english-digital-1-1-1.pdf)
- Fertility is falling but remains about three times the non-Haredi Jewish rate. Because half the community is children, growth continues for decades even if fertility keeps falling.

### Projections
- CBS projection (cited by IDI, Feb 2026): Haredim ~20% of the population by 2040, **24.4% by 2050**. IDI's own updated estimate: ~22% by 2050. [IDI, "Haredim in Israel 2050," 2026-02-10](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/63385)
- CBS's 2017 long-range projection: **32% by 2065**, nearly 6.5 million people, and half of all children under 15. [Israel National News, 2017-05-22, reporting the CBS release](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/229972). JPost's headline the same day: "A third of the population is forecast to be ultra-Orthodox" (body text paywalled; [JPost](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/report-israels-population-to-reach-20-million-by-2065-492429)).
- Machon's 2065 scenarios, which build in falling fertility: **17.7% to 22.6%** (TFR 5.0 to 6.2). It says these "differ significantly from conventional estimates predicting 32% by 2065." [Machon 2024](https://machon.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/english-digital-1-1-1.pdf)
- INSS (DellaPergola, May 2024), CBS-based: Haredim 13% in 2020 → 24% by 2050; Haredi children 26% of Jewish children in 2020 → 46% by 2050. [INSS Strategic Assessment](https://www.inss.org.il/strategic_assessment/dellapergola/)
- Draft-age pipeline: Haredim are 23.5% of those reaching draft eligibility now, projected 25% (2030), 33% (2040), 40% (2050). [IDI 63385](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/63385)

### Where they live
- Machon counts (2023): Jerusalem 292,600 (28.9% of the city); **Bnei Brak 209,700 (94.8%)**; Beit Shemesh 108,000 (73.2%); **Modi'in Illit 86,600 (99.1%)**; **Beitar Illit 67,100 (98.5%)**; Ashdod 52,600 (22.4%); **Elad 48,700 (94.4%)**. [Machon 2024](https://machon.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/english-digital-1-1-1.pdf)
- IDI (share of all Haredim): Jerusalem 22.6%, Bnei Brak 15.6%, the four "satellite" cities (Beit Shemesh, Modi'in Illit, Beitar Illit, Elad) 25%, other major cities 11%. [IDI 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf)
- Movement: about 75,000 Haredim left Jerusalem and 42,000 left Bnei Brak over the past decade; Beit Shemesh gained about 40,000. [Ynet on Machon](https://www.ynetnews.com/jewish-world/article/h1cch618jg)
- Worth noticing for an American reader: two of the largest Haredi cities, Modi'in Illit and Beitar Illit, are West Bank settlements. They make up a large part of the settler population (class doc 05: Yesha gives settlers as 36% Haredi), though their residents moved there for cheap housing, not ideology.

### The streams
- Machon 2024: **Lithuanian ("Litvish") 433,000 (34%)**, **Sephardi 408,000 (33%)**, **Hasidic excluding Chabad 369,000 (29%)**, Chabad ~50,000 (4%). [Machon 2024](https://machon.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/english-digital-1-1-1.pdf). Class doc 05 (Machon Dec 2024 Hebrew) gives Sephardi 32%.
- These map loosely onto parties: Lithuanian → Degel HaTorah (UTJ); Hasidic → Agudat Yisrael (UTJ) and now the new Homat Torat Yisrael faction; Sephardi → Shas. Shas also draws many non-Haredi traditional Mizrahi voters (section 2).
- IDI's yearbook does not break its data down by stream.

### Work and income
- Employment rate, ages 25–66 (IDI 2025): **men ~33% (2003) → 51% (2015) → 54% (2024–25)**; **women ~50% → 78% → 82%**. Non-Haredi Jews: men 87%, women 82%. [IDI 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf). The Media Line's account of the same report gives 53% and 81% for 2025.
- Malach and Cahaner: "The data reflects a decade of stagnation and even a decline in the integration of ultra-Orthodox men." [The Media Line, 2025-12-14](https://themedialine.org/headlines/israel-democracy-institute-report-finds-stalled-integration-of-ultra-orthodox-men-as-community-growth-accelerates/)
- Machon: men 48% (2014) → 55% (2023) → 54% (2024). [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/jewish-world/article/h1cch618jg)
- Taub Center (June 2026): Haredi men's employment "rose slightly at the beginning of the war, but in 2025 it fell to a level below that recorded in 2023"; among Haredi women "it continued to rise, almost closing the gap." [Taub, A Picture of the Nation 2026](https://www.taubcenter.org.il/en/pr/pon-2026/)
- Wages: Haredi men average NIS 9,929/month, 49% of non-Haredi Jewish men; women NIS 8,617, 67% of non-Haredi Jewish women. [The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/headlines/israel-democracy-institute-report-finds-stalled-integration-of-ultra-orthodox-men-as-community-growth-accelerates/)
- Yeshiva/kollel students (2023): 169,366. Haredi students in higher education 2024–25: 18,953, up 306% since 2010. Matriculation exam takers 2022–23: girls 77%, boys 16%. [IDI 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf)

### Poverty
- IDI: **33%** of Haredim below the poverty line (2023) vs 14% of non-Haredi Jews; yet 75% own their apartments. [IDI 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf); [Media Line](https://themedialine.org/headlines/israel-democracy-institute-report-finds-stalled-integration-of-ultra-orthodox-men-as-community-growth-accelerates/)
- Machon: **40% of Haredi households** (2022) vs 11% of non-Haredi Jewish households; child poverty 47% vs 13%. [Machon 2024](https://machon.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/english-digital-1-1-1.pdf)
- Worth noticing: high poverty and high home ownership together. The poverty line is income per person, and large families with one modest income fall below it even when they own a flat. Only 41% of Haredim say they could meet an unexpected expense without family help (63% of others). [IDI 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf)

## 2. Voting pattern, 2019–2022 and 2026

### What the ballot boxes show
Computed from the Central Elections Committee's locality pages for five elections (votes21–votes25.bechirot.gov.il, `cityresults?cityID=` 6100 Bnei Brak, 3797 Modi'in Illit, 3780 Beitar Illit, 1309 Elad, 1137 Kiryat Ye'arim, 3000 Jerusalem, 2610 Beit Shemesh). Shares are of valid votes; turnout is against the voter register.

**Five almost entirely Haredi towns combined** (Bnei Brak, Modi'in Illit, Beitar Illit, Elad, Kiryat Ye'arim; ~144,000–160,000 valid votes):

| Election | Turnout | UTJ | Shas | Likud | Far-right list |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apr 2019 | 84.8 | 62.1 | 27.1 | 4.6 | 3.0 (URWP, incl. Otzma) |
| Sep 2019 | 80.0 | 61.9 | 28.7 | 4.0 | 2.0 (Otzma alone) + 1.6 (Yamina) |
| Mar 2020 | 79.6 | 61.8 | 29.7 | 5.1 | 0.5 (Otzma alone) + 1.4 (Yamina) |
| Mar 2021 | 76.3 | 60.8 | 28.2 | 3.0 | 5.7 (Religious Zionism, incl. Ben-Gvir) |
| Nov 2022 | 79.0 | 59.7 | 30.8 | 2.6 | 5.4 (RZ–Otzma) |

National turnout for comparison: 68.5, 69.8, 71.5, 67.4, 70.6 (CEC national pages, votes21–25.bechirot.gov.il).

City detail, Nov 2022 ([CEC](https://votes25.bechirot.gov.il/cityresults?cityID=6100)):

| City | Turnout | UTJ | Shas | RZ–Otzma | Likud |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bnei Brak | 76.5 | 59.8 | 30.2 | 4.4 | 3.4 |
| Modi'in Illit (mostly Lithuanian/Hasidic) | 82.0 | 77.5 | 19.8 | 1.9 | 0.5 |
| Beitar Illit | 81.3 | 59.4 | 30.8 | 7.8 | 1.4 |
| Elad (Sephardi majority) | 84.4 | 34.9 | **49.6** | 11.2 | 3.4 |
| Jerusalem (mixed) | 61.6 | 23.8 | 18.3 | 14.2 | 19.1 |
| Beit Shemesh (mixed) | 66.1 | 38.8 | 21.6 | 14.7 | 15.5 |

What the table shows:
- About nine in ten votes in Haredi towns go to the two Haredi parties, every time. The UTJ/Shas split is steady; the stream mix of each town decides it (Elad is the one town where Shas leads).
- Likud barely registers in Haredi towns (0.5–5%). Haredim are Netanyahu's partners, not his voters.
- The far-right list is the only outside party that grew among Haredim: about 2–3% before 2021, 5–11% after Ben-Gvir joined Religious Zionism (2021, 2022). It is strongest among young and Sephardi Haredim (Elad 11.2%, Beitar Illit 7.8%) and weakest in Modi'in Illit (1.9%).
- Turnout in Haredi towns runs about 8–16 points above the national figure. The real gap is larger: the register includes many non-Haredi Israelis living abroad, who drag down national turnout more than Haredi-town turnout.
- One oddity to note on the page: the CEC prints 97.8% turnout for Modi'in Illit in April 2019, against 84.5% five months later. Treat that point with caution.

**Survey view of the same vote** (IDI, Finkelstein, published 2026-06-28; 20 surveys Nov 2022–Oct 2023, 10,694 Jewish voters): self-identified Haredim voted **UTJ 58%, Shas 28%, RZ–Otzma 7%, Likud 4%, other 3%**. 86% voted for a Haredi list in 2022, down from 89% in 2021; RZ–Otzma rose from 4% to 7%. [IDI 64807](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/64807)

**Shas is not only Haredi.** IDI's Gilad Malach: "Shas has at least three or four Knesset seats' worth of voters who are simply not Haredi" (class doc 11a, Shomrim 2026-06-30). ToI cites Shas activists putting the core Haredi base at about four seats, with the rest working-class traditional Sephardim in places like Kiryat Shmona and Or Yehuda. [ToI 2026-08-21](https://www.timesofisrael.com/eisenkot-threatens-to-siphon-traditional-voters-away-from-a-panicking-shas/). One activist quoted there: "They all serve in the IDF. They can't stand seeing Shas turn into the anti-draft Jerusalem Faction."

National: Shas 8.25% / 11 seats; UTJ 5.88% / 7 seats (2022). Combined Haredi-party share 8.2% in 1992 → 14.1% in 2022. [IDI 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf)

### 2026: is UTJ still one list? (verified)
Yes. UTJ filed one list, but with three factions for the first time since 1992. Davar (2026-09-08) confirms one combined list with a new third faction, **Homat Torat Yisrael** (Meir Porush and Elyakim Stark), alongside Degel HaTorah and Agudat Yisrael. Top of the list: 1 Yaakov Asher (Degel), 2 Yitzhak Goldknopf (Agudah), 3 Yitzhak Pindrus (Degel), 4 Meir Porush (Homat), 5 Moshe Rosenthal (Degel), 6 Elyakim Stark (Homat). The faction structure gives its members separate party funding and "the ability to split from United Torah Judaism if elected to the Knesset." The cause was complaints by non-Gur Hasidic courts about how the Gur-led Agudah divided funds. [Davar 696518](https://www.davar1.co.il/696518/). Background (class doc 11a): Degel's Rav Dov Landau removed Moshe Gafni and Uri Maklev from the slate by letter on Sept 5, 2026; both factions left the government in July 2025 over the draft.

Leaders are not promising a bloc. Asked by Netanyahu in July to commit to his bloc after the election, Deri said: "First you pass the laws, then we'll talk"; Gafni cited his rabbi: "as of now, there is no bloc." [ToI 2026-07-07](https://www.timesofisrael.com/netanyahu-said-seeking-haredi-loyalty-after-election-but-party-leaders-noncommittal/). Asher (Sept 28): UTJ "will not support or enter any government without regulating the status of Torah learners" (class doc 11a).

Shas turbulence: Haaretz reported (Aug 12) that former Chief Rabbi Yitzhak Yosef told students "The time has come to topple the right"; he denied it and Shas called the report false. [ToI 2026-08-12](https://www.timesofisrael.com/former-chief-rabbi-yitzhak-yosef-reportedly-orders-students-not-to-vote-for-shas/). Deri staged a public reconciliation with him on Aug 21. [ToI 2026-08-21](https://www.timesofisrael.com/eisenkot-threatens-to-siphon-traditional-voters-away-from-a-panicking-shas/)

New small Haredi lists, both polling below the threshold:
- **Haredi Public** (Moti Leitner, Beit Shemesh deputy mayor; rabbinic committee head Rabbi Yehoshua Pfeffer): those not studying full-time should serve; math and English in schools; billboards "Those who do not learn should serve." [ToI 2026-08-24](https://www.timesofisrael.com/launching-campaign-new-haredi-party-slams-detachment-stupidity-of-existing-factions/)
- **Tzeva Shahor ("The Color Black")**: began as an alert network warning of military-police arrests; launched as a party at a Bnei Brak anti-draft march of "hundreds" on Sept 28. [JPost 909909](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-909909)

### 2026 polling
- Seats (class doc 11a, five polls Sept 28–Oct 2): **UTJ 7–8; Shas 7–10** (Shas 11 in 2022). Most of Shas's drop is attributed to non-Haredi traditional voters.
- Haredi first-time voters (N12/Midgam, Sept 15–22, n=301 all first-time voters, small Haredi subsample): UTJ 45%, Shas 20%, Otzma 13% (JPost via class doc 11a; not reopened).
- IDI (Lafair and Malach, 2026-08-03): 36% of Haredim say they will vote for the same party as always (7% of the public); only 28% give Haredi political leaders high marks for advancing their interests (June 2026 Voice Index). [IDI 65364](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65364)

## 3. Attitudes on the six issues

Most public IDI and INSS releases print only Jews vs Arabs. Haredi breakdowns come from the IDI Democracy Index 2025 (fieldwork May 4–28, 2025; 1,205 Jews, 1,569 total), the INSS July 2026 survey, IDI article-level releases, and Pew (which combines Haredim with Datiim).

### Draft
- INSS (July 23–26, 2026; n=936): the law freezing arrests of yeshiva students who did not report for service — **ultra-Orthodox 90% support (78% strongly)**, religious 56%, traditional 31%, secular 13%; Jewish public 32% support, 63% oppose. INSS Data Center, "National Security Survey – July 2026" (page opened; public URL to be added).
- IDI Nov 2024 (sibling file): Haredim 61% keep the exemption, 20% support enlistment, 19% unsure. IDI Nov 2025 (sibling file): ~75% of Haredim prefer the current arrangement.
- JPPI July 2026 (sibling file): Haredim 45% favor drafting only by agreement with Haredi leaders, 15% tougher sanctions.
- IDI Feb 2024: Haredim were "the only group in which the majority of respondents think that the new circumstances created by the war should not affect the issue of conscription of Haredim." [IDI 53305](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/53305)
- IDI book on Oct 7 and Haredi society (Ravitsky Tur-Paz, Dec 2024): the Haredi public stayed "steadfast" in opposing conscription through the war's first year, and an overwhelming majority believe Torah study contributes to the war effort at least as much as military service. [IDI PDF](https://en.idi.org.il/media/29492/as-you-stand-silent-has-october-7th-changed-the-haredi-community-english-introduction-shlomit-ravitsky-tur-paz.pdf) (text extraction was poor; no direct quotes taken).
- Reserve duty, 2025, men 21–44 serving in an average month: Haredi 0.3%, secular 1.7%, traditional 2.6%, religious 4.3%. [IDI 65427, 2026-08-20](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65427)
- Enlistment by draft year (sibling file, IDF via ToI): ~1,800 before Oct 7 → 2,200 → 2,800 → ~4,000 (2025–26 preliminary), against a yearly Haredi cohort of ~14,000. IDI counts 2,560 Haredi-school graduates enlisted in 2024 plus 500 in civilian service. [IDI 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30207/final-final-haredi-shnaton-2025-pdf.pdf)

### Courts
- Trust in the Supreme Court, Haredim: **11% (2023) → 7% (2024) → 3% (2025)**; secular 66.5 → 58.5 → 66. Attorney General: 11 → 6.5 → 1. [IDI Democracy Index 2025, Tables 2.6, 2.18](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)
- 93.5% of Haredim agree the court intervenes too much in government decisions (sibling issue-courts file, same source).
- 64.5% of Haredim say decisions by a Knesset majority are inherently democratic; 26.5% say basic democratic values come first (Table 3.8).
- 73% of Haredim say the democratic component of the state is too dominant relative to the Jewish one; 5% say the Jewish component is too dominant (Table 2.26).

### The war and hostages
- Trust in the IDF, Haredim: 67% (June 2023) → 50% (2024) → 61% (2025); every other group 80–88%. (Table 2.4)
- "Israel ensures the security of its citizens": Haredim 23% (2022) → **68% (2025)**; secular 52% → 30%. The two groups swapped places. (Table 2.29)
- IDI August 2025: support for Jewish settlement in Gaza was highest among Haredim, and higher than in Nov 2024. Yet a majority of Shas and UTJ voters supported a deal to release all hostages, end the fighting and withdraw fully from Gaza; only RZ voters opposed. [IDI 61601](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/61601) (exact Haredi percentages are in a chart image, not captured).
- IDI Sept 2026: only "around a quarter" of Haredim say Oct 7 is central to their vote, vs about two-thirds of secular and traditional Jews. [IDI 66177](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66177)
- What counts for the vote: 41% of Haredim name religion-state positions as the most influential factor (17% of others); security 5%, economy 3%, the party leader 4% (others 22, 21, 19). [IDI 65364](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/65364)

### West Bank, religion-state, economy (short)
- Pew (report June 3, 2025; Haredim and Datiim combined): 78% say settlement building helps security (Hilonim 27%); 9% think a Palestinian state could coexist peacefully with Israel (Hilonim 25%); 86% view Netanyahu favorably (Hilonim 23%). [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/06/pg_2025.06.03_israel_report.pdf)
- 2016 IDI data on self-placement (old): right-wing identification was 43% of Hasidim, 44% of Sephardim, but 22% of Lithuanians, who more often chose "moderate right" (48%). [IDI 49206](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/49206)
- Religion-state (sibling file): Haredi support for a civil-marriage option 15% (JPPI 2026); Shabbat public transit outside religious areas 16% (Reichman 2025).
- Mood: Haredim are the most satisfied group — personal situation good 85% (secular 43%); Israel's situation good 42% (secular 9%); prefer to stay in Israel 92% (secular 56%). But only 75% "feel part of the state and its problems," the lowest of the Jewish groups (others 87–92%). [Democracy Index 2025](https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf)

## 4. Lived texture

### Streets, 2024–26
- June 2024: the High Court ruled the yeshiva exemption had no legal basis. Since then, draft orders, arrests and protests have become routine (sibling file: ~73,664 counted as evaders, 219 arrested, Sept 2026).
- June 1, 2026: the Jerusalem Faction (~60,000 members) used its "national alert system" to block Route 4, the Jerusalem entrance and the light rail after six yeshiva students were arrested; chant: "We will die and not enlist!" [ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/mass-ultra-orthodox-protests-block-roads-halt-traffic-over-arrest-of-draft-dodgers/). Same day, dozens broke into the Beit Shemesh police station (ToI headline, via ToI draft topic page).
- July 15, 2026: protests at induction centers against about 140 Haredim enlisting. [ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/haredim-protest-at-idf-induction-centers-against-peers-who-enlist/)
- Aug 30–31, 2026: Edah HaCharedit protesters blocked Tel Hashomer; a sign read "it was better to die as a Jew in the Holocaust than to spiritually die in IDF camps." [ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/haredi-anti-draft-protesters-block-roads-at-idf-induction-center-for-second-day/)
- The counter-protest: June 26, 2026, "Mothers at the Front" and reservists blocked a Bnei Brak bridge. Ayelet Hashachar Saydof: "Our children didn't go out to defend a state that protects the right of Haredim to be draft dodgers." [ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/dozens-of-protesters-block-road-in-bnei-brak-in-demand-for-ultra-orthodox-military-draft/)
- IDI's Eliyahu Berkovits read the large Jerusalem anti-draft rally of autumn 2025 as turned inward: "a final attempt to unite the Haredi world around the question of who we are, before the outside world answers this question for us." [IDI 62014, 2025-11-04](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/62014)

### The "new Haredim" and the working middle class
- The data that shows the change: women's employment at 82%, level with non-Haredi women; higher-education enrollment up 306% since 2010; internet use 69% (39% in 2014 per Machon); 8% of Haredi boys now in the state-Haredi school stream, up from 4% (Machon).
- The politics of that group is now on the ballot as Haredi Public. Pfeffer: "Tens of thousands of Haredim... are asking the same question and seeking an alternative, and they are right." Leitner: "To fight poverty, we will work to ensure our children acquire basic life skills and study math and English at a high level." [ToI 2026-08-24](https://www.timesofisrael.com/launching-campaign-new-haredi-party-slams-detachment-stupidity-of-existing-factions/) (quotes seen through a summarizing fetch; check wording before publishing).
- But the counter-trend is real: men's employment is flat for a decade, and only 16% of Haredi boys take matriculation exams.

### Oct 7
- The IDI field study (Malchi and Lachmanovich, in the Dec 2024 IDI book) found that many Haredim from many communities volunteered in civilian efforts in the first months of the war, while their presence in the IDF stayed small, and that there was "no sign... of social revolution." [IDI PDF](https://en.idi.org.il/media/29492/as-you-stand-silent-has-october-7th-changed-the-haredi-community-english-introduction-shlomit-ravitsky-tur-paz.pdf) (paraphrase; extraction garbled the quotes).
- No count of Haredi volunteers (ZAKA, United Hatzalah, food networks) or of post-Oct 7 enlistment requests was found in this pass.

## Source disagreements
1. **Population**: IDI 1,452,350 / 14.3% (2025) vs Machon 1.26M / 12.5% (late 2024) vs CBS 11.5% of Jewish adults (2026) vs Taub 15% (class doc 05).
2. **Fertility**: IDI 6.5 (2021–23) vs Machon 6.1 (2023).
