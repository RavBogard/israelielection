# Ethiopian-Israelis — research findings

Slug: group-ethiopian. Researched 2026-10-05. All URLs below were opened unless marked "not opened" or "secondhand".

A note on method up front: Ethiopian-Israelis are about 1.8% of Israel's population and are spread across many mixed cities. Israel's voter rolls do not record origin, the main opinion surveys reach only a handful of Ethiopian-Israeli respondents, and no locality is majority Ethiopian-Israeli. Every voting and attitude claim on this page therefore rests on thin evidence, and the page should say so plainly.

---

## 1. Size, growth, geography

### Population
- End of 2024: **177,600** people of Ethiopian origin — 93,400 born in Ethiopia, 84,200 born in Israel to Ethiopian-born fathers. About 2.3% of Israel's Jewish population. Source: CBS data digest for Sigd 2025 (release 367/2025), as reported by JNS 2025-11-17 (https://www.clevelandjewishnews.com/jns/israel-s-jewish-ethiopian-population-reached-177-600-in-2024/article_02076886-a899-516a-9bd9-64a7abace4b0.html) and by the Tedros community portal (https://tedros.co.il/en/statistics/demographics). The CBS original for 2025 could not be opened (cbs.gov.il refused connections); figure is secondhand but two independent reports agree.
- End of 2023: 171,600 (93,600 Ethiopia-born, 78,000 Israel-born). CBS Sigd 2024 release, opened: https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/DocLib/2024/371/11_24_371b.pdf
- End of 2021: 164,400 (90,600 Ethiopia-born, 73,800 Israel-born). CBS Sigd 2022 release, opened: https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/DocLib/2022/371/11_22_371e.pdf
- What is worth noticing: the Israel-born share is rising fast (45% in 2021, 47% in 2024). Within a few years most Ethiopian-Israelis will have been born in Israel. The community is young: 7.0% are 65 or older, against 15.0% of Jewish Israelis overall (CBS 2024 release; Tedros for 2025).
- Share of total population: 177,600 of roughly 10 million ≈ 1.8% (arithmetic; the class docs already use ~1.8%).
- Counting rule worth flagging: CBS counts "Ethiopian origin" as born in Ethiopia or Israel-born with an Ethiopian-born father. Third-generation Israelis and children of Ethiopian-born mothers only are not counted, so the figure understates the community as people experience it.

### How they came
- Operation Moses (Nov 1984–Jan 1985): about 6,500 by Wikipedia's count (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Jews_in_Israel); Tedros gives 6,364 as an exact flight count and a range up to 8,000 (https://tedros.co.il/en/statistics/immigration). Brought out through Sudan.
- Operation Joshua (1985): 494, US Air Force (Wikipedia, same page).
- Operation Solomon (May 24–25, 1991): 14,325 people in 36 hours on 34 El Al planes (Wikipedia, citing the Jewish Agency); Tedros gives a range of 14,310–14,500.
- After 1993: an irregular flow of Falash Mura (see next), turned on and off by cabinet decisions.
- Operation Tzur Israel (Nov 2020–July 2023): about 5,000 in two phases, 2,000 then ~3,000 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Tzur_Israel). Tedros's own count from phases is ~3,550 — a disagreement (see below).
- Recent annual arrivals from Ethiopia (CBS): 1,755 (2021), 1,680 (2022), 1,812 (2023), 285 (2024). The 2024 drop follows the end of Tzur Israel.

### The Falash Mura question and those still in Ethiopia
- Falash Mura are descendants of Beta Israel whose families converted to Christianity in the 19th and 20th centuries. Beta Israel who came in Moses and Solomon entered under the Law of Return without conversion; Falash Mura come under special cabinet decisions and must undergo Orthodox conversion in Israel (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falash_Mura). Government decision 2958 (2003) allowed maternal-line descendants to immigrate conditional on conversion.
- The two sides in the argument, both on the record: Rabbi Ovadia Yosef (2002) held they "converted out of fear and persecution and therefore should be considered Jews"; Bezalel Smotrich warned the policy would "open the door to an endless extension of a family chain from all over the world" (both quoted on the Wikipedia page; originals not opened).
- How many are still waiting — sources disagree widely:
  - ~12,000 in Addis Ababa and Gondar, per community lists held by the Interior Ministry (Wikipedia Falash Mura page, citing ToI; not independently opened).
  - ~9,000, July 2023 point estimate (Tedros, which notes no verified update since).
  - "Roughly 7,000," Jerusalem Post op-ed by Shmuel Legesse, 2025-09-14 (https://www.jpost.com/opinion/article-867319, opened). The op-ed says those waiting have close relatives in Israel and describes waits of 17 and 25 years.
  - 4,226 "awaiting approval" (Hebrew-language citation on the Tzur Israel Wikipedia page; source not opened).
  - A September 2025 government approval for 2,000 more is stated on the English Wikipedia page without a citation I could open. Unverified.
- Terminology: "Falash Mura" is the common Israeli term; many in the community use "Zera Beta Israel" (descendants of Beta Israel). Framing choice for Daniel.

### Where they live
- About 64% live in the Central (37.2%) and Southern (27.4%) districts (CBS 2025 via Tedros; CBS 2024 gives 37%/27%).
- Largest numbers by city: Netanya 13,300 (5.6% of the city) in 2024; in 2023 Netanya 12,900, Beersheba 10,600, Petah Tikva 10,100 (CBS 2024 release).
- Highest share: Kiryat Malachi, 13.8% of residents (2024, Tedros/CBS); 13.9% (2023, CBS 2024 release); 15.5% (2021, CBS 2022 release). English Wikipedia gives 15.1%. No locality is majority Ethiopian-Israeli.
- Other cities named in the brief (Rehovot, Ashdod, Haifa, Kiryat Gat): CBS releases I opened did not give their counts. Neighborhood concentrations exist (Kiryat Moshe in Rehovot, Weizmann in Kiryat Malachi, Shofetim in Kiryat Gat, Ramat Eliyahu in Rishon LeZion — named in Ynet 2019, below).

### Education, income, employment
- Bagrut (matriculation): in 2022/23, 77.7% of Ethiopian-origin 12th-graders were eligible vs 85.6% in Hebrew-language education; university-threshold bagrut 51.5% vs 76.0% (CBS 2024 release, opened). In 2023/24, eligibility reportedly equaled the overall Hebrew-education rate for the first time (Jerusalem Post 2024-12-23, https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-834508, opened — gives no percentage; Wikipedia cites CBS 2025). University-threshold bagrut 2023/24: 60.7% vs 76.1% (Wikipedia citing CBS Sigd 2025; not opened). The jump from 51.5% to 60.7% in one year is large; check against the CBS original before publishing.
- Higher education: 4,144 Ethiopian-origin students in 2023/24, up 29.7% since 2016/17 (vs 3.9% growth among others); 60.4% at colleges, 27.5% at universities (CBS 2024 release). 4,151 university BA students in 2024/25 (Tedros citing CBS 2025).
- Household income: net monthly household income 14,676 NIS vs 18,237 NIS for the general population — about 20% lower; spending per person 2,749 vs 4,580 NIS, about 40% lower (CBS 2024 release; data year as given in the release, likely 2022 — confirm). Earlier points: 2019, 14,027 vs 17,779 NIS for all Jewish households (Wikipedia citing CBS/ENP); 2017, ~11,000 NIS, about 30% below all households (~15,800) (Hebrew Wikipedia, https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/ישראלים_יוצאי_אתיופיה, raw text opened). Trend: gap narrowing from ~30% to ~20%.
- Employment: no recent official figure found. Older: share employed rose from 50% to 72% between 2003 and 2015; women's participation from 35% to 65% in a decade; "only 5% of Ethiopians hold 'quality' jobs compared to 33% of Jewish Israelis" (Jewish Virtual Library, https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/the-situation-of-ethiopian-jews-in-israel; JVL is a compiler, sources not given per figure). Poverty: "more than 35%" of families below the line vs 18.6% overall (JVL, undated figure). These are 10 years old.
- Public sector: 2.44% of employees in government companies (1,273 of 52,181) in 2023, roughly matching population share, but one senior employee across 32 companies (Knesset Research and Information Center, via Tedros, https://tedros.co.il/en/statistics/civic-participation; RIC original not opened). Local authorities, senior ranks: 4 of 1,711 (0.2%), 2021 (State Comptroller via Tedros).

### Army service
- Enlistment 2010: ~86% vs 74% across the IDF; about 5,800 Ethiopian-Israeli soldiers serving (IDF Manpower data, quoted on Hebrew Wikipedia; original not opened). The same source adds that after enlistment, Ethiopian-Israeli soldiers fall into "service problems" at high rates. A 2013–14 Haaretz report (cited there, not opened) described high rates of military-prison sentences.
- 2025 figures from IDF Club, an advocacy/support organization (https://idfclub.org/from-immigration-to-integration-ethiopian-jews-excel-in-idf/, opened, 2025-08-13): over 85% of men and 50% of women enlist; about half of men in combat roles; about 40 Ethiopian-Israeli soldiers killed since Oct 7, 2023, "4.5% of military deaths." No primary source given. Do not publish without IDF confirmation.

## 2. Voting pattern 2019–2022

### What the evidence can and cannot show
- There is no direct measure. Israel's voter rolls do not record origin (Tedros notes the same). IDI's Democracy Index 2025 had 1,205 Jewish respondents, of whom 0.6% self-identified as Ethiopian — about 7 people (https://en.idi.org.il/media/30427/the-israeli-democracy-index-2025-full-english-version.pdf, local copy p.322 and method note). No survey cross-tab by Ethiopian origin is possible from IDI, INSS or INES toplines.
- Adults: roughly 120,000 of the 177,600 are 18+ (arithmetic from the 26.2% under-15 share plus an estimate for ages 15–17). At ~40,000 votes per seat (2022: 4.76M valid votes / 120), that is about two seats' worth of votes at most. An estimate, not a datum.

### Evidence 1 — neighborhood polling stations, 2019 (press analysis)
Ynet, 2019-09-18 (https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-5591507,00.html, opened), compared April and September 2019 at polling stations in heavily Ethiopian-Israeli neighborhoods, after the July 2019 protests:

| Neighborhood | Likud Apr→Sep 2019 | Blue and White Apr→Sep 2019 |
|---|---|---|
| Kiryat Moshe, Rehovot | 53% → 42% | 13% → 28% |
| Ramat Eliyahu (as printed; probably Rishon LeZion), | 53% → 43% | 15% → 24% |
| Weizmann, Kiryat Malachi | 50% → 39% | 6.5% → 18% |
| Shofetim, Kiryat Gat | 49% → 38% | 13% → 23% |

The machine summary rendered the second place as "Ramat Hasharon"; a later Ynet article locates Ramat Eliyahu in Rishon LeZion. Check the original before publishing. These stations are mixed, not purely Ethiopian-Israeli.

March 2020: Ynet (https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-5688315,00.html, opened) reported Blue and White falling back at the same kind of stations: Ramat Eliyahu 21.8% → 18.5%; Rehovot 27.4% → 20.1%. Community activists quoted said many had returned to Likud.

Use CEC.

### Reading of the pattern (what the sources say, not my conclusion)
- Historic lean to Likud, explained by community members as gratitude for Operation Moses. Avi Yossef, 35: "Usually we vote for Likud because it's the party that brought Ethiopians back to Israel. But in the last election, we voted for Blue and White." (ToI 2020-02-24, https://www.timesofisrael.com/seeking-likud-ouster-and-racial-equality-mk-highlights-ethiopian-israeli-shift/, opened.)
- Hebrew Wikipedia states that Ethiopian-Israelis identify mainly with right-wing parties (Likud, also Religious Zionism and Shas) and the center (Kadima, Blue and White), and cites a 2001 study finding reluctance to vote for Labor/Meretz, linked to memories of the period when aliyah was denied. Study not opened.
- A generational split is described: older voters reliably Likud; younger voters focused on police treatment (ToI 2020-03-02).
- An ethnic party has been tried once: Atid Ehad (Avraham Neguise), 2006, 14,005 votes, below threshold (Hebrew Wikipedia).

## 3. Attitudes on the six issues

No survey cross-tab by Ethiopian origin was found for any of the six issues (Haredi draft; courts; war and hostages; West Bank and annexation; religion and state; cost of living). IDI, INSS and the Israeli Voice Index do not publish an Ethiopian breakdown, and their samples contain too few Ethiopian-Israeli respondents (≈7 in IDI DI 2025) to produce one. INES microdata may record father's country of birth; that would need checking, and the cell would still be tiny.

What can be said, with sources, without claiming opinion data:
- **Haredi draft:** high enlistment (86% vs 74% in 2010, IDF data via Hebrew Wikipedia) and a reported high share of war dead (IDF Club, unverified) make the draft a sharp issue for the community, and the community's best-known religious-right MK broke with his party over it (Solomon). This is behavior and elite positioning, not survey opinion.
- **Religion and state:** the community's own history with the Chief Rabbinate (section 4) is the relevant fact.
- **Cost of living:** household income ~20% below average, spending per person ~40% below (CBS 2024 release).
- **Courts, war and hostages, West Bank:** nothing found.
- Older perception data: a 2012 Myers-JDC-Brookdale survey of immigrants who arrived 1979–1991 found "almost all" feel at home in Israel; about one-third reported personal discrimination at work and in the army; larger shares saw group discrimination by police, the education ministry and the army (https://brookdale.jdc.org.il/en/publication/twenty-years-later-survey-ethiopian-immigrants-lived-israel-two-decades/, opened). Fourteen years old and limited to older immigrants.

The page should say outright that there is no opinion data, rather than infer attitudes from voting.

## 4. Lived texture

### 2015 protests
- April 2015: video showed police beating Damas Pakada, an Ethiopian-Israeli IDF soldier (ToI 2015-04-27, https://www.timesofisrael.com/cops-beat-ethiopian-idf-soldier-in-alleged-racist-attack/ — cited on Wikipedia, not opened). Protests in Jerusalem and Tel Aviv over about ten days; tear gas; at least 7 protesters and 50 officers injured (English Wikipedia, "July 2019 Ethiopian Jews protest in Israel" background section, raw text opened). Netanyahu: "I strongly condemn the beating of the Ethiopian IDF soldier, and those responsible will be held accountable" (JPost 2015-04-30, quoted on Wikipedia; not opened). The case closed without significant punishment (same Wikipedia page).
- January 2019: Yehuda Biadga, 24, killed by police who said he held a knife; his family said he was mentally ill (Wikipedia, same page).

### 2019 protests — Solomon Tekah
- 2019-06-30: Solomon Tekah (Teka), 18, born in Gondar, in Israel since about age 12, was shot dead by an off-duty police officer at a playground in Kiryat Haim, Haifa. The officer said he fired a warning shot at the ground that ricocheted (Wikipedia, raw text opened).
- Protests from July 2: 15 major intersections blocked; police reported more than 130 arrests and more than 110 officers injured (Wikipedia, citing JPost, JTA, Reuters).
- 2020-02-04: officer indicted for negligent homicide. 2024-04-02: acquitted by Haifa Magistrate's Court (Judge Zaid Falah), on self-defense grounds (ToI, https://www.timesofisrael.com/police-officer-in-solomon-tekah-slaying-acquitted-of-negligent-manslaughter/, opened). Tekah's mother: "We had a small hope and it was dashed. There is no justice." 2024-05-19: the Police Internal Investigations Department appealed (ToI, https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/police-investigators-appeal-acquittal-of-officer-in-killing-of-ethiopian-israeli-teen/, opened). Outcome of the appeal not found.
- Wikipedia reports that, per Israeli media, 11 Ethiopian-Israelis were killed by police between 1997 and 2019. Not independently verified.
- Enforcement data: Ethiopian-Israelis were 8.2%–9.4% of arrests in 2019–2023, against 2.2% of the Jewish population (Knesset RIC via Tedros; RIC original not opened). CBS 2024 release: 7.4% of those convicted in 2022 (machine summary unclear on the comparison; confirm).

### Pnina Tamano-Shata
Born in Ethiopia, came in Operation Moses as a child (Wikipedia). MK for Yesh Atid (2013–15, 2018–), Blue and White, National Unity; first Ethiopian-born minister (Aliyah and Integration, 2020–21); chaired the Knesset Committee on the Status of Women 2023–2025. In 2020: "There are more Ethiopians in prison, more police brutality against us, higher poverty and a higher suicide rate." (ToI 2020-02-24, opened.) 2026: #2 on Blue and White.

### Sigd
Held on 29 Cheshvan, 50 days after Yom Kippur; observed with fasting, psalms and kessim reading from the Orit, centered in Jerusalem. The Knesset made it a state holiday in July 2008, on a bill by MK Uri Ariel (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigd, opened). Sigd 2026 falls on November 8–9, about two weeks after the election.

### The Chief Rabbinate and Beta Israel
- 1973: Sephardi Chief Rabbi Ovadia Yosef ruled Beta Israel Jewish; Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi Shlomo Goren followed in 1974; Israel applied the Law of Return to them in 1977 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Israel, raw text opened).
- On arrival the Rabbinate still required ritual immersion as a symbolic renewal — not framed as conversion, but many felt it "humiliating and unnecessary." The practice "was eventually abolished in 1985 following a month-long strike" (My Jewish Learning, Atira Winchester, https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/ethiopian-jews-in-israel/, opened). Other accounts describe the 1985 protest as a sit-in opposite the Chief Rabbinate and say restrictions on marriage registration lasted longer. I could not open a source for those details; treat as unverified.
- February 2018: the government recognized the kessim (traditional priests) as able to perform official religious functions, ending a 30-year dispute (JVL, opened). Today most of the community follows Sephardi practice under the Chief Rabbinate; there is an Ethiopian community chief rabbi, Reuven Wabashat (Beta Israel Wikipedia).
- Falash Mura must still convert (section 1).

### Other episodes the page could mention briefly
- 1996: Magen David Adom was found to have been secretly discarding blood donated by Ethiopian immigrants; the Navon commission called for a policy change and did not find racism (Wikipedia).
- 2012–2016 Depo-Provera claims: a 2016 State Comptroller investigation found no evidence of forced birth-control injections (Wikipedia citing Haaretz 2016-01-20).

### Voices (all from pages opened)
- Avi Yossef, 35 — on leaving Likud (ToI 2020-02-24), quoted above.
- Chaim Tekaleh, 31, Likud supporter, Kiryat Malachi: "I understand the trend among my friends who are calling for Netanyahu's ouster, but the problems our community needs answers to run much deeper than Balfour street." (ToI 2020-03-02.)
- "Karen," Blue and White voter, Kiryat Malachi: "We want a party that will truly represent us. Pnina has come to every one of our protests." (ToI 2020-03-02.)
- Solomon Tekah's mother, 2024 (ToI), quoted above.
- Hebrew, Ynet 2019: "מקווה שיבינו שלא נלך כמו עדר עיוור אחרי מי שמתעלם מהדרישות הבסיסיות שלנו" — "We hope they understand we won't follow like a blind herd after whoever ignores our basic demands." Speaker's name not captured; re-open to attribute before use.

## Framing choices for Daniel
1. Name: "Ethiopian-Israelis" vs "Israelis of Ethiopian origin" (CBS) vs "Beta Israel" (the community's historical name, which excludes Falash Mura).
2. "Falash Mura" vs "Zera Beta Israel."
3. Police deaths and the 2019 protests: describe them as "police violence," as the brief does, or use neutral wording ("killings by police," "the Tekah shooting") and give the acquittal in the same paragraph.
4. Race: say outright that Ethiopian-Israelis are Black Israelis and draw the US parallel (protesters chanted "I can't breathe"; Yevarkan cited George Floyd), or report the parallel only as something the protesters themselves drew.
5. 1980s immersion: call it "symbolic conversion" (common usage) or "ritual immersion the Rabbinate said was not conversion."
6. Whether to say the 26th Knesset may have no Ethiopian-Israeli MK (a projection from polls).
