## Ready now: Q1 / S1 legal model

| Motion / quantity | Correct rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute seat majority | 61 of the Knesset's 120 seats | Arithmetic; do not label it a universal confidence requirement |
| Initial investiture | More votes for than against; abstentions excluded | Basic Law: Government §13(d), together with Basic Law: Knesset §25 |
| Constructive no-confidence | At least 61 MKs vote confidence in an alternative government which announces policy guidelines, composition and ministerial allocation | Government §28(b) |
| Final formation route | A majority of MKs can request a named consenting MK during the final 21-day period; the nominee then has 14 days | Government §10; this 61-signature requirement is different from ordinary investiture |

[Government official PDF](https://main.knesset.gov.il/EN/activity/Documents/BasicLawsPDF/BasicLawTheGovernment.pdf) and [Knesset official PDF](https://main.knesset.gov.il/EN/activity/documents/BasicLawsPDF/BasicLawTheKnesset.pdf), current official English texts checked October 5. Search-readable §25 and §28(b) confirmed; direct access sometimes redirects to a geographic maintenance block. [IDI, Assaf Shapira, November 12, 2019](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/28888), explicitly explains simple-majority investiture and outside support. The law's wording has higher authority than this older explanatory article.

Ready copy: “61 seats is an absolute Knesset majority and a useful planning target. A government's initial confidence vote can pass with fewer than 61 votes if more MKs vote for than against; abstentions do not count. Replacing an existing government through constructive no-confidence requires 61 MKs to support an alternative government.”

The familiar historical example is the Bennett–Lapid government, June 13, 2021, 60–59 with one abstention.
